A fringe that the envelope removes

Two patterns with different length scales are superimposed, and the student must recognise that a bright fringe disappears when the two conditions coincide; the count of visible fringes then depends on excluding exactly those orders that fall on an envelope minimum, which is a discrete argument rather than a calculation.

Why the conveyor motor needs twice the power you expect

The motor power is not the rate at which the gravel gains kinetic energy but twice that, because the belt's friction force acts through the belt's displacement while the gravel slides and lags behind, so exactly half of the energy supplied is dissipated as heat at the sliding surface and the efficiency is pinned at 50 per cent.

E.3 Radioactive decay

A two-component decay: two isotopes with half-lives differing by a factor of eight are mixed, and their separate initial activities have to be recovered from only the total activity at two times — which means solving simultaneous equations in exponentials, then solving a logarithmic equation for the crossover time.

The ball that bounces higher than it was dropped

The light ball rebounds to about eight times its drop height rather than to the drop height, because the elastic floor bounce sends the heavy ball upwards while the light one is still falling, so their relative approach speed is $2v$ and the second elastic collision reverses it; the exact rebound factor $(3M-m)/(M+m)$ is not set by the drop height and has to be derived rather than guessed.

B.4 Thermodynamics

The cycle looks like a simple rectangle but requires three separate ideas to be combined: temperature ratios from the gas laws, work as area, and the first law applied to only the two legs where heat flows in — and then the efficiency has to be read against a Carnot bound the student must supply themselves.

A flywheel, a hanging mass, and a brake

The string tension is not the weight of the hanging mass, so the acceleration has to be found by solving the linear and rotational equations simultaneously; the same tension then carries into the energy check, and the final stage switches from dynamics to rotational work with the string no longer driving anything.

Two readings in a vacuum chamber: emissivity and specific heat

The cooling rate is not set by the temperature difference the student reaches for but by the difference of the fourth powers, and the specific heat capacity is recovered from the instantaneous energy balance at the very temperature at which the emissivity was fixed, so the two unknowns separate only once the student realises the radiated power is the same in both experiments.

The layer that adds a fixed factor to the surface temperature

The greenhouse warming factor is not set by the albedo or the solar constant that students reach for, because the surface temperature is always exactly $2^{1/4}$ times the effective radiating temperature, so changing the albedo moves both temperatures together while leaving their ratio untouched.

C.5 Doppler effect

The Doppler shift has to be applied twice — once for the outgoing trip and once for the reflection — and the two shifts compose multiplicatively, not additively. The final part asks for a small-speed approximation to be derived, which most students can only do by guessing.

Two cells in parallel, one of them being charged

The two cells have different emfs and the stronger one drives current backwards through the weaker, so the second branch current is negative; the question then requires a full power audit in which the weaker cell absorbs energy rather than supplying it, which most students will not anticipate from the circuit diagram.

A rod on rails that finds its own terminal speed

The rod accelerates until the magnetic force balances the driving force, so the terminal speed has to be found from a force balance rather than from kinematics, and the resulting expression is inversely proportional to the square of both the field and the length, which two separate parts test by different routes that give the same answer.
