A fringe that the envelope removes

Two patterns with different length scales are superimposed, and the student must recognise that a bright fringe disappears when the two conditions coincide; the count of visible fringes then depends on excluding exactly those orders that fall on an envelope minimum, which is a discrete argument rather than a calculation.

Why the conveyor motor needs twice the power you expect

The motor power is not the rate at which the gravel gains kinetic energy but twice that, because the belt's friction force acts through the belt's displacement while the gravel slides and lags behind, so exactly half of the energy supplied is dissipated as heat at the sliding surface and the efficiency is pinned at 50 per cent.

The coupling spring that the period ignores

In the symmetric normal mode the two gliders keep a constant separation, so the coupling spring is never stretched and its stiffness cancels completely from the equation of motion; the period is fixed by the wall springs alone, so the quantity the student instinctively reaches for is not the quantity that sets the frequency of the wave that is launched into the tube.

The touch that silences the first seven harmonics

Touching the string three-eighths of the way along does not split it into two independent strings but forces a single node there, and because the nodes of the nth mode fall at multiples of L/n the mode number must be a multiple of 8; the lowest note the touched string can give is therefore not the fundamental, and not the third harmonic that the familiar one-third touch would produce, but the eighth harmonic.

The work function that cancels in the difference

Each stopping voltage is an aggregate of the photon energy and the work function, so one reading gives a single equation in two unknowns and cannot be solved; only the difference of the two stopping voltages makes the work function cancel and exposes the energy-level scale A, after which the work function is recovered from either reading.

The enrichment that cannot move the energy per fission

The energy released in a single fission is fixed by the mass defect of the reaction and is therefore almost completely insensitive to the enrichment of the fuel or the choice of moderator; what enrichment and moderation actually change is the number of fissionable nuclei and the rate at which the chain reaction proceeds, so the quantity the student instinctively reaches for to raise the reactor's output is not the quantity that sets it.

The binding energy that must be counted twelve times

The energy released in the triple-alpha step is a small difference between two large total binding energies, so each binding energy per nucleon has to be multiplied by its mass number before subtracting; the resulting yield is only 0.61 MeV per nucleon, about a tenth of the yield of hydrogen fusion, so the reaction rate needed to supply the star's luminosity is far larger than an estimate based on the luminosity alone would suggest.

The ball that bounces higher than it was dropped

The light ball rebounds to about eight times its drop height rather than to the drop height, because the elastic floor bounce sends the heavy ball upwards while the light one is still falling, so their relative approach speed is $2v$ and the second elastic collision reverses it; the exact rebound factor $(3M-m)/(M+m)$ is not set by the drop height and has to be derived rather than guessed.

The bend that is set by a ratio, not a field

The undeflected reading fixes only the ratio E/B1 and not either field on its own, so doubling both fields together leaves the ion's speed, its straight path and its downstream radius completely unchanged, while the time spent in the magnetic region is set by the period 2 pi m/(q B2) and therefore does not respond at all to the speed that the student assumes controls it.

The layer that adds a fixed factor to the surface temperature

The greenhouse warming factor is not set by the albedo or the solar constant that students reach for, because the surface temperature is always exactly $2^{1/4}$ times the effective radiating temperature, so changing the albedo moves both temperatures together while leaving their ratio untouched.

A rod on rails that finds its own terminal speed

The rod accelerates until the magnetic force balances the driving force, so the terminal speed has to be found from a force balance rather than from kinematics, and the resulting expression is inversely proportional to the square of both the field and the length, which two separate parts test by different routes that give the same answer.
