1. A counting formula that fails on the regular hexagon 16 marks

MATH-AHL1.10-001 · AHL 1.10 (counting principles, including permutations and combinations) · difficulty 5

$n$ points are placed on a circle and every pair of points is joined by a straight chord. Throughout, assume first that the points are in general position, meaning that no three of the chords are concurrent at a point strictly inside the circle.

  1. [4 marks](a)

    Show that, for $n\geq4$ points in general position, the number of intersection points of the chords that lie strictly inside the circle is $\dbinom{n}{4}$.

    (Show that)
  2. [3 marks](b)

    Hence determine the least value of $n$ for which the number of interior intersection points exceeds $1000$.

    (Determine)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    Now suppose the six points are the vertices of a regular hexagon. Explain why $\dbinom{6}{4}=15$ is not the number of distinct interior intersection points, identifying precisely which intersections coincide.

    (Explain)
  4. [5 marks](d)

    Hence determine the number of distinct interior intersection points for the regular hexagon, justifying why no further coincidences occur.

    (Hence)

2. Which powers n make z^n = conj(z) reachable at real part one half 16 marks

MATH-AHL1.12-001 · AHL 1.12 (complex numbers: the number i, where i^2 = -1), AHL 1.13 (modulus-argument form and Euler form) · difficulty 5

Let $z$ be a complex number and let $\bar z$ denote its complex conjugate.

  1. [4 marks](a)

    By writing $z=a+bi$, where $a,b\in\mathbb{R}$, solve the equation $z^{2}=\bar z$, giving every solution in the form $a+bi$.

    (Solve)
  2. [3 marks](b)

    Show that every non-zero solution of $z^{2}=\bar z$ satisfies $|z|=1$, and deduce that every non-zero solution is a cube root of unity.

    (Show that)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    Hence, using modulus-argument form, solve the equation $z^{n}=\bar z$ for a general integer $n\geq1$, and state how many solutions it has.

    (Hence)
  4. [5 marks](d)

    Determine the smallest positive integer $n$ for which $z^{n}=\bar z$ has a solution whose real part is exactly $\dfrac12$, and describe all such integers $n$.

    (Determine)

3. When a vanishing triple product hides two different geometries 16 marks

MATH-AHL3.12-001 · AHL 3.12 (concept of a vector; position vectors; displacement), AHL 3.15 (coincident, parallel, intersecting and skew lines) · difficulty 5

Relative to an origin $O$, the point $A$ has position vector $\mathbf{a}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}$ and the point $B$ has position vector $\mathbf{b}(k)=\begin{pmatrix}k\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}$, where $k$ is a real parameter. The line $L_{1}$ passes through $A$ and has direction vector $\mathbf{u}=\begin{pmatrix}2\\1\\0\end{pmatrix}$; the line $L_{2}$ passes through $B(k)$ and has direction vector $\mathbf{v}(k)=\begin{pmatrix}2k\\k\\k-1\end{pmatrix}$.

  1. [3 marks](a)

    Show that the displacement vector $\overrightarrow{AB}$ is $\begin{pmatrix}k\\0\\-1\end{pmatrix}$, and show that the scalar triple product $(\mathbf{b}(k)-\mathbf{a})\cdot(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}(k))$ equals $k(k-1)$.

    (Show that)
  2. [3 marks](b)

    Hence determine the two values of $k$ for which $L_{1}$ and $L_{2}$ are coplanar, and explain why a vanishing scalar triple product cannot by itself distinguish parallel lines from intersecting lines.

    (Hence)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    For $k=1$, determine the relationship between $L_{1}$ and $L_{2}$, deciding in particular whether the two lines coincide, and justify your conclusion fully.

    (Determine)
  4. [3 marks](d)

    For $k=0$, show that $L_{1}$ and $L_{2}$ intersect, and determine the position vector of their point of intersection.

    (Determine)
  5. [3 marks](e)

    Hence classify the relationship between $L_{1}$ and $L_{2}$ for every real value of $k$, stating clearly the values of $k$ for which the lines are skew.

    (Hence)

4. An induction that only closes once the hypothesis is strengthened 15 marks

MATH-AHL1.15-001 · AHL 1.15 (proof by mathematical induction) · difficulty 5

For each integer $n\ge1$, let $S_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{k^{2}+1}$.

  1. [3 marks](a)

    Show that $\dfrac{1}{(k+1)^{2}+1}\le\dfrac{1}{k}-\dfrac{1}{k+1}$ for every integer $k\ge1$.

    (Show that)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    A student tries to prove by induction that $S_n<\dfrac32$ for all $n\ge1$. Explain why the inductive step cannot succeed when the hypothesis is only $S_k<\dfrac32$, writing down the strongest inequality that the step actually gives.

    (Explain)
  3. [5 marks](c)

    Prove by induction that $S_n\le\dfrac32-\dfrac1n$ for every integer $n\ge1$, and deduce that $S_n<\dfrac32$ for every integer $n\ge1$.

    (Prove)
  4. [3 marks](d)

    Determine the greatest real constant $c$ for which the inequality $S_n\le\dfrac32-\dfrac{c}{n}$ holds for every integer $n\ge1$, justifying your answer.

    (Determine)

5. The cubic whose odd symmetry is hidden by a translation 15 marks

MATH-AHL2.12-001 · AHL 2.12 (polynomial functions, their graphs and equations), AHL 2.14 (odd and even functions) · difficulty 5

The function $f$ is a cubic with leading coefficient $1$, and for all real $t$ it satisfies $$f(2+t)+f(2-t)=4.$$

  1. [4 marks](a)

    Show that $g(t)=f(2+t)-2$ is an odd function, and deduce that $f(x)=2+a(x-2)^{3}+b(x-2)$ for some real constants $a$ and $b$.

    (Show that)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    Given also that $f$ has a repeated root, determine the values of $a$ and $b$ and hence the equation of $f(x)$.

    (Determine)
  3. [3 marks](c)

    Hence factorise $f(x)$ completely, state its roots, and determine the coordinates of its point of inflection.

    (Hence)
  4. [4 marks](d)

    Determine the number of real solutions of $f(x)=k$ for each real value of $k$, using the symmetry and the turning points of the graph.

    (Determine)

6. A compound-angle equation whose squared version invents solutions 15 marks

MATH-AHL3.10-001 · AHL 3.10 (compound angle identities), AHL 3.11 (relationships between trigonometric functions) · difficulty 5

Consider the equation $$\cos 2x=\sqrt{2}\cos\left(x+\frac{\pi}{4}\right).$$

  1. [3 marks](a)

    Show that $\sqrt{2}\cos\left(x+\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)=\cos x-\sin x$ and that $\cos 2x=(\cos x-\sin x)(\cos x+\sin x)$.

    (Show that)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    Hence solve the equation $\cos 2x=\sqrt{2}\cos\left(x+\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)$ for $0\le x<2\pi$, giving each answer as a multiple of $\pi$.

    (Hence)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    A student solves the equation by squaring both sides. Explain how this leads to $\sin 2x(\sin 2x-1)=0$, and identify the extraneous solutions that the squaring introduces.

    (Explain)
  4. [4 marks](d)

    Determine the general solution of the equation, and hence the number of solutions that lie in the interval $-2\pi\le x\le2\pi$.

    (Determine)

7. The false root invented by multiplying through a vanishing denominator 15 marks

MATH-AHL3.9-001 · AHL 3.9 (definition of the reciprocal trigonometric ratios sec, csc, cot) · difficulty 5

For real $x$ where it is defined, let $$f(x)=\csc x-\cot x.$$

  1. [3 marks](a)

    State the values of $x$ in the interval $[0,2\pi)$ at which $f(x)$ is undefined, and write down the period of $f$.

    (State)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    (Show that)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    (Show that)

8. A join where continuity and differentiability impose independent relations 15 marks

MATH-AHL5.12-001 · AHL 5.12 (informal understanding of continuity and differentiability), AHL 5.15 (derivatives of tan x, sec x, csc x, cot x, a^x, log_a x) · difficulty 5

Let $a>0$ with $a\ne1$, and let $p,q,r,s,t$ be real constants. Define $$f(x)=\begin{cases} a^{x}+\log_a x+p, & 0<x\le1,\\[4pt] q\tan(x-1)+r, & 1<x\le1+\frac{\pi}{4},\\[4pt] s\csc(x-1)+t, & 1+\frac{\pi}{4}<x<1+\pi. \end{cases}$$

  1. [4 marks](a)

    Show that $f$ is continuous at $x=1$ exactly when $a+p=r$, and that $f$ is continuous at $x=1+\frac{\pi}{4}$ exactly when $q+r=s\sqrt2+t$.

    (Show that)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    By differentiating each branch, show that $f$ is differentiable at $x=1$ exactly when $a+p=r$ and $q=a\ln a+\frac{1}{\ln a}$, and that $f$ is differentiable at $x=1+\frac{\pi}{4}$ exactly when $q+r=s\sqrt2+t$ and $s=-\sqrt2\,q$.

    (Show that)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    Determine all parameter choices for which $f$ is continuous at $x=1$ but not differentiable there, and explain why no choice of parameters makes $f$ differentiable at $x=1$ while failing to be continuous there.

    (Determine)
  4. [3 marks](d)

    Taking $a=e$ and $r=0$, determine the coordinates of the stationary point of $f$ on the third branch when $f$ is differentiable at both joins, and justify that this point is a local maximum.

    (Hence)

9. 4.13 Bayes' theorem 16 marks

MATH-AHL4.13-001 · AHL 4.13 (Bayes' theorem for a maximum of three events); SL 4.9 (binomial distribution) · difficulty 4

Stimulus

A factory has three machines that produce the same component.

  • Machine $M_{1}$ produces 40% of output; 2% of its components are defective.
  • Machine $M_{2}$ produces 35% of output; 3% of its components are defective.
  • Machine $M_{3}$ produces 25% of output; 5% of its components are defective.

Finished components are mixed together before packing, so a component gives no visual clue as to which machine made it.

A component is selected at random from a day's output.

  1. [4 marks](a)

    Find the probability that the component is defective.

    (Find)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    Given that the component is defective, determine the probability that it was made by machine $M_{2}$.

    (Determine)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    Components are packed in boxes of 20. Find the probability that a box contains at least one defective component.

    (Find)
  4. [4 marks](d)

    A quality-control inspector tests components one at a time until a defective one is found. Given that the fifth component tested is the first defective one, find the probability that it was made by machine $M_{3}$, and explain why the answer is the same as if the first component tested had been defective.

    (Explain)
