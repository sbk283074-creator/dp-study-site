1. A mean that says grow and a spread that says do not

[16 marks]BM-6.6-001

Foxglove Bakery

Foxglove Bakery runs a single shop in a market town and is considering a second outlet. Two sites are available on the same high street: a small kiosk, which would cost EUR 40,000 to fit out, and a large cafe, which would cost EUR 120,000. Both would be financed by a bank loan at 6% a year, and the directors compare annual profit after interest.

The directors have two sets of profit forecasts, before interest, for each option. If demand is high, the kiosk would make EUR 40,000 a year and the cafe EUR 120,000. If demand is low, the kiosk would still make EUR 10,000, but the cafe would lose EUR 60,000 a year.

The finance director has assumed a 0.6 probability of high demand, based on her reading of the local economy. The marketing director disagrees. She has collected the daily customer numbers from a trial stall that traded in the same high street, with the same opening hours, for twelve months. Her figures are in the table, and she defines a high-demand month as one in which the stall served more than 80 customers a day.

The directors of Foxglove Bakery must decide whether to open a kiosk or a cafe as the second outlet.

  1. [5 marks](a)

    Using the table, calculate the mean, the median and the range of daily customers, and comment on how well the mean represents a typical month.

    (Calculate)
  2. [5 marks](b)

    Estimate the probability of a high-demand month from the trial data. Then calculate the expected annual profit after interest of each option using the finance director's probability and using your estimate.

    (Calculate)
  3. [6 marks](c)

    Evaluate which option Foxglove Bakery should choose. Justify your answer with reference to your decision tree and to the descriptive statistics.

    (Evaluate)
