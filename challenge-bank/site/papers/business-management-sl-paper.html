Foxglove Bakery

Foxglove Bakery runs a single shop in a market town and is considering a second outlet. The directors have two sets of profit forecasts, before interest, for each option. If demand is high, the kiosk would make EUR 40,000 a year and the cafe EUR 120,000. If demand is low, the kiosk would still make EUR 10,000, but the cafe would lose EUR 60,000 a year.

She has collected the daily customer numbers from a trial stall that traded in the same high street, with the same opening hours, for twelve months. Her figures are in the table, and she defines a high-demand month as one in which the stall served more than 80 customers a day.

The directors of Foxglove Bakery must decide whether to open a kiosk or a cafe as the second outlet.

The best-selling product is a two-seat bench, which accounts for 48 000 units of the annual output. Sales of the bench are highly seasonal: 80% of the annual volume is sold in April, May and June, and the remaining 20% is spread evenly over the other nine months. It could make only the bench; the other eleven products would stay on batch production.

Because the flow line cannot be expanded, any output needed above 8 000 benches in a month would have to be made in advance and stored. Hartswood's warehouse can hold the finished benches, but storing them costs EUR 3 per bench per month, and the consultant expects benches to be held for an average of four months. The finance director has also pointed out that a flow line is difficult to change over, so any design change to the bench would be expensive and slow.

Hartswood Garden Furniture must decide whether to invest EUR 600 000 in a flow production line for its best-selling bench.

Dale & Vale Dairy

Ravi argues that the plan's figures are the right ones because they describe how the scheme will work once it is established, and that the dairy has a public commitment to remove single-use plastic. Meg is not sure the dairy can afford to be wrong.

The founders of Dale & Vale Dairy must decide whether to commit to the returnable glass scheme in the business plan.

Deliveries would be steady, week by week, twelve months a year, and the supermarket would charge a penalty for any late delivery.

The bought-in cost per litre would fall to EUR 0.40, because the supermarket would supply the fruit pulp in bulk at cost and would use its own low-cost packaging. The supermarket has told Tom that the EUR 1.55 price would be fixed for the first year and then reviewed annually. It has also asked for the right to visit the production site without notice and to change the specification of the cordial, within limits, without renegotiating the price. Other small suppliers to the same chain have complained publicly that their margins were cut at the first review.

The alternative - Option A - is to change nothing. Nettle & Vine keeps its own brand, its recipe, its 40 trade customers and its six staff. The founders know, however, that the business cannot grow on its current site, that a rival cordial maker has just been bought by a larger drinks group, and that two of their largest trade customers have asked for a lower price next year.

Priya and Tom each take a salary of EUR 26 000 a year, below what they could earn elsewhere, and the business has not paid a dividend in three years. All of the profit is reinvested. The trade customers value the cordial partly because it is not available in supermarkets; one cafe owner told Tom that the day he could buy it in a supermarket would be the day he stopped stocking it.

Priya is opposed to the contract. 'The moment our cordial goes into their bottle with their name on it, we are not a business any more, we are a factory. I did not spend nine years building a recipe for somebody else's label.' Tom disagrees. '180 000 litres at a stroke. We would be secure for three years, we would employ eight people instead of six, and I could finally pay myself properly. Sentiment does not repay a loan.' Wagtail & Co is a dog daycare and grooming business that Nia and Rob, two former veterinary nurses, plan to open in a converted unit on an industrial estate. Neither has run a business before, and they are preparing the financial section of a business plan in order to raise the money they need.
