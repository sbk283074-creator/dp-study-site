Reading a quadratic from its graph and deducing the features of the three related graphs y = |f(x)|, y = f(|x|) and y = 1/f(x)

Question

The diagram shows the graph of a quadratic function $f(x)$. The graph crosses the $x$-axis at $x = 1$ and $x = 3$, crosses the $y$-axis at $3$, and has its vertex at $(2, -1)$.

  1. [3 marks](a)

    State the equation of $f(x)$ in the form $f(x) = a(x - p)(x - q)$ and find the value of $a$.

    (State)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    The graph of $y = \dfrac{1}{f(x)}$ has two vertical asymptotes. Determine their equations, and determine the coordinates of the local maximum of $y = \dfrac{1}{f(x)}$.

    (Determine)
  3. [3 marks](c)

    State the equation of $y = f(|x|)$ and the number of solutions of $f(|x|) = 0$.

    (State)
  4. [3 marks](d)

    Explain how the graph of $y = |f(x)|$ differs from the graph of $y = f(|x|)$.

    (Explain)

The diagram shows the graph of a quadratic function $f(x)$. The graph crosses the $x$-axis at $x = 1$ and $x = 3$, crosses the $y$-axis at $3$, and has its vertex at $(2, -1)$. (a) State the equation of $f(x)$ in the form $f(x) = a(x - p)(x - q)$ and find the value of $a$. [3 marks] (State) (b) The graph of $y = \dfrac{1}{f(x)}$ has two vertical asymptotes. Determine their equations, and determine the coordinates of the local maximum of $y = \dfrac{1}{f(x)}$. [4 marks] (Determine) (c) State the equation of $y = f(|x|)$ and the number of solutions of $f(|x|) = 0$. [3 marks] (State) (d) Explain how the graph of $y = |f(x)|$ differs from the graph of $y = f(|x|)$. [3 marks] (Explain)
Reveal the answer

(a) The graph meets the $x$-axis at $x = 1$ and at $x = 3$, so those are the
roots and the factorised form is

$$f(x) = a(x - 1)(x - 3).$$ (M1)

To find $a$, use the $y$-intercept. Substituting $x = 0$ gives $f(0) = 3$:

$$a(0 - 1)(0 - 3) = a(3) = 3, \quad\text{so}\quad a = 1.$$ (M1)(A1)

Hence

$$f(x) = (x - 1)(x - 3) = x^{2} - 4x + 3,$$

and the vertex of this parabola is at $x = 2$, where $f(2) = -1$, matching the diagram. (AG)

(b) The reciprocal $y = \dfrac{1}{f(x)}$ is undefined wherever $f(x) = 0$, so its vertical
asymptotes are the zeros of $f$:

$$x = 1 \quad\text{and}\quad x = 3.$$ (M1)(A1)

For the stationary point, differentiate $f$: $f'(x) = 2x - 4 = 0$ at $x = 2$. Since
$f(x) = (x - 2)^{2} - 1$, the function $f$ is least at $x = 2$ with $f(2) = -1$. On the interval
$1 < x < 3$ the value of $f$ is negative, so $1/f$ is also negative, and its magnitude is
largest where the magnitude of $f$ is smallest. That happens at $x = 2$, so $y = 1/f(x)$ has a
stationary point there:

$$\frac{1}{f(2)} = \frac{1}{-1} = -1.$$ (M1)

The local maximum of $y = \dfrac{1}{f(x)}$ is therefore at

$$(2, -1).$$ (A1)

This is a maximum even though its value is negative: for $x$ just below or just above $2$ the
value of $f$ is a small negative number, so the reciprocal is a large negative number, and the
reciprocal rises to $-1$ at $x = 2$ and falls away again on both sides.

(c) Replacing $x$ by $|x|$ gives

$$f(|x|) = (|x| - 1)(|x| - 3) = |x|^{2} - 4|x| + 3 = x^{2} - 4|x| + 3.$$ (M1)(A1)

For $x \geq 0$ this agrees with $f$, so the right-hand half of the graph is unchanged; for
$x < 0$ it is the mirror image of the right-hand half in the $y$-axis. The roots occur where
$|x| = 1$ or $|x| = 3$, that is at $x = \pm 1$ and $x = \pm 3$, so $f(|x|) = 0$ has four
solutions. (A1)

(d) The two graphs transform different variables and so look quite different.

For $y = |f(x)|$ the absolute value is applied to the output: wherever $f(x)$ is negative
the value is replaced by its positive counterpart, and wherever $f(x)$ is already non-negative
nothing changes. The part of the original curve that lies below the $x$-axis, which here is the
portion between $x = 1$ and $x = 3$, is reflected in the $x$-axis. (A1) The zeros of $f$ stay
zeros and become sharp corners, the vertex $(2, -1)$ becomes $(2, 1)$, and the curve is
otherwise untouched. (A1)

For $y = f(|x|)$ the absolute value is applied to the input: the value at $x$ is the value
the original function takes at $|x|$. Negative inputs are replaced by their positive
counterparts, so the left-hand half of the graph is discarded and replaced by a mirror image of
the right-hand half reflected in the $y$-axis. Nothing is reflected in the $x$-axis, the curve
never rises above its original height, and the vertex at $(2, -1)$ is joined by a matching
vertex at $(-2, -1)$. (A1)

In short, $y = |f(x)|$ fixes the $x$-values and turns negative outputs positive, while
$y = f(|x|)$ fixes the $y$-values and turns negative inputs positive; the first reflects in the
$x$-axis and the second in the $y$-axis.

Markscheme notes

(a) (M1) for the factorised form using the two roots, (M1) for substituting the $y$-intercept, (A1) for $a = 1$ and hence $f(x) = x^{2} - 4x + 3$, (AG) for confirming the vertex. (b) (M1) for identifying the asymptotes with the zeros of $f$, (A1) for $x = 1$ and $x = 3$, (M1) for the stationary point, (A1) for $(2, -1)$. The value $-1$ must be given; an answer of $(2, 1)$ scores (M1)(A0) because the sign of the reciprocal of a negative number is negative. (c) (M1) for replacing $x$ by $|x|$, (A1) for $x^{2} - 4|x| + 3$, (A1) for four solutions. Accept the four values $x = \pm 1, \pm 3$ as equivalent to the count. (d) (A1) for identifying that $|f(x)|$ reflects the negative part of the curve in the $x$-axis, (A1) for the coordinates $(2, -1)$ becoming $(2, 1)$, (A1) for identifying that $f(|x|)$ mirrors the right-hand branch in the $y$-axis and stating that the graph is otherwise unchanged. Award the third mark only if the student distinguishes which variable is transformed; a general statement that one is a reflection in each axis without that distinction scores (A0).

Why this question is hard

The three graphs in node 2.16 are grouped because they are all built from a function
and an absolute value, and the grouping is exactly what makes them confusable. The item is
constructed so that the student must separate them by asking which variable is being
transformed, because that is the only distinction that survives.

Part (a) is the entry point and it is deliberately graphical. The roots and the $y$-intercept
are read off the diagram, and $a$ follows from the intercept, giving $f(x) = x^{2} - 4x + 3$.
The vertex at $(2, -1)$ is then a consequence rather than a given, which matters because part
(b) turns on that vertex.

Part (b) is where the reciprocal graph has to be reasoned about rather than sketched. Two
features are asked for. The asymptotes are the zeros of $f$, since a reciprocal cannot be formed
where the denominator vanishes; that is a structural observation and most students get it. The
stationary point is the harder half. It sits at the same $x$-coordinate as the vertex of $f$,
because $1/f$ is extremal where $f$ is extremal, but its value is $1/(-1) = -1$, which is
negative. A student working from a template expects a maximum to be a high point and may report
$+1$, having taken the absolute value of the reciprocal without noticing. The value $-1$ is
correct and it is genuinely a local maximum: on either side of $x = 2$ the function $f$ is a
small negative number, so its reciprocal is a large negative number, and the curve rises to
$-1$ at the centre and falls away again. The item makes that explicit in the answer because it
is the single most likely place to lose a mark.

Part (c) asks for the graph with the absolute value on the input. Here $f$ happens to be a
function of $x^{2}$ and $x$ only, so replacing $x$ by $|x|$ leaves the expression formally
unchanged except that $x$ becomes $|x|$; the effect is that the right-hand branch is mirrored
into the left, and the number of roots doubles from two to four. The count is the part worth
testing, because a student who reflects in the wrong axis will still produce a plausible
picture but will usually report two roots rather than four.

Part (d) asks for the distinction in words, and it is the part that cannot be reached by
pattern-matching. The two graphs are both reflections, and a student who has memorised "one is
a reflection in the $x$-axis and one in the $y$-axis" has the right conclusion for the wrong
reason. The reason is that $|f(x)|$ acts on the output and $f(|x|)$ acts on the input, and the
axis of reflection is a consequence of which variable was transformed, not a separate fact to
be remembered. The item asks for the explanation rather than the conclusion for that reason.

Challenge lever
The three related graphs look like the same transformation and are not. y = |f(x)| acts on the output and reflects the part of the curve below the axis upwards; y = f(|x|) acts on the input and reflects the part of the curve to the right of the axis leftwards; y = 1/f(x) acts on the output in a way that inverts the ordering, so where f is large the reciprocal is small. The first move a prepared student makes is to treat all three as reflections and to assume that the reciprocal graph inherits the vertex of the original as a maximum. Because f(2) = -1 is negative, the reciprocal there is also -1 and it is a maximum of a negative function, so the vertex survives with its value unchanged only because the value happens to be -1. The demand that separates the item is that the student must give the two vertical asymptotes of 1/f(x) at the zeros of f, and must explain the difference between reflecting in the x-axis and reflecting in the y-axis, which is a difference in which variable is transformed rather than in which axis is used.
