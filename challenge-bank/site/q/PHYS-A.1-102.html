A.1 Kinematics: reading a velocity-time graph

Physics HL · PHYS-A.1-102

Theme A: Space, time and motionA.1 (Kinematics: velocity-time graphs, displacement from the area under a graph, average velocity, acceleration as the gradient)P1 · Section A5 marksdifficulty 4technology: permittedDetermineCalculate

Question

The figure is the velocity-time graph for an object moving in a straight line. The object accelerates uniformly from rest, then travels at a constant velocity, then decelerates uniformly until it is momentarily at rest, and then continues to accelerate in the opposite direction. The letters A, B, C and D label the four regions between the line and the time axis: A, B and C lie above the axis and D lies below it. Because the motion is along a single straight line, the sign of the velocity gives the direction of travel, and the area between the line and the axis counts as positive displacement above the axis and negative displacement below it. Questions (a) to (e) all refer to this graph, each is worth one mark, and numerical answers should be given to three significant figures.

  1. [1 mark](a)

    Determine the displacement of the object over the whole 12 s interval shown on the graph, taking account of the direction of travel.

    (Determine)
  2. [1 mark](b)

    Determine the acceleration of the object at the instant t = 11 s, at which the object is momentarily at rest.

    (Determine)
  3. [1 mark](c)

    Calculate the average velocity of the object over the whole 12 s interval shown on the graph.

    (Calculate)
  4. [1 mark](d)

    Calculate the total distance travelled by the object over the whole 12 s interval shown on the graph.

    (Calculate)
  5. [1 mark](e)

    Determine the speed of the object at the instant t = 9.5 s.

    (Determine)

Total: 5 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) A. The displacement is the signed area between the graph and the time axis. From 0 to 3.0 s the region is a triangle, $\frac{1}{2}(3.0)(9.0) = 13.5$ m; from 3.0 s to 8.0 s it is a rectangle, $(5.0)(9.0) = 45$ m; from 8.0 s to 11 s it is a triangle, $\frac{1}{2}(3.0)(9.0) = 13.5$ m; from 11 s to 12 s it is a triangle below the axis, $\frac{1}{2}(1.0)(-3.0) = -1.5$ m. Adding, $13.5 + 45 + 13.5 - 1.5 = 70.5$ m. (A1)

(b) A. The acceleration is the gradient of the graph. From 8.0 s to 12 s the graph is one straight line, so the gradient is constant over that whole interval and is $(v_{12} - v_8)/(12 - 8) = (-3.0 - 9.0)/4.0 = -3.0$ m s$^{-2}$. The velocity happens to be zero at t = 11 s, but that says nothing about the gradient: the object is passing through rest, not sitting at it. (A1)

(c) A. Average velocity is displacement divided by time, $70.5/12 = 5.875$, i.e. 5.88 m s$^{-1}$. (A1)

(d) A. Distance travelled treats every area as positive, so $13.5 + 45 + 13.5 + 1.5 = 73.5$ m. The difference between this and the answer to (a) is twice the final area, 3.0 m, which is the ground covered while moving backwards and then given up again. (A1)

(e) A. On 8.0 s to 11 s the velocity falls at 3.0 m s$^{-2}$, so $v(9.5) = 9.0 - 3.0(9.5 - 8.0) = 9.0 - 4.5 = 4.5$ m s$^{-1}$. Since the velocity is still positive there, the speed equals the velocity. (A1)

Markscheme notes

One mark per question, no negative marking, and no method marks: the key is the answer. (a) Accept 70.5 m. The distractors are all arithmetic consequences of a defensible misreading, so a candidate who answers B has found the distance and a candidate who answers C has stopped the motion at t = 11 s. (b) This is the hardest question in the cluster and the one most often failed: accept only -3.0 m s$^{-2}$. An answer of 0 m s$^{-2}$ is the error the question exists to detect, because it confuses a zero value of the function with a zero value of its gradient. (c) Accept 5.88 m s$^{-1}$. An answer of 6.13 m s$^{-1}$ is the average speed and shows the candidate has not separated the two averages. (d) Accept 73.5 m. (e) Accept 4.5 m s$^{-1}$; accept -4.5 m s$^{-1}$ only if the candidate has explicitly read the gradient as positive, since the question asks for speed and the velocity at 9.5 s is positive. Where a candidate's two answers to (a) and (d) differ by exactly 3.0 m, the pair is internally consistent and both are likely to be right.

Why this question is hard

A velocity-time graph is worth more than the formula it encodes, and this cluster is built to make that visible. The single graph carries four pieces of information that candidates routinely run together, and each question isolates one of them.

The first distinction is between signed and unsigned area. The region D lies below the time axis, so it represents motion in the negative direction, and the displacement it contributes is negative. Distance travelled, by contrast, counts every area as positive. Parts (a) and (d) ask for the two quantities and differ by exactly twice the final triangle, 3.0 m. A candidate who gets (a) and (d) both right understands the distinction; a candidate who gets (d) right and (a) wrong has found the distance when asked for the displacement.

The second distinction is between a quantity and its rate of change. At t = 11 s the object is momentarily at rest, and the tempting answer to part (b) is that the acceleration is zero because the velocity is. That is wrong, and the graph shows why: the line is straight from t = 8 s all the way to t = 12 s, so the gradient, and therefore the acceleration, is the same at t = 11 s as it is at t = 9 s. The object does not pause at rest; it passes through. This is the same error as assuming that a ball thrown vertically upwards has zero acceleration at the top of its flight.

The third distinction is between the two averages. Average velocity uses displacement, average speed uses distance, and because the object reverses, the two differ. Part (c) asks for the velocity and offers the speed as a distractor.

Part (e) checks whether the candidate can read an intermediate value off the graph rather than only its endpoints, and the distractors correspond to reading the deceleration as ending at t = 12 s instead of t = 11 s, which is the commonest way to misplace the zero crossing.
