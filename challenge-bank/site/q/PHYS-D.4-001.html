A rod on rails that finds its own terminal speed

Physics HL · PHYS-D.4-001

D.4 Induction (AHL): magnetic flux, Faraday's law, emf induced in a straight conductor moving perpendicular to a field as epsilon = BvL, Lenz's law as a consequence of energy conservationP2 · Section B15 marksdifficulty 4technology: permittedShow thatCalculateExplainDetermine

Question

Two long parallel rails, separated by a distance $L=0.60\ \mathrm{m}$, lie in a horizontal plane and are frictionless. A straight conducting rod of mass $m=0.12\ \mathrm{kg}$ rests across the rails and can slide along them. The rails and the rod have negligible resistance, and a resistor of resistance $R=0.80\ \Omega$ connects the rails at one end. Over the whole region between the rails there is a uniform magnetic field of flux density $B=0.45\ \mathrm{T}$ directed perpendicular to the plane of the rails. A light inextensible string attached to the rod passes over a frictionless pulley and supports a hanging mass $m_{2}=0.050\ \mathrm{kg}$. The system is released from rest.

  1. [6 marks](a)

    Show that the rod reaches a terminal speed and derive an expression for that speed in terms of $m_{2}$, $g$, $R$, $B$ and $L$. Calculate its value.

    (Show that)
  2. [3 marks](b)

    Show that, at the terminal speed, the rate at which the hanging mass loses gravitational potential energy equals the rate at which energy is dissipated in the resistor.

    (Show that)
  3. [3 marks](c)

    The flux density is doubled to $0.90\ \mathrm{T}$ with everything else unchanged. Determine the new terminal speed and explain why the change is larger than a factor of two.

    (Determine)
  4. [3 marks](d)

    Instead the rod is replaced by a longer rod of the same mass that spans rails a distance $1.20\ \mathrm{m}$ apart, with the field restored to $0.45\ \mathrm{T}$. Determine the new terminal speed and explain why it is numerically the same as your answer to part (c).

    (Determine)

Total: 15 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) As the rod accelerates, the emf induced across it is $\varepsilon=BvL$ and the current around the circuit is $I=\dfrac{\varepsilon}{R}=\dfrac{BvL}{R}$. The magnetic force opposing the motion is $F=BIL=\dfrac{B^{2}L^{2}v}{R}$. (M1)(M1)

For the rod and hanging mass moving together, the resultant driving force is $m_{2}g-F$, so $m_{2}g-\dfrac{B^{2}L^{2}v}{R}=(m+m_{2})a$. (M1)

As $v$ increases the opposing force grows, so $a$ falls; when $m_{2}g=\dfrac{B^{2}L^{2}v}{R}$ the acceleration is zero and the speed stops changing. (A1)

Hence $v=\dfrac{m_{2}gR}{B^{2}L^{2}}$. (A1)

Numerically $v=\dfrac{0.050\times 9.81\times 0.80}{(0.45)^{2}(0.60)^{2}}=\dfrac{0.3924}{0.0729}=5.38\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. (A1)

(b) At terminal speed the current is $I=\dfrac{BvL}{R}=\dfrac{0.45\times 5.383\times 0.60}{0.80}=1.82\ \mathrm{A}$. (M1)

Power dissipated in the resistor: $I^{2}R=(1.8167)^{2}\times 0.80=2.64\ \mathrm{W}$. (A1)

Rate of loss of gravitational potential energy of the hanging mass: $m_{2}gv=0.050\times 9.81\times 5.383=2.64\ \mathrm{W}$, equal to the electrical power. (A1) This is the content of Lenz's law: the induced current dissipates energy at exactly the rate at which mechanical energy is supplied, so no energy is created in the circuit.

(c) Since $v\propto\dfrac{1}{B^{2}}$, doubling $B$ divides the terminal speed by four: $v=\dfrac{0.3924}{(0.90)^{2}(0.60)^{2}}=\dfrac{0.3924}{0.2916}=1.35\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. (M1)(A1)

The effect is more than a factor of two because the field enters twice. Doubling $B$ doubles the induced emf at a given speed, which on its own would double the current; but that doubled current also experiences twice the magnetic force per ampere. The two doublings compound, so the same retarding force is produced at one quarter of the speed. (A1)

(d) Since $v\propto\dfrac{1}{L^{2}}$ as well, doubling $L$ again divides the speed by four: $v=\dfrac{0.3924}{(0.45)^{2}(1.20)^{2}}=\dfrac{0.3924}{0.2916}=1.35\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. (M1)(A1)

The two answers agree because $B$ and $L$ appear in the terminal-speed expression only through the product $BL$, which is the quantity that sets both the emf at a given speed and the force at a given current. Doubling either one therefore produces exactly the same change, and the mass of the rod does not enter the expression at all, since at terminal speed the rod's own acceleration is zero. (A1)

Markscheme notes

Part (a): the derivation must include both the emf and the force; a candidate who quotes $F=B^{2}L^{2}v/R$ without showing where it comes from earns one (M1) only. The (A1) for recognising the terminal condition requires the force balance, not simply 'a is zero'. A candidate who neglects the rod's own mass in the acceleration term still reaches the correct terminal speed, since the masses cancel there; award full marks. Do not accept $v=m_{2}gR/(BL)$ — a confusion between $B$ and $B^{2}$ is a common and costly error that loses the final two marks in (a) and, by follow-through, the marks in (c) and (d) unless the method is otherwise correct. Part (b): the numbers must be compared, not merely asserted; accept $2.6\ \mathrm{W}$ on both sides. Part (c): follow-through applies from (a). The explanation mark requires the double role of B — in the emf and in the force — not just the observation that it is squared. Part (d): credit an answer that notices $BL$ is the only combination that matters. A candidate who tries to include the rod's mass in a momentum argument will not reach 1.35 m/s and should not be given the explanation mark.

Why this question is hard

The rod does not accelerate indefinitely, and the reason is worth stating precisely: the magnetic force is not constant but proportional to the speed. At rest there is no current and no magnetic force, so the driving weight accelerates the system; as the speed rises the induced emf rises, the current rises, and so does the retarding force. Equilibrium is reached when the retarding force equals the driving force, and thereafter the speed is constant even though the hanging mass continues to descend.

The important structural point is that the terminal condition is a force balance, not a statement that the acceleration is zero in some vague sense, and it is reached without solving the differential equation for $v(t)$. This is the same reasoning used for a falling object with air resistance, and recognising the pattern is half the work.

The two final parts are deliberately constructed to produce the same numerical answer by different routes. The expression is $v=m_{2}gR/(B^{2}L^{2})$, so both doubling $B$ and doubling $L$ divide the speed by four, because both quantities enter only through the product $BL$. That product appears once in the emf and once in the force, which is why the square appears. A student who works out that $B$ enters twice but treats $L$ as entering once — for instance by assuming the magnetic force is $BIL$ with $I$ independent of $L$ — will get part (d) wrong and, more importantly, will have missed the structure of the problem.

The mass of the rod never appears in the final answer, which surprises students who expect it to matter. It affects how quickly the terminal speed is reached, not what that speed is, and part (d) makes this concrete by doubling the length while holding the mass fixed.

Challenge lever
The rod accelerates until the magnetic force balances the driving force, so the terminal speed has to be found from a force balance rather than from kinematics, and the resulting expression is inversely proportional to the square of both the field and the length, which two separate parts test by different routes that give the same answer.

Adaptation
Rods on rails with a hanging mass are standard in A-level and IB induction problems, but they usually stop at the terminal speed. This version requires the terminal condition to be justified as a force balance, adds an explicit energy audit that doubles as a statement of Lenz's law, and then sets two different changes (doubling B, doubling L) that must give the same answer for a structural reason. The values, the paired parts and the explanation of the BL product are original; every figure was recomputed numerically.
Verification
Terminal speed from v = m2*g*R/(B^2*L^2). At B = 0.45 T, L = 0.60 m: v = 0.3924/0.0729 = 5.3827 m/s, I = 1.8167 A, I^2 R = 2.6402 W, and m2*g*v = 2.6402 W, confirming the energy balance. Doubling B to 0.90 T gives 0.3924/0.2916 = 1.3457 m/s; doubling L to 1.20 m at 0.45 T gives the same 1.3457 m/s.
