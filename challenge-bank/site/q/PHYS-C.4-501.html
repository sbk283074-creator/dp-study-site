C.4 Standing waves: the resonant modes of a pipe closed at one end against a pipe open at both ends

Question

The figure shows two organ pipes drawn to scale. Pipe P and pipe Q have the same length, 0.850 m, but pipe P is closed by a solid stopper at one end and open at the other, while pipe Q is open at both ends. The speed of sound in the air inside both pipes is 340 m s$^{-1}$. End corrections may be neglected, so a displacement antinode may be taken to lie in the plane of each open end and a displacement node at each closed end. Reveal the answer

(a) A. The closed end must be a displacement node and the open end a displacement
antinode, and the shortest way to join a node to an antinode is a quarter of a
wavelength. So $\lambda = 4L = 4(0.850) = 3.40$ m and $f = v/\lambda = 340/3.40 =
100$ Hz. (A1)

(b) C. Each successive mode adds another half wavelength between the node and the
antinode, which adds $2f_{1}$ to the frequency rather than $f_{1}$. The series is
therefore $f_{1},\ 3f_{1},\ 5f_{1},\ \ldots$, that is 100, 300, 500, 700 Hz. The third
lowest of these is 500 Hz, which is the fifth harmonic. (A1)

(c) D. With both ends open, both are antinodes, and the shortest way to join an
antinode to an antinode is half a wavelength. So $\lambda = 2L = 1.70$ m and
$f = 340/1.70 = 200$ Hz, an octave above pipe P of the same length. (A1)

(d) A. The two fundamentals are $v/4L$ for the closed pipe and $v/2L$ for the open
one. Setting them equal, $v/2L_{\text{open}} = v/4(0.850)$, gives
$L_{\text{open}} = 2(0.850) = 1.70$ m. The open pipe has to be twice as long to sound
as low. (A1)

(e) B. At 500 Hz the wavelength in air is $\lambda = v/f = 340/500 = 0.680$ m. In
any standing wave the nodes are spaced half a wavelength apart, so the separation is
0.340 m. As a check, the pipe holds $0.850/0.680 = 1.25$ wavelengths, which is five
quarters — exactly what the fifth harmonic of a stopped pipe requires. (A1)

Markscheme notes

One mark per question, no negative marking, and no method marks: the key is the answer.

(a) Accept 100 Hz. An answer of 200 Hz has applied the open-pipe condition to a closed
pipe; 400 Hz and 800 Hz take the pipe length to be one wavelength and half a
wavelength respectively.

(b) Accept 500 Hz and no other value. This question separates the count of resonances
from the harmonic number, and 300 Hz is the answer it exists to catch: that is the
third harmonic but only the second resonance. Do not accept 400 Hz under any
circumstances — it is an even multiple of the fundamental and a stopped pipe cannot
resonate there at all.

(c) Accept 200 Hz. An answer of 100 Hz has carried the closed-pipe value across
unchanged; 50.0 Hz uses the closed-pipe condition on a pipe with no closed end.

(d) Accept 1.70 m. An answer of 0.425 m is the discriminating error: it takes the
ordering from part (c), where the closed pipe is the lower of the two at equal length,
and assumes the closed pipe must therefore be the shorter at equal pitch. It is the
other way round. An answer of 3.40 m has given the wavelength rather than the length.

(e) Accept 0.340 m. An answer of 0.680 m has given the wavelength instead of half of
it, and 0.170 m is the node-to-antinode distance.

Why this question is hard

Everything in this cluster comes from two sentences: a closed end is a displacement
node, an open end is a displacement antinode. Everything else is counting how many
quarter-wavelengths fit between them.

For the stopped pipe the count is odd, always. The fundamental fits one quarter; each
mode above it adds a node–antinode pair, which is a further half wavelength, so the
series runs $1, 3, 5, 7$ quarters and the frequencies run $f_{1}, 3f_{1}, 5f_{1},
7f_{1}$. Two consequences get confused in examinations and both are tested here. The
first is that the harmonic number and the position in the list are not the same thing:
500 Hz is the third resonance and the fifth harmonic. The second is that the missing
even harmonics are not merely absent but *forbidden* — a standing wave with an even
number of quarter-wavelengths would need the far end to be both a node and an
antinode. That is why 400 Hz is not a quiet resonance but no resonance at all.

The open pipe has no such restriction. Both ends are antinodes, so the count is even —
$2, 4, 6$ quarters — and every harmonic is present. At the same length its fundamental
is twice the stopped pipe's, which is the organ builder's reason for using stopped
pipes: a stopped pipe gives you an octave for half the metal.

Part (d) is where the cluster turns. The comparison has been made once with length
held fixed, and now it is made again with frequency held fixed, and the two answers
point opposite ways. At equal length the stopped pipe is lower, 100 Hz against 200 Hz.
At equal frequency the stopped pipe is shorter, 0.850 m against 1.70 m. The algebra
says this without any risk of confusion — equating $v/4L_{c}$ with $v/2L_{o}$ gives
$L_{o} = 2L_{c}$ — but a candidate reasoning from the remembered ordering rather than
from the expression will halve where they should double. That is a general hazard of
comparative reasoning, not a fact about pipes: the ranking of two things is a property
of the comparison, not of the things, and it can reverse when the quantity held fixed
changes.

Part (e) is a small check that the picture still makes sense. At 500 Hz the wavelength
is 0.680 m and the pipe is 1.25 wavelengths long, which is the fifth harmonic of a
stopped pipe exactly as part (b) claimed.
