De Broglie wavelength and electron diffraction

Physics HL · PHYS-E.2-401

Theme E: Nuclear and quantum physicsE.2 Quantum physics: the de Broglie wavelength of a particle and its relation to the momentum given by an accelerating potential difference, and the electron-diffraction evidence for the wave nature of matterP211 marksdifficulty 5technology: permittedCalculateDetermineExplainDiscuss

Question

An electron-diffraction tube accelerates electrons from rest through a potential difference V and directs them at a thin graphite film. Electrons scattered through the correct angle reach a fluorescent screen held a distance L from the film and produce a pattern of concentric rings. The figure shows the tube, the film and the screen. The set of crystal planes that produces the first-order ring has a separation d = 2.13 x 10^-10 m, and because the angles involved are small the radius of that ring is given by r = 2L lambda / d, where lambda is the de Broglie wavelength of the electrons. The tube is operated with V = 3.00 kV.

  1. [2 marks](a)

    Calculate the de Broglie wavelength of an electron that has been accelerated from rest through a potential difference of 3.00 kV. Give your answer in picometres.

    (Calculate)
  2. [3 marks](b)

    Determine the potential difference that would be required to reduce the radius of the first-order ring from the value shown in the figure to 2.50 cm. Give your answer in kilovolts.

    (Determine)
  3. [3 marks](c)

    Explain how the ring pattern provides evidence that an electron has a wave nature, and why the electrons must all have very nearly the same speed for the rings to be sharp.

    (Explain)
  4. [3 marks](d)

    The electrons are replaced by protons that have the same speed as the electrons in part (a). Discuss, with a calculation, how the ring pattern on the screen would change.

    (Discuss)

Total: 11 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) An electron accelerated from rest through a potential difference V gains kinetic energy $eV$, so $\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = eV$ and the momentum is $p = mv = \sqrt{2meV}$ (M1).

The de Broglie wavelength is therefore

$$\lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2meV}} = \frac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{\sqrt{2 \times 9.11 \times 10^{-31} \times 1.60 \times 10^{-19} \times 3000}}$$

The quantity under the square root is $8.75 \times 10^{-46}$, whose square root is $2.96 \times 10^{-23}$ (A1). This gives

$$\lambda = \frac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{2.96 \times 10^{-23}} = 2.24 \times 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{m} = 22.4\ \mathrm{pm}$$

The wavelength is about 22 pm, which is roughly a tenth of the spacing between the graphite planes, so diffraction is expected.

(b) The ring radius is $r = 2L\lambda/d$ and the wavelength is proportional to $V^{-1/2}$, so $r$ is proportional to $V^{-1/2}$ and therefore $V$ is proportional to $r^{-2}$ (M1).

At 3.00 kV the ring radius is $r_1 = 2 \times 0.140 \times 2.24 \times 10^{-11} / 2.13 \times 10^{-10} = 2.95 \times 10^{-2}\ \mathrm{m}$, which is the 2.95 cm marked on the figure (A1).

To reduce the radius to 2.50 cm, the ratio of the radii is $2.95/2.50 = 1.18$. Since the voltage varies as the inverse square of the radius,

$$V_2 = 3.00\ \mathrm{kV} \times (1.18)^2 = 3.00 \times 1.39 = 4.17\ \mathrm{kV}$$ (A1)

The voltage must be increased, not decreased, because a faster electron has a shorter wavelength and is diffracted through a smaller angle.

(c) A ring pattern is a diffraction pattern, and diffraction is a property of waves. If the electrons behaved only as particles they would travel in straight lines from the film and the screen would show a single bright spot where the undeflected beam strikes it (M1). Instead the intensity on the screen varies with angle in maxima and minima that depend on the wavelength and on the spacing of the crystal planes, which is the signature of wave interference (A1).

The rings are sharp only if every electron arrives with the same wavelength. Since $\lambda = h/\sqrt{2meV}$, the wavelength depends on the accelerating voltage, and electrons that leave the gun with slightly different kinetic energies have slightly different wavelengths. Each wavelength produces its ring at a slightly different radius, so a spread of speeds smears the ring into a broad band and eventually washes the pattern out (A1). The accelerating voltage therefore has to be stable, and the film has to be thin so that electrons lose little energy within it.

(d) The proton has the same speed as the electron, so its momentum is larger in the ratio of the masses. The de Broglie wavelength of the proton is

$$\lambda_p = \frac{h}{m_p v}$$

with $v = \sqrt{2eV/m_e} = 3.25 \times 10^{7}\ \mathrm{m\ s^{-1}}$ (M1). Substituting,

$$\lambda_p = \frac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{1.67 \times 10^{-27} \times 3.25 \times 10^{7}} = 1.22 \times 10^{-14}\ \mathrm{m}$$ (A1)

This is about $5.5 \times 10^{-4}$ of the electron wavelength, the same ratio as $m_e/m_p$. The ring radius is proportional to the wavelength, so the first-order ring would fall from 2.95 cm to

$$r_p = 2.95\ \mathrm{cm} \times 5.5 \times 10^{-4} = 1.6 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{cm} = 0.016\ \mathrm{mm}$$ (A1)

which is far smaller than the beam itself. The rings would therefore not be resolved: the pattern would collapse to a single spot indistinguishable from the undeflected beam, and the experiment would give no evidence of diffraction at this speed. To see proton diffraction the protons would need a much lower speed, and hence a much lower accelerating voltage, because the wavelength has to be comparable with the plane spacing of about 0.2 nm.

Markscheme notes

Part (a): M1 for $p = \sqrt{2meV}$, A1 for the evaluated wavelength. Accept 2.2 to 2.3 x 10^-11 m, or 22 to 23 pm. A candidate who uses $E = hf$ for the electron scores nothing here; the de Broglie relation is required.

Part (b): M1 for $V$ proportional to $r^{-2}$, A1 for the ratio 2.95/2.50 or 1.18, A1 for 4.2 kV. The commonest error is to scale the voltage linearly with the radius and obtain about 2.5 kV; that scores M1 at most. Accept any answer from 4.1 to 4.3 kV.

Part (c): M1 for saying that particles alone would give one spot, A1 for diffraction or interference as the wave signature, A1 for the wavelength-spread argument. A candidate who writes only that electrons are waves without linking the pattern to wavelength and plane spacing scores M1.

Part (d): M1 for $\lambda_p = h/(m_p v)$ or for the ratio $m_e/m_p$, A1 for the wavelength about 1.2 x 10^-14 m, A1 for the conclusion that the rings are unresolvable. Accept the ratio argument in place of the absolute value if the conclusion is drawn.

Why this question is hard

The item is built around an inversion. The forward calculation, from an accelerating voltage to a de Broglie wavelength and then to a ring radius, is routine and is what most textbooks practise. Part (b) asks for the voltage that produces a stated radius, and the relation between the two is not linear. Because $\lambda = h/\sqrt{2meV}$ and $r = 2L\lambda/d$, the radius varies as $V^{-1/2}$, so the voltage varies as $r^{-2}$ and the ratio of the radii has to be squared. The natural move, scaling the voltage in proportion to the radius, gives 2.54 kV instead of 4.17 kV, and it fails in the direction that matters: it would have the candidate reduce the voltage to shrink the ring, when in fact a shorter wavelength requires a faster electron.

The figure supplies the geometry that the question needs and nothing more. The 2.95 cm marked on the screen is the value the candidate must reproduce in part (b), so the figure acts as a consistency check on the forward calculation and as the starting point for the inverse one. The distance L, the plane spacing d and the accelerating voltage are all given, and the ring itself is drawn at the radius that the calculation produces, so a candidate who obtains a very different radius can see the discrepancy. The figure is generated from the same expression the markscheme uses, which is what keeps the drawn geometry and the quoted answer from drifting apart.

Part (c) asks for the physics behind the pattern rather than the arithmetic. A ring is only evidence of wave behaviour if the alternative is stated, which is that a purely particle description predicts a single spot. The sharpness argument follows from the same relation used in part (a): the wavelength depends on the kinetic energy, so a spread of energies is a spread of wavelengths and therefore a spread of ring radii. This connects the two halves of the item and gives the candidate a reason to care that the voltage is stabilised.

Part (d) extends the wavelength relation to a different particle at the same speed rather than the same energy, which is the discriminating choice. The ratio of the wavelengths is then the inverse ratio of the masses, about 1/1836, so the proton ring is roughly a thousandth of a millimetre across and cannot be resolved. A candidate who instead gives the proton the same kinetic energy as the electron obtains a wavelength larger by $\sqrt{m_p/m_e}$, about 43 times the electron wavelength, and would predict a pattern that is easier to see rather than impossible. The two readings differ by a factor of about 79, so the item separates them cleanly.
