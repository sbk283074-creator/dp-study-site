The coding that leaves the mean alone but doubles the spread

Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL · MATH-AHL4.3-001

SL 4.3 (measures of central tendency and dispersion; effect of constant changes to the data)P2 · Section B16 marksdifficulty 4technology: permittedCalculateDetermineFindExplain

Question

The data set $A$ is $\{4, 5, 6, 7, 8\}$, and the set $B$ is obtained from $A$ by the coding $b=2a-6$. In the final parts a different set $x$ is coded as $w=1.5x-4$.

  1. [4 marks](a)

    Calculate the mean and the standard deviation of the set $A$.

    (Calculate)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    Determine the mean and the standard deviation of the coded values $2a-6$, and explain why the mean changes by the whole transformation while the standard deviation is affected only by the multiplicative part.

    (Determine)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    A different set $x$ is coded as $w=1.5x-4$, and the coded values have mean $14$ and standard deviation $4.5$. Find the mean and the standard deviation of $x$.

    (Find)
  4. [4 marks](d)

    Explain why subtracting $4$ leaves the standard deviation unchanged while multiplying by $1.5$ multiplies it, and give one reason a researcher might code data.

    (Explain)

Total: 16 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) The mean of $A$ is $\frac{4+5+6+7+8}{5}=6$ (M1)(A1). The deviations from the mean are $-2$, $-1$, $0$, $1$ and $2$, whose squares are $4$, $1$, $0$, $1$ and $4$ (M1). The variance is $\frac{10}{5}=2$, so the standard deviation is $\sqrt{2}=1.41$ to three significant figures (A1). The divisor is $5$ because these values are treated as the whole population.

(b) Coding with $b=2a-6$ gives $2$, $4$, $6$, $8$ and $10$, whose mean is $\frac{30}{5}=6$ (M1)(A1). The deviations are doubled, so each squared deviation is multiplied by $4$ and the variance becomes $4\times 2=8$ (M1), giving a standard deviation of $\sqrt{8}=2\sqrt{2}=2.83$ (A1). The mean changes by the whole transformation because every value is shifted and scaled, while the standard deviation ignores the shift of $-6$ and is scaled only by the factor $2$.

(c) Since the coding is $w=1.5x-4$, the mean satisfies $14=1.5\bar{x}-4$ (M1), so $1.5\bar{x}=18$ and $\bar{x}=12$ (A1). The standard deviation is unaffected by the subtraction of $4$, so $4.5=1.5\sigma_x$ (M1), giving $\sigma_x=\frac{4.5}{1.5}=3$ (A1). The inverse transformation is obtained by solving the coding rule rather than applying it again.

(d) Subtracting $4$ translates every value by the same amount, so all deviations from the mean are unchanged and the standard deviation is unchanged (M1)(A1). Multiplying by $1.5$ scales every deviation by $1.5$, so the variance is multiplied by $1.5^2$ and the standard deviation by $1.5$ (A1). A researcher might code data to remove an awkward constant, such as a large offset, so that the numbers are easier to handle later (R1). Only the multiplicative part of the coding changes the spread.

Markscheme notes

(a) Award (M1) for the sum and (A1) for the mean $6$. The second (M1) is for the squared deviations, which may be listed rather than summed. The (A1) is for the variance $2$ or the standard deviation $\sqrt{2}$; accept $1.41$ but do not accept a standard deviation quoted without the variance shown.

(b) The (M1)(A1) pair is for the coded values and their mean $6$. The second (M1) is for recognising that the variance is multiplied by $4$, and the final (A1) for $2\sqrt{2}$. A candidate who recomputes from the coded list earns full credit, but the explanation must still refer to the multiplier.

(c) The (M1) is for forming $14=1.5\bar{x}-4$, and the (A1) for $\bar{x}=12$. The second (M1) is for $4.5=1.5\sigma_x$, with the final (A1) for $\sigma_x=3$. Follow through from an incorrect coding equation is allowed if the algebra is consistent.

(d) The (M1)(A1) pair is for the translation argument, and the (A1) for the scaling of the standard deviation by $1.5$. The (R1) is for any valid reason for coding, such as simplifying large or awkward numbers. A candidate who claims the subtraction changes the spread forfeits the reasoning mark.

Why this question is hard

The item is built on the algebra of constant changes to a data set. For set $A$ the mean is $6$ and the variance is $2$. Coding by $b=2a-6$ produces the set $2$, $4$, $6$, $8$, $10$, which has the same mean of $6$ but a variance of $8$. The two sets therefore sit at exactly the same place on the number line while the second is visibly more spread out, which is the contrast the title names.

The intended route separates the two parts of a linear coding. A shift of $-6$ moves every value by the same amount, so every deviation from the mean is unchanged and the standard deviation is untouched. A multiplier of $2$ scales every deviation by $2$, so each squared deviation is scaled by $4$ and the variance by $4$; the standard deviation, being a square root, is scaled by $2$ alone. This is why $\sqrt{8}=2\sqrt{2}$.

Part (c) reverses the direction. Given the coded mean $14$ and the coded standard deviation $4.5$, the candidate must solve $14=1.5\bar{x}-4$ and $4.5=1.5\sigma_x$, obtaining $12$ and $3$. The common error is to apply the coding rule again rather than invert it, which produces a mean of $17$ instead of $12$. A second error is to divide by $1.5^2$ rather than $1.5$ when recovering the standard deviation, confusing variance with standard deviation. The numbers were chosen so that the coded mean equals the original mean, which disguises the shift, and so that the inverse values are whole numbers.
