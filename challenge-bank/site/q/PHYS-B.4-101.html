B.4 Thermodynamics

Physics HL · PHYS-B.4-101

Theme B: The particulate nature of matterB.4 (Thermodynamics: first law, internal energy, work, heat engine efficiency, second law)P1 · Section A5 marksdifficulty 4technology: allowedCalculateDetermineState

Question

Five independent multiple-choice questions on thermodynamics. Each one stands alone, so you can attempt any of them without the others. Throughout, take the gas to be ideal unless a question says otherwise, and remember that the first law relates the change in internal energy to both the thermal energy transferred and the work done. Use $R = 8.31\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}$ where it is needed.

  1. [1 mark](a)

    A gas is compressed adiabatically. Which statement about the gas is correct?

    (State)
  2. [1 mark](b)

    A gas absorbs $500\ \mathrm{J}$ of thermal energy and expands, doing $200\ \mathrm{J}$ of work on its surroundings. What is the change in its internal energy?

    (Calculate)
  3. [1 mark](c)

    A heat engine takes in $900\ \mathrm{J}$ from a reservoir at $600\ \mathrm{K}$ and rejects heat to a reservoir at $300\ \mathrm{K}$. It does $300\ \mathrm{J}$ of work in each cycle. What are its actual efficiency and the maximum efficiency possible between these temperatures?

    (Calculate)
  4. [1 mark](d)

    One mole of an ideal gas expands isothermally at $300\ \mathrm{K}$ from a volume $V$ to a volume $2V$. How much work is done by the gas?

    (Calculate)
  5. [1 mark](e)

    Which of the following processes is permitted by the second law of thermodynamics?

    (Determine)

Total: 5 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) A. Adiabatic means $Q=0$, so the first law $\Delta U = Q - W_{\text{by}}$ becomes $\Delta U = -W_{\text{by}} = W_{\text{on}}$. Compressing the gas means work is done *on* it, so $\Delta U>0$ and the temperature rises. (A1) B confuses adiabatic with isothermal and D then inherits that confusion.

(b) A. With $W_{\text{by}}=+200\ \mathrm{J}$ and $Q=+500\ \mathrm{J}$, $\Delta U = 500-200=+300\ \mathrm{J}$. (A1) The sign convention is the whole question: B adds, C keeps the magnitude but flips the sign.

(c) A. The achieved efficiency is $\eta = W/Q_{\text{in}} = 300/900 = 0.333$. The Carnot limit depends only on the reservoir temperatures: $\eta_{C}=1-T_{C}/T_{H}=1-300/600=0.500$. (A1) The two numbers answer different questions and D, at $67\%$, comes from subtracting the achieved efficiency from one and so reporting the rejected fraction of the input as if it were the useful output.

(d) A. For an isothermal change $pV=nRT$ is constant, so $W=\int p\,dV = nRT\ln(V_{2}/V_{1}) = 8.31\times300\times\ln 2 = 1.73\times10^{3}\ \mathrm{J}$. (A1) B is $nRT$ with the logarithm omitted, and C treats the pressure as falling linearly so that a mean pressure could be used.

(e) A. Free expansion into a vacuum does no work and involves no heat transfer, so $\Delta U=0$ for an ideal gas, but the entropy increases because the gas occupies a larger volume. The second law forbids the other three: B is the Clausius statement, C is the Kelvin statement, and D contradicts the entropy form directly. (A1)

Markscheme notes

One mark per question, no negative marking. (a) Accept any answer that identifies the internal energy as increasing by the work done on the gas. (b) Candidates using the opposite sign convention consistently, so that work done on the gas is positive, should still be credited if their arithmetic and conclusion match their convention. (c) Accept $33$ or $33.3\%$ and $50\%$; a candidate who quotes the Carnot value as the achieved efficiency has not distinguished the two ideas and earns nothing. (d) Accept $1.7$ to $1.73\ \mathrm{kJ}$; note that $\ln 2$ is the only place a logarithm appears in this subtopic and it is frequently forgotten. (e) Accept any correct statement of why free expansion is permitted; a candidate who says it is permitted because no heat flows has the right answer for the wrong reason, since the decisive point is that the entropy increases. Note that B and C are both statements of the second law rather than the first, so a candidate who justifies rejecting them by saying energy would not be conserved has used the wrong law and should not be credited. Candidates often find (e) the hardest item despite it requiring no calculation, because it asks which process is permitted rather than which is forbidden; three of the four options are familiar prohibitions, so the correct answer looks wrong at first reading.

Why this question is hard

This cluster exists because the first law of thermodynamics is a signed equation in three quantities and students routinely lose marks on the signs rather than on the physics. Items (a) and (b) isolate exactly that: in (a) the compression means the work term enters with the opposite sign to an expansion, and in (b) the gas both receives heat and spends energy doing work, so the change in internal energy is the difference, not the sum. The distractors are the four ways of getting a sign wrong.

Items (c) and (d) isolate a different and more interesting confusion: the difference between what a device actually achieves and what is theoretically available. In (c) the engine's real efficiency and the Carnot efficiency are computed from entirely different information, one from the energy flows and the other from the reservoir temperatures alone, and a candidate who reaches for the temperatures to compute the achieved efficiency has conflated them. In (d) the work of an isothermal expansion is logarithmic, which surprises students who expect a pressure-times-volume product; the $2.49\ \mathrm{kJ}$ distractor is $nRT$ and the $1.25\ \mathrm{kJ}$ distractor is the mean-pressure approximation that would be correct if the pressure fell linearly with volume, which it does not. Item (e) closes the cluster by asking which process the second law permits, and the correct answer is the one that increases entropy, which is the unifying idea behind the whole cluster.
