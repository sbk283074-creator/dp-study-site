Exact coordinates on the unit circle for an obtuse angle, the tangent of that angle, the chord between two symmetric points and the area of the sector

Question

The diagram shows the unit circle. The point $P$ lies on the circle in the second quadrant with an angle of $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ measured from the positive $x$-axis, and the point $Q$ lies on the circle in the first quadrant with an angle of $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$. The sector $POQ$ is shaded and the chord $PQ$ is drawn.

  1. [2 marks](a)

    State the exact coordinates of $P$ and of $Q$.

    (State)
  2. [2 marks](b)

    Find the exact value of $\tan\left( \dfrac{5\pi}{6} \right)$.

    (Find)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    Show that the length of the chord $PQ$ is $\sqrt{3}$.

    (Show that)
  4. [3 marks](d)

    Hence find the exact area of the shaded sector $POQ$.

    (Hence)

The sector $POQ$ is shaded and the chord $PQ$ is drawn. (a) State the exact coordinates of $P$ and of $Q$. [2 marks] (State) (b) Find the exact value of $\tan\left( \dfrac{5\pi}{6} \right)$. [2 marks] (Find) (c) Show that the length of the chord $PQ$ is $\sqrt{3}$. [4 marks] (Show that) (d) Hence find the exact area of the shaded sector $POQ$. [3 marks] (Hence)
Reveal the answer

(a) On the unit circle a point at angle $\theta$ has coordinates
$(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$. The reference angle for $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ is
$\pi - \dfrac{5\pi}{6} = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$, and because $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ is in the second
quadrant its cosine is negative and its sine is positive:

$$\cos\left( \frac{5\pi}{6} \right) = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \qquad
\sin\left( \frac{5\pi}{6} \right) = \frac{1}{2}.$$

Hence

$$P\left( -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \; \frac{1}{2} \right).$$ (A1)

For $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, which is in the first quadrant, both coordinates are positive:

$$Q\left( \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \; \frac{1}{2} \right).$$ (A1)

The two points have the same $y$-coordinate, so the chord $PQ$ is horizontal, and $P$ and $Q$
are mirror images of one another in the $y$-axis.

(b) The tangent of an angle is the quotient of the sine and the cosine, which on the unit
circle is the quotient of the two coordinates:

$$\tan\left( \frac{5\pi}{6} \right)
= \frac{\sin(5\pi/6)}{\cos(5\pi/6)}
= \frac{\;1/2\;}{-\sqrt{3}/2}.$$ (M1)

Dividing by the fraction $-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ multiplies by its reciprocal:

$$\tan\left( \frac{5\pi}{6} \right) = \frac{1}{2} \times \left( -\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \right)
= -\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}.$$ (A1)

The value is negative, as it must be for an angle in the second quadrant, where the sine is
positive and the cosine is negative.

(c) The chord $PQ$ joins $P\left( -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$ and
$Q\left( \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2} \right)$. Because the $y$-coordinates are equal, the
chord is horizontal and its length is the difference of the $x$-coordinates:

$$PQ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - \left( -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right)
= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \sqrt{3}.$$ (M1)(A1)

Alternatively, the angle $POQ$ is

$$\frac{5\pi}{6} - \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{4\pi}{6} = \frac{2\pi}{3},$$

and $OP$ and $OQ$ are both radii of the unit circle, so triangle $POQ$ is isosceles with two
sides of length $1$ and included angle $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$. The chord is therefore the base of
that triangle:

$$PQ = 2(1)\sin\left( \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{2\pi}{3} \right)
= 2\sin\left( \frac{\pi}{3} \right) = 2 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \sqrt{3}.$$ (M1)(A1)

Both routes give $\sqrt{3}$, as required. (AG)

(d) The sector $POQ$ has radius $r = 1$ and angle $\theta = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$, so its area is

$$A = \frac{1}{2} r^{2} \theta
= \frac{1}{2}(1)^{2} \left( \frac{2\pi}{3} \right)
= \frac{\pi}{3}.$$ (M1)(A1)(A1)

The area of the shaded sector is $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ square units. Note that the chord length found
in part (c) is not needed here; the area of a sector depends only on the radius and the angle
between the radii, whereas the chord depends on the angle as well but is not required once the
angle is known.

Markscheme notes

(a) (A1) for $P\left( -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \frac{1}{2} \right)$, (A1) for $Q\left( \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \frac{1}{2} \right)$. Accept the coordinates in either order provided the labels match. An answer of $P\left( \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \frac{1}{2} \right)$ scores (A0) for the first mark: the cosine of an angle in the second quadrant is negative. (b) (M1) for forming the quotient of the coordinates, (A1) for $-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$ or the equivalent $-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$. Accept $-0.577$ only if given to at least three significant figures. A positive value scores (M1)(A0). (c) (M1) for a valid method, either the horizontal difference of the two $x$-coordinates or the isosceles-triangle relation with the correct included angle, (A1) for reaching $\sqrt{3}$, (M1)(A1) for the second valid method or (AG) where the student completes the algebra to the stated result. Do not award the method mark for merely asserting that the chord is $\sqrt{3}$. (d) (M1) for using $A = \frac{1}{2}r^{2}\theta$ with $r = 1$, (A1) for the correct angle $\frac{2\pi}{3}$, (A1) for $\frac{\pi}{3}$. Award follow-through from an incorrect angle in (c) only if the angle used is consistent with the coordinates given in (a).

Why this question is hard

This item is set on Paper 1, where no calculator is available, so every value has to
come from the geometry of the unit circle. That is the point of the diagram: it supplies the
two angles and nothing else, and the coordinates, the tangent, the chord and the sector area all
have to be reconstructed from the circle itself.

Part (a) is short and decides the rest of the item. The angle 5pi/6 is obtuse, so the point lies
in the second quadrant, where the cosine is negative and the sine is positive. The reference
angle is pi/6, whose coordinates are familiar, and the sign is the only thing that changes. A
student who applies the first-quadrant values without adjusting the sign writes
(sqrt(3)/2, 1/2) for P and then finds, in part (c), a chord of length zero, because the two
points would coincide. That is a useful internal warning: the coordinates in (a) have to be
different from those in (b) or the chord cannot have the length the question claims.

Part (b) asks for the tangent, and the safest route on the unit circle is to form the quotient
of the two coordinates rather than to recall a value for the tangent of 5pi/6. The quotient
involves a compound fraction, and simplifying it requires multiplying by the reciprocal of
-sqrt(3)/2, which is where the sign is easily lost. The result -sqrt(3)/3 is negative, and the
sign is a free check, since the tangent is negative in the second quadrant.

Part (c) can be done in two ways and both are accepted. Because the two points share a
y-coordinate, the chord is horizontal and its length is simply the difference of the
x-coordinates, which is sqrt(3). The second route is to notice that the triangle POQ is
isosceles with two radii of length 1 and an included angle of 2pi/3, so the chord is
2 sin(pi/3), which is also sqrt(3). The second route is worth making explicit because it is the
one that generalises: the same reasoning gives the chord of any sector on the unit circle. The
item asks the student to show the result rather than to state it, so a bare assertion of sqrt(3)
does not earn the method mark.

Part (d) uses the sector area formula, which needs only the radius and the angle. The angle is
the same 2pi/3 that part (c) established, so the part is genuinely a consequence of the earlier
work, and the radius is 1 because the circle is the unit circle. The area pi/3 follows. A
student who tries to use the chord length here has misread the question: the chord is a
straight line between the two points, whereas the sector is bounded by the arc, and the two
quantities are not interchangeable.
