3.8 Unequal asset lives, a replacement chain, and a common cost that cancels only in part

Business management SL · BM-3.8-002

Unit 3: Finance and accounts3.8 Investment appraisal (payback period, average rate of return, net present value); 3.2 Sources of financeP2 · Section B16 marksdifficulty 4technology: permittedCalculateJustifyDetermine

Question

Vastra Teknik must choose between two machines for a four-year contract. The contract will
generate net cash inflows of $\pounds 90{,}000$ a year for each of the four years whichever machine
is used, so the choice turns entirely on the costs. The company's cost of capital is $10\%$ per
year and all cash flows arise at the ends of years.

Machine P costs $\pounds 120{,}000$ and has a working life of two years, so it must be replaced
by an identical machine at the end of year 2 at the same price. It is decommissioned at the end of
year 2 and again at the end of year 4.

Machine Q costs $\pounds 230{,}000$ and has a working life of four years, so it is not replaced.
It is decommissioned once, at the end of year 4.

The decommissioning charge is $\pounds D$ on each occasion it arises, and is the same for both
machines. Assume initially that $D = 20{,}000$.

(a) Calculate the payback period for each machine. \tag{3}

(b) Calculate the net present value of each machine over the four years, and state which machine
should be chosen on this basis. \tag{5}

(c) The finance director argues that the decommissioning charge can be ignored, because both
machines incur it and it will therefore cancel. Explain, with a calculation, why this is only
partly correct. \tag{4}

(d) Determine the value of $D$ at which the recommendation would change. \tag{4}

  1. [3 marks](a)

    Calculate the payback period for each machine, taking no account for now of the replacement that machine P requires at the end of year 2.

Reveal the answer

(a) Machine P returns $\pounds 90{,}000$ a year against an outlay of $\pounds 120{,}000$, so

$$\text{payback}_{P} = \frac{120{,}000}{90{,}000} = 1.33 \text{ years}$$

which is about one year and four months (A1). Machine Q recovers $\pounds 230{,}000$ at the same
rate, so

$$\text{payback}_{Q} = \frac{230{,}000}{90{,}000} = 2.56 \text{ years}$$

or roughly two years and seven months (A1). On payback, P is the clear winner, recovering its cost
more than a year sooner (A1). Note that payback ignores the second purchase P must make at the end
of year 2, which is one reason the ranking it produces should not be trusted on its own.

(b) The four-year annuity factor at $10\%$ is

$$\frac{1}{1.1} + \frac{1}{1.1^{2}} + \frac{1}{1.1^{3}} + \frac{1}{1.1^{4}} = 3.1699$$

so the present value of the inflows, identical for both machines, is

$$90{,}000 \times 3.1699 = \pounds 285{,}288 \tag{A1}$$

For P the outflows are the purchase at $t = 0$, the replacement at $t = 2$, and decommissioning at
$t = 2$ and $t = 4$ (M1):

$$120{,}000 + \frac{120{,}000}{1.1^{2}} + \frac{20{,}000}{1.1^{2}} + \frac{20{,}000}{1.1^{4}}
= 120{,}000 + 99{,}174 + 16{,}529 + 13{,}660 = \pounds 249{,}363 \tag{A1}$$

For Q the outflows are the single purchase at $t = 0$ and decommissioning at $t = 4$ (M1):

$$230{,}000 + \frac{20{,}000}{1.1^{4}} = 230{,}000 + 13{,}660 = \pounds 243{,}660 \tag{A1}$$

Hence

$$\text{NPV}_{P} = 285{,}288 - 249{,}363 = \pounds 35{,}925, \qquad
\text{NPV}_{Q} = 285{,}288 - 243{,}660 = \pounds 41{,}628 \tag{A1}$$

Both are positive, so both are worth undertaking; on net present value Q should be chosen, by
$\pounds 5{,}702$ (A1). This reverses the payback ranking in (a), and it also reverses the average
rate of return, which favours P at $16.7\%$ against $12.0\%$ for Q precisely because P is measured
against a much smaller capital base.

(c) The director is right about one of the two payments and wrong about the other. Both machines
pay $D$ at the end of year 4, and that payment is multiplied by the same discount factor
$\frac{1}{1.1^{4}}$ in both columns, so it is common and does cancel (A1). Machine P, however, also
pays $D$ at the end of year 2, and Q pays nothing at that date; this payment has no counterpart to
cancel against (A1). Its present value is

$$\frac{20{,}000}{1.1^{2}} = \pounds 16{,}529 \tag{A1}$$

so the decommissioning charge contributes $\pounds 16{,}529$ to the difference between the two
machines — not zero, and not the $\pounds 33{,}058$ that a candidate who doubled it would report. The
claim is therefore only partly correct: the charge is irrelevant to the comparison in year 4 and
decisive in year 2 (A1).

The practical consequence is that the charge is not a detail. Without it the two machines would
differ by

$$(120{,}000 + 99{,}174) - 230{,}000 = -\pounds 10{,}826$$

in P's favour, and P would be chosen. It is the year-2 payment alone, worth $\pounds 16{,}529$, that
overturns a gap of $\pounds 10{,}826$ and turns the decision round.

(d) Let the decommissioning charge be $D$. The year-4 payments cancel as before, so the difference
between the present values of the two cost streams is

$$\left(120{,}000 + \frac{120{,}000}{1.1^{2}} + \frac{D}{1.1^{2}} + \frac{D}{1.1^{4}}\right)
- \left(230{,}000 + \frac{D}{1.1^{4}}\right) = -10{,}826 + \frac{D}{1.1^{2}} \tag{M1}$$

where the two $\frac{D}{1.1^{4}}$ terms have been cancelled (A1). Q is preferred while this is
positive and P while it is negative, so the recommendation changes where

$$-10{,}826 + \frac{D}{1.21} = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad D = 10{,}826 \times 1.21
= \pounds 13{,}100 \tag{A1}$$

At $D = 13{,}100$ the two machines are exactly equivalent, and the firm is indifferent (A1). Since
the actual charge of $\pounds 20{,}000$ exceeds $\pounds 13{,}100$, Q is confirmed as the better
choice — but the margin is $\pounds 5{,}702$, and a decommissioning quote that came in under
$\pounds 13{,}100$ would switch the decision to P.

Markscheme notes

(a) One mark for each payback figure, one for the comparison. Accept 1.3 and 2.6 years or the equivalent in months. Allow follow-through to (b) from a payback error. (b) One mark for the present value of the inflows, one for identifying the four outflow terms for P, one for the P total, one for the Q total, one for the two NPVs and the recommendation. Do not award the recommendation mark without both NPVs. Condone rounding to the nearest pound throughout; the exact totals are 249,362.75 and 243,660.27. A candidate who omits the replacement purchase should lose the term mark but may still earn the method mark for discounting. (c) One mark for saying the year-4 payment cancels, one for saying the year-2 payment does not, one for the figure 16,529, one for the conclusion that the claim is partly right. Credit the figure even if the candidate describes it as 'the extra cost of P'. (d) One mark for setting up the difference with the year-4 terms cancelled, one for the equation, one for D = 13,100, one for stating which machine wins on either side of it. A candidate who cancels both D terms gets -10,826 and should be held at the first mark: the point of the part is precisely that the cancellation is only partial.

Why this question is hard

This item turns on a piece of arithmetic that looks like bookkeeping and is in fact the
whole decision. Two identical charges are described in the same words, and the instinct is to treat
them the same way. They are not the same: one occurs at a date when both machines are spending money
and the other at a date when only one is. Discounting preserves that difference, nominal addition
destroys it, and the item is built so that the destroyed difference is larger than the gap it
overturns.

The naive path is to take the finance director at his word. If both decommissioning payments are
dropped, P costs $120{,}000 + 99{,}174 = \pounds 219{,}174$ against Q's $\pounds 230{,}000$, P wins
by $\pounds 10{,}826$, and no value of $D$ can change that — the answer to part (d) becomes 'the
charge makes no difference'. That is a confident, well-argued, entirely wrong answer, and it is
wrong because of one omission rather than one mistake. Nothing in the stem says the two payments
happen at different times; the candidate has to read the replacement chain and notice it.

What makes the item worth sixteen marks rather than six is that the correct treatment then has to be
carried through three different appraisal methods that disagree with each other. Payback favours P
by more than a year. Average rate of return favours P by nearly five percentage points, for the
reason that P is measured against a smaller capital base and is therefore flattered by any
percentage measure. Net present value favours Q, because it is the only one of the three that sees
the second purchase at the end of year 2 and the second decommissioning charge that comes with it.
A candidate who reports all three rankings and then recommends Q on the grounds that NPV accounts
for the timing of every cash flow over the full horizon has done the real work; a candidate who
reports one ranking and stops has not, however accurate the arithmetic.

The item is adapted from a UK A-Level investment-appraisal question that asks which of two machines
to buy given their purchase prices and running costs. The replacement chain, the decommissioning
charge, the partial cancellation and the break-even value of $D$ are all new here. The source asks
for a decision; this asks for the decision, for the term that produces it, and for the parameter
value at which it flips. That last move is what forces the cancellation to be understood rather than
performed, and it is why the item sits at difficulty 4 rather than 3.

Challenge lever
Both machines carry the same decommissioning charge, so the obvious move is to cancel it and compare the rest. But the two machines do not decommission at the same time: one pays the charge twice over the four-year horizon and the other pays it once. The payment at the end of year four is genuinely common and does cancel; the payment at the end of year two is not, and it is the single term that decides the whole question. Cancelling both removes exactly the term that carries the decision.
