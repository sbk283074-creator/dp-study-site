5.4 Two distinct tangents to a parabola pass through one exterior point, so the point named in the question is not the point of contact

Question

The curve $C$ has equation $y = x^2$. The point $P(3,\,8)$ is not on $C$. The figure shows $C$, the point $P$, and the point of $C$ that lies vertically above $P$.

  1. [2 marks](a)

    Show that the tangent to $C$ at the point where $x = t$ has equation $y = 2tx - t^2$.

    (Show that)
  2. [2 marks](b)

    The point $P(3,\,8)$ lies on the tangent to $C$ at the point where $x = t$. Find the two possible values of $t$, and the coordinates of the two corresponding points of $C$.

    (Find)
  3. [2 marks](c)

    Find the area of the triangle formed by these two tangents and the $y$-axis.

    (Find)

[2 marks] (Find)
Reveal the answer

(a) Differentiating $y = x^2$ gives $\frac{dy}{dx} = 2x$, so at the point where $x = t$ the gradient of the tangent is $2t$ (M1). The tangent passes through the point $(t,\,t^2)$, so its equation is $y - t^2 = 2t(x - t)$. Expanding gives $y = 2tx - 2t^2 + t^2 = 2tx - t^2$ (A1).

(b) Substituting the coordinates of $P(3,\,8)$ into $y = 2tx - t^2$ gives $8 = 2t(3) - t^2$, that is $t^2 - 6t + 8 = 0$ (M1). This factorises as $(t - 2)(t - 4) = 0$, so $t = 2$ or $t = 4$ (A1). The corresponding points of $C$ are $(2,\,4)$ and $(4,\,16)$. Note that neither of these is $P$ itself, and that both tangents pass through the single point $P$. It is worth checking that the two roots are genuinely distinct: the discriminant of $t^2 - 6t + 8$ is $36 - 32 = 4$, which is positive, so the two tangents are different lines and not a repeated one. The quadratic would have had a repeated root only if $P$ had been on the curve itself, which the figure shows it is not.

(c) The two tangents are $y = 4x - 4$ (from $t = 2$) and $y = 8x - 16$ (from $t = 4$) (M1). The first meets the $y$-axis at $(0,\,-4)$ and the second at $(0,\,-16)$, so the side of the triangle lying along the $y$-axis has length $|-4 - (-16)| = 12$. The two tangents meet each other at $P(3,\,8)$, so the perpendicular height of the triangle measured from $P$ to the $y$-axis is $3$. The area is therefore $\frac{1}{2} \times 12 \times 3 = 18$ (A1).

Markscheme notes

(a) (M1) for differentiating to get gradient $2t$; (A1) for the correct line through $(t,\,t^2)$, simplified. A candidate who writes $y = 2tx + t^2$ has the sign of the constant term wrong and should lose the accuracy mark; the substitution in part (b) then produces a quadratic with no real roots, which is the check available to them.

(b) (M1) for substituting $P$ into the tangent and forming a quadratic in $t$; (A1) for both roots *and* both contact points. Award (M1) alone for a candidate who finds only $t = 2$, or who assumes $t = 3$ because the $x$-coordinate of $P$ is 3. Do not penalise a candidate who also writes down $P$ as a point of contact: it is wrong, but if the two correct contact points are given the accuracy mark is earned.

(c) (M1) for finding the two $y$-intercepts, or for identifying the triangle's vertices; (A1) for $18$. Accept any correct method, including integrating or using the determinant formula. A candidate who takes the base along the $y$-axis and the height as the $x$-coordinate of $P$ has the right idea and should be credited; one who uses the distance $|AP|$ or $|BP|$ as a height has misidentified which side is the base.

Why this question is hard

The item exists because the phrase 'the tangent at a point' and the phrase 'a tangent through a point' look almost identical and mean entirely different things. When the point is on the curve there is one tangent and it is found by differentiating at that point. When the point is outside the curve there may be two, they touch elsewhere, and the coordinates of the given point never enter the derivative. A candidate who does not notice the difference differentiates at $x = 3$, obtains the single line $y = 6x - 9$, and reports one tangent for a question that has two.

The figure is what makes the difference visible before any algebra is attempted. The dashed segment rising from $P(3,\,8)$ to the point $(3,\,9)$ on the curve shows that $P$ sits below $C$, and the marked point above it belongs to the curve while $P$ does not. That single segment is the whole content of the first sentence of the question, and it is drawn rather than stated because a candidate who reads it will not make the mistake at all.

Part (b) is where the two roots appear, and it is worth noticing that the quadratic in $t$ is genuine: the condition 'the tangent at $t$ passes through $P$' is one equation in one unknown, and its having two roots is exactly the statement that two tangents can be drawn. Part (c) then closes the configuration by asking for the area of the triangle the two tangents make with the $y$-axis, which forces the candidate to have both tangents rather than one, and to see that they meet at $P$.
