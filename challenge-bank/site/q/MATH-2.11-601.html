2.11 A reflection of a curve in its own axis of symmetry produces an identical curve, so this transformation leaves no trace in the image

Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL · MATH-2.11-601

Topic 2: FunctionsSL 2.11 Transformations of graphs; SL 2.6 The quadratic function f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c: its graph, its vertex and its axis of symmetryP1 · Section A6 marksdifficulty 5technology: not allowedFindWrite downExplain

Question

The graph of $y = f(x)$, where $f(x) = x^2 + bx + c$ for constants $b$ and $c$, is shown in the figure. The graph passes through the two marked points $(0,\,5)$ and $(6,\,5)$, and the dashed vertical line shown is its axis of symmetry.

  1. [2 marks](a)

    Find the values of the constants $b$ and $c$.

    (Find)
  2. [2 marks](b)

    Write down the equation of the axis of symmetry of the graph of $y = f(x)$.

    (Write down)
  3. [2 marks](c)

    The function $g$ is defined by $g(x) = f(6 - x)$. Explain why the graph of $y = g(x)$ is identical to the graph of $y = f(x)$.

    (Explain)

Reveal the answer

(a) The graph passes through $(0,\,5)$, so $f(0) = c = 5$ (M1). It also passes through $(6,\,5)$, so $36 + 6b + 5 = 5$, which gives $6b = -36$ and hence $b = -6$ (A1). The function is therefore $f(x) = x^2 - 6x + 5$.

(b) For a quadratic $f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$ the axis of symmetry is $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$ (M1). Here $a = 1$ and $b = -6$, so the axis is $x = -\frac{-6}{2} = 3$ (A1). The same answer comes out of the figure without the formula. The two marked points $(0,\,5)$ and $(6,\,5)$ have the same $y$-coordinate, so each is the mirror image of the other in the axis, and the axis must lie midway between their $x$-coordinates, at $x = \frac{0 + 6}{2} = 3$. The dashed line drawn in the figure is consistent with both readings, and a candidate may use either.

(c) Substituting $6 - x$ for $x$ in the expression for $f$ (M1) gives $f(6 - x) = (6 - x)^2 - 6(6 - x) + 5 = 36 - 12x + x^2 - 36 + 6x + 5 = x^2 - 6x + 5$, which is exactly $f(x)$ for every value of $x$ (A1). Since $g(x) = f(6 - x) = f(x)$ at every point, the two functions agree everywhere and their graphs coincide. The geometric reason is the one identified in part (b): replacing $x$ by $6 - x$ is reflection in the vertical line $x = 3$, and $x = 3$ is the axis of symmetry of this parabola. Reflecting a figure in its own axis of symmetry leaves it exactly where it was, so the image graph is the original graph. The vertex stays at $(3,\,-4)$, the two roots stay at $x = 1$ and $x = 5$, and nothing about the shape or the position changes.

Markscheme notes

(a) (M1) for using the point $(0,\,5)$ to obtain $c = 5$; (A1) for $b = -6$ from $(6,\,5)$. A candidate who sets up both conditions and solves them simultaneously earns the two marks by the same route. Do not credit a candidate who reads $b = -6$ off the figure's dashed line without substituting: that line gives $b$ only because $a = 1$, and recovering it from the axis is part (b)'s reasoning rather than part (a)'s.

(b) (M1) for the axis formula $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$, or for taking the midpoint of the two marked points; (A1) for $x = 3$. Either route is acceptable. The midpoint argument is the better one here because it uses the figure rather than the algebra, and a candidate who gives it has already understood the symmetry that part (c) turns on.

(c) (M1) for substituting $6 - x$ into the expression for $f$ and expanding it; (A1) for concluding that the two expressions are identical and giving the symmetry as the reason. A candidate who does the algebra but says nothing about reflection has shown *that* the graphs coincide without explaining *why*, which is what the command term asks for: award (M1) and press for the reason. Accept 'the graph is symmetric about $x = 3$ and $x \mapsto 6 - x$ is reflection in that line' in any wording.

Why this question is hard

The item exists because a transformation of a graph is normally visible. Translate a curve and it moves; stretch it and its shape changes; reflect it and it turns over. Part (c) asks for a transformation that does none of these, and the reason is not that the transformation is trivial but that the object has a symmetry which the transformation happens to respect. Reflection in the line $x = 3$ is a genuine, non-identity transformation of the plane; applied to this particular parabola it returns the parabola unchanged, because $x = 3$ is the axis of symmetry of that parabola.

That is why the item is filed under transformations of graphs rather than under the quadratic function. A candidate who treats $g(x) = f(6 - x)$ as 'the same function written differently' has the right answer for the wrong reason, and part (b) is placed before part (c) deliberately, because the axis of symmetry is the bridge between them. Reading the axis off two points of equal height is the same observation as seeing why the reflection leaves no trace, and a candidate who finds the axis by formula alone can still complete part (c) but has not been given the reason it works.

The figure carries the argument rather than decorating it. The two marked points at $(0,\,5)$ and $(6,\,5)$ are the data for part (a) and the evidence for the axis in part (b), and the dashed line shows the axis without naming it, so the picture prompts the symmetry without supplying the equation. A candidate who answers $x = 6$ or $x = -3$ has read the wrong feature of the same picture, and it is the picture that makes that misreading visible to them.
