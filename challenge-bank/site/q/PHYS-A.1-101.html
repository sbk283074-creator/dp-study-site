A.1 Kinematics

Question

Five independent multiple-choice questions on kinematics. Each one stands alone, so you can attempt any of them without the others. Use $g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$ where a numerical value is needed, and give each answer to three significant figures.

  1. [1 mark](a)

    A projectile is launched from level ground at $30\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ at $40^{\circ}$ above the horizontal. Air resistance is negligible. What is the speed of the projectile at the highest point of its path?

    (Calculate)
  2. [1 mark](b)

    Two projectiles are launched from the same point at the same speed, one at an angle $\theta$ and the other at $90^{\circ}-\theta$, where $\theta \ne 45^{\circ}$. Both land at the launch height. Which statement is correct?

    (Determine)
  3. [1 mark](c)

    A ball is released from rest at the same instant that an identical ball at the same height is fired horizontally. Both balls experience air resistance with a drag force proportional to their speed. Which statement is correct?

    (Determine)
  4. [1 mark](d)

    A particle moves along a straight line at $8.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ for $3.0\ \mathrm{s}$ and then at $4.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ in the opposite direction for $2.0\ \mathrm{s}$. What are its average speed and its average velocity over the $5.0\ \mathrm{s}$?

    (Calculate)
  5. [1 mark](e)

    A boat whose speed in still water is $4.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ must cross a river $120\ \mathrm{m}$ wide in which the current flows at $3.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. What is the shortest possible crossing time, and how far downstream does the boat land?

    (Calculate)

Reveal the answer

(a) A. Resolve the launch velocity: the horizontal component is $30\cos 40^{\circ}=22.98$ and the vertical component is $30\sin 40^{\circ}=19.28$. The horizontal component is constant throughout, and at the highest point the vertical component has been reduced to zero by gravity, so the speed there is simply $22.98$, i.e. $23.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. (A1) The near miss is C, which reports the component that has vanished rather than the one that remains.

(b) A. The range is $v^{2}\sin 2\theta/g$, and $\sin\bigl(2(90^{\circ}-\theta)\bigr)=\sin(180^{\circ}-2\theta)=\sin 2\theta$, so the two ranges are equal. The flight time is $2v\sin\theta/g$, which is larger for the larger angle. (A1) Distractor D inverts both conclusions and C gets only the time right.

(c) A. With drag $\mathbf{F}=-k\mathbf{v}$ the component equations are separate: vertically, $m\dot v_{y}=-kv_{y}-mg$ for both balls, with the same initial condition $v_{y}=0$. The horizontal motion therefore cannot influence the descent, and both land together. (A1) This is a genuinely counter-intuitive result and B, C and D are all attempts to make the longer or faster trajectory cost time.

(d) A. Distance $=8.0(3.0)+4.0(2.0)=24+8=32\ \mathrm{m}$; displacement $=24-8=16\ \mathrm{m}$. Dividing by $5.0\ \mathrm{s}$ gives an average speed of $6.4$ and an average velocity of $3.2\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. (A1) The pair is what is being tested: many candidates compute one correctly and then quote it twice.

(e) A. The crossing time is the width divided by the component of the boat's velocity perpendicular to the bank. That component is greatest, at the full $4.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, when the boat points straight across, so $t=120/4.0=30\ \mathrm{s}$ and the drift is $3.0\times30=90\ \mathrm{m}$. (A1) D is the trap: $45\ \mathrm{s}$ and landing opposite is a correct calculation of the *shortest path*, since the resultant speed is then $\sqrt{4.0^{2}-3.0^{2}}=2.65\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $120/2.65=45\ \mathrm{s}$, but the question asks for the shortest time.

Markscheme notes

One mark per question, no negative marking. (a) Accept $23.0$ or $23.1\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. (b) The reasoning mark is implicit in the key: a candidate who states equal ranges but equal times has not earned it. (c) This is the hardest item in the cluster and the one most likely to be guessed wrong; accept any answer that identifies the independence of the components, and note that the result would be different for drag proportional to the square of the speed, where the components no longer separate and the fired ball would in fact land later. (d) Accept fractions; the unit mark is not separate here. (e) Award no follow-through from a mistaken minimum-time aim: the $45\ \mathrm{s}$ figure belongs to a different optimisation and using it for the drift gives $135\ \mathrm{m}$, which is not offered. Candidates who eliminate C and D first, on the grounds that cancelling the current must cost time, are using the strongest route to the key.

Why this question is hard

This cluster is built around one idea: in kinematics, the first move is always to decide which quantities are shared and which are not. In (a) the horizontal component survives and the vertical one does not. In (b) complementary launch angles share a range because the range depends on $\sin 2\theta$, but they do not share a flight time because that depends on $\sin\theta$. In (c) a horizontally fired ball shares its entire vertical motion with a dropped one, and the surprise is that this remains true when linear drag is present, because the drag then acts independently on each component. That last point is the conceptual peak of the cluster: most students have been taught the vacuum case and assume that adding air resistance must break the tie, when in fact it depends on the drag law.

Items (d) and (e) target the same separation instinct in a computational setting. In (d) the distance and the displacement differ by $16\ \mathrm{m}$, and a candidate who computes only one of them halves the question. In (e) the shortest time and the shortest path are different journeys, and the boat cannot achieve both; the $45\ \mathrm{s}$ option is a correct calculation of the wrong thing, which is the most reliable way to separate a candidate who has understood the vector structure from one who has only memorised a formula. A guessing student has no easy route here, because the four options in (e) are all internally consistent descriptions of a real journey.
