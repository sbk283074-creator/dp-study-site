D.3 Motion in electromagnetic fields

Physics HL · PHYS-D.3-101

Theme D: FieldsD.3 (Motion in electromagnetic fields: charged particles in electric and magnetic fields)P1 · Section A5 marksdifficulty 5technology: permittedCalculateDetermineState

Question

Five independent multiple-choice questions on the motion of charged particles in electromagnetic fields. Each one stands alone. Unless a question states otherwise, any magnetic field is uniform and the particle enters it travelling at right angles to the field.

  1. [1 mark](a)

    A charged particle enters a uniform magnetic field at right angles to the field. Which statement about the particle is correct?

    (State)
    1. A. The direction of its velocity changes but the magnitude does not.
    2. B. Both the direction and the magnitude of its velocity change.
    3. C. Neither the direction nor the magnitude of its velocity changes.
    4. D. The magnitude of its velocity changes but the direction does not.
  2. [1 mark](b)

    A proton and an alpha particle enter the same uniform magnetic field at right angles, both moving at the same speed. What is the ratio of the radius of the alpha particle's path to the radius of the proton's path?

    (Calculate)
    1. A. $2$
    2. B. $\dfrac{1}{2}$
    3. C. $4$
    4. D. $1$
  3. [1 mark](c)

    A proton and an alpha particle enter the same uniform magnetic field at right angles, this time with the same kinetic energy. What is the ratio of the radius of the alpha particle's path to the radius of the proton's path?

    (Calculate)
    1. A. $1$
    2. B. $2$
    3. C. $\dfrac{1}{2}$
    4. D. $4$
  4. [1 mark](d)

    A particle of mass $m$ and charge $q$ moves in a circle in a uniform magnetic field of magnitude $B$. What is the period of its motion?

    (Determine)
    1. A. $\dfrac{2\pi m}{qB}$
    2. B. $\dfrac{qB}{2\pi m}$
    3. C. $\dfrac{2\pi q B}{m}$
    4. D. $\dfrac{2\pi m B}{q}$
  5. [1 mark](e)

    In a cyclotron, a charged particle is accelerated each time it crosses the gap between the two dees. Which statement is correct?

    (State)
    1. A. The magnetic field does no work on the particle; the increase in kinetic energy comes from the electric field in the gap.
    2. B. The magnetic field increases the kinetic energy of the particle during each half-turn.
    3. C. The time for each half-turn increases as the particle gains speed.
    4. D. The radius of the path is independent of the speed of the particle.

Total: 5 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) A. The magnetic force is $q\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}$, which is perpendicular to $\mathbf{v}$ by construction, so it does no work and cannot change the speed; it only turns the velocity, producing circular motion. (A1) Every other option in this item attributes some change in speed to the field.

(b) A. Setting $qvB=mv^{2}/r$ gives $r=mv/(qB)$. An alpha particle has mass $4m_{p}$ and charge $2e$, so $r_{\alpha}/r_{p}=(4/1)\times(1/2)=2$. (A1) C, at $4$, is the mass ratio with the charge forgotten.

(c) A. Now the speed is not common, so write $r=\sqrt{2mK}/(qB)$. Then $r_{\alpha}/r_{p}=\sqrt{4}\times(1/2)=2\times0.5=1$: the paths have the same radius. (A1) The contrast with (b) is the whole point of the pair, and B is the answer to the previous question rather than this one.

(d) A. $T=2\pi r/v$ with $r=mv/(qB)$ gives $T=2\pi m/(qB)$; the speed cancels, so the period does not depend on how fast the particle is moving. (A1) B is the reciprocal, the cyclotron frequency, and is the commonest slip here.

(e) A. The magnetic field bends the path and fixes the period; only the electric field in the gap can change the kinetic energy, because only it has a component along the velocity. (A1) C is wrong for a reason worth stating explicitly: the period is independent of speed, which is what makes the cyclotron work at a fixed frequency at all.

Markscheme notes

One mark per question, no negative marking. (a) Accept any wording that separates the change of direction from the constancy of speed. (b) and (c) are a pair and should be marked together in the sense that a candidate who gives the same answer to both has almost certainly not noticed that the controlled quantity changed; accept $2$ and $1$ respectively, and note that an alpha particle is a helium nucleus with mass number four and charge $+2e$. (d) Accept the expression in any equivalent form; a candidate who writes the frequency instead of the period has inverted the quantity and earns nothing unless the inversion is clearly a slip in labelling. (e) Accept any statement that identifies the electric field as the source of the energy gain; credit should not be withheld from a candidate who adds correctly that the magnetic field is what keeps the particle returning to the gap. A candidate who claims that the magnetic field accelerates the particle has misunderstood the direction of the force and should not be credited even if the rest of the description is accurate.

Why this question is hard

This cluster is built on a single fact and its consequences: the magnetic force is perpendicular to the velocity, so it does no work. Once that is understood, (a) is immediate, (e) follows, and the speed-independence of the period in (d) is a one-line consequence rather than a formula to be memorised. The distractors are all what you get by treating the magnetic field as though it could speed a particle up or slow it down.

The pair (b) and (c) is the heart of the cluster and is deliberately constructed so that the same two particles give different answers depending on which quantity is held fixed. At equal speed the radius scales as $m/q$, giving a factor of two in favour of the alpha particle. At equal kinetic energy the radius scales as $\sqrt{m}/q$, and the square root turns the mass factor of four into a factor of two that exactly cancels the doubled charge, so the radii are equal. A student who has memorised one of these expressions and applies it to both questions gets one right and one wrong, and a student who re-derives the radius from whichever quantity is stated as common gets both right. That is exactly the discrimination the cluster is designed to make, and it is why the two items are placed next to each other with only the controlled quantity changed.
