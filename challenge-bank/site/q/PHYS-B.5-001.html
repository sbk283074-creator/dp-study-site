Two cells in parallel, one of them being charged

Physics HL · PHYS-B.5-001

B.5 Current and circuits: emf and internal resistance, epsilon = I(R + r), combinations of resistors in series and parallel, electrical power P = IV = I^2 R = V^2/RP2 · Section A15 marksdifficulty 4technology: permittedShow thatCalculateDetermineExplain

Question

The diagram shows two cells connected in parallel between the junctions A and B, together with a resistor R of resistance $2.0\ \Omega$ connected between the same two junctions. Cell 1 has emf $\varepsilon_{1}=12.0\ \mathrm{V}$ and internal resistance $r_{1}=0.40\ \Omega$; cell 2 has emf $\varepsilon_{2}=9.0\ \mathrm{V}$ and internal resistance $r_{2}=0.60\ \Omega$. The connecting wires have negligible resistance.

  1. [4 marks](a)

    By applying $\varepsilon=I(R+r)$ to each source branch, and by requiring that the current arriving at junction A equals the current leaving it, show that the potential difference between A and B is $9.64\ \mathrm{V}$.

    (Show that)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    Calculate the current in each of the two source branches, stating the direction of each current, and calculate the current in R.

    (Calculate)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    Show that the total power delivered by cell 1 equals the sum of the power dissipated in the two internal resistances, the power dissipated in R, and the power absorbed by cell 2.

    (Show that)
  4. [3 marks](d)

    Determine the value of $\varepsilon_{2}$ for which no current flows in branch 2, leaving $\varepsilon_{1}$, $r_{1}$ and $R$ unchanged.

    (Determine)

Total: 15 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) Let $V$ be the potential difference between A and B. In branch 1 the current is $I_{1}=\dfrac{\varepsilon_{1}-V}{r_{1}}$ and in branch 2 it is $I_{2}=\dfrac{\varepsilon_{2}-V}{r_{2}}$, while the current in R is $I=\dfrac{V}{R}$. (M1)

Current is conserved at junction A, so $I_{1}+I_{2}=I$: (M1)

$$\frac{12.0-V}{0.40}+\frac{9.0-V}{0.60}=\frac{V}{2.0}$$

Multiplying throughout by $1.2$: $3(12.0-V)+2(9.0-V)=0.6V$. (M1)

So $36.0-3V+18.0-2V=0.6V$, giving $54.0=5.6V$ and $V=9.64\ \mathrm{V}$. (AG)

(b) $I_{1}=\dfrac{12.0-9.643}{0.40}=5.89\ \mathrm{A}$ (M1)(A1), flowing from the positive terminal of cell 1 through junction A and out into the rest of the circuit.

$I_{2}=\dfrac{9.0-9.643}{0.60}=-1.07\ \mathrm{A}$ (M1), so a current of $1.07\ \mathrm{A}$ flows into the positive terminal of cell 2: cell 1 is charging cell 2. (A1)

The current in R is $I=\dfrac{9.643}{2.0}=4.82\ \mathrm{A}$. (A1)

Check: $5.893-1.071=4.821\ \mathrm{A}$, consistent with current conservation.

(c) Power delivered by cell 1: $\varepsilon_{1}I_{1}=12.0\times 5.893=70.7\ \mathrm{W}$. (M1)

Power dissipated in the two internal resistances: $I_{1}^{2}r_{1}=5.893^{2}\times 0.40=13.9\ \mathrm{W}$ and $I_{2}^{2}r_{2}=1.071^{2}\times 0.60=0.689\ \mathrm{W}$. (M1)

Power dissipated in R: $\dfrac{V^{2}}{R}=\dfrac{9.643^{2}}{2.0}=46.5\ \mathrm{W}$. (M1)

Power absorbed by cell 2, which is being charged: $\varepsilon_{2}I_{2}=9.0\times 1.071=9.64\ \mathrm{W}$.

Total $=13.89+0.689+46.49+9.64=70.7\ \mathrm{W}$, which equals the power delivered by cell 1. (A1)

(d) No current in branch 2 requires $V=\varepsilon_{2}$, since branch 2 then has no potential difference across its internal resistance. (M1)

With $I_{2}=0$ the current in branch 1 equals the current in R: $\dfrac{12.0-V}{0.40}=\dfrac{V}{2.0}$, so $2(12.0-V)=0.40V$ and $24.0=2.4V$. (M1)

Hence $V=10.0\ \mathrm{V}$ and $\varepsilon_{2}=10.0\ \mathrm{V}$. (A1)

Markscheme notes

Part (a): the model must include the internal resistances in series with each cell; a candidate who treats the cells as ideal earns at most the second (M1). Accept any consistent choice of sign convention for the current directions, provided it is stated. Part (b): a negative value of $I_{2}$ is the correct outcome and must not be treated as an error; award the (A1) for identifying that cell 2 is being charged. A candidate who reverses the direction convention throughout and reports $I_{2}=+1.07\ \mathrm{A}$ into the cell earns full marks if the direction is clearly stated. Part (c): the four power terms are each worth (M1), with the final (A1) for demonstrating equality. Follow-through applies from (a) and (b) — if the candidate's currents are consistent with their $V$, the arithmetic will still balance and should be credited. A candidate who omits the power absorbed by cell 2 cannot balance the audit and should not be given the (A1). Part (d): accept a solution by inspection that recognises the no-current condition requires the terminal p.d. of cell 2 to equal its emf.

Why this question is hard

The circuit looks symmetrical and is not. Two cells in parallel with different emfs cannot both deliver current: the potential difference settles between the two emfs, so the stronger cell drives current backwards through the weaker one. Students who assume both cells supply current will set up two positive branch currents, reach a value of $V$ above $9.0\ \mathrm{V}$, and produce an inconsistent current balance without noticing. The sign of $I_{2}$ is the whole point of the question, and part (b) asks for the direction explicitly to make sure it is confronted.

The power audit in part (c) is the strongest check available, and it is worth doing whenever a circuit contains more than one source. Cell 2 absorbs energy at the rate $\varepsilon_{2}I_{2}$ in addition to dissipating $I_{2}^{2}r_{2}$ in its own internal resistance. The two terms are different: the first is energy converted into chemical energy in the cell being charged, the second is energy lost as heat inside it. Omitting the first term is the standard error and it leaves about 14% of the power unaccounted for, which is why the question asks for the audit rather than for a single value.

Part (d) inverts the reasoning. Rather than solving for $V$ given the emfs, it asks what emf would make a branch carry no current, and the answer is that the cell's emf must equal the potential difference across it — the condition for a cell neither charging nor discharging. It is a short part but it tests whether the mechanism behind the terminal potential difference has been understood rather than memorised.

Here the emfs are chosen so that the weaker cell is unambiguously charged, and the question adds an explicit four-term power audit and an inverse part asking for the emf at which a branch would carry no current. Verification
Node voltage solved from (12-V)/0.40 + (9-V)/0.60 = V/2.0, giving V = 54/5.6 = 9.6429 V. Branch currents 5.8929 A and -1.0714 A; load current 4.8214 A, and the current balance closes to machine precision. The power audit balances: 70.7143 W delivered equals 13.8903 + 0.6888 + 46.4923 + 9.6429 W.
