5.18 First order differential equations — coupled mixing model

Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL · MATH-P3-003

Topic 5: CalculusAHL 5.18 (first order differential equations; integrating factor)P324 marksdifficulty 5technology: permittedWrite downFindShow thatHence

Question

Two tanks are connected in series. Tank $A$ holds $100$ litres and tank $B$ holds $150$ litres, and both volumes stay constant. Brine of concentration $0.2$ kg per litre enters tank $A$ at $5$ litres per minute. Tank $A$ is kept well stirred and drains into tank $B$ at $5$ litres per minute; tank $B$ is also kept well stirred and drains at $5$ litres per minute. Initially both tanks contain pure water. Let $x(t)$ and $y(t)$ be the masses, in kg, of dissolved salt in tanks $A$ and $B$ after $t$ minutes.

  1. [2 marks](a)

    Write down $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}$ in terms of $x$.

    (Write down)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    Find $x(t)$, given that tank $A$ initially contains pure water.

    (Find)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    Show that $y$ satisfies $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}+\dfrac{y}{30}=1-e^{-t/20}$.

    (Show that)
  4. [5 marks](d)

    Hence find $y(t)$, given that tank $B$ initially contains pure water.

    (Hence)
  5. [3 marks](e)

    Show that the mass of salt in tank $B$ never exceeds $30$ kg.

    (Show that)
  6. [3 marks](f)

    Determine the time at which the rate of increase of salt in tank $B$ is greatest, and the mass of salt in tank $B$ at that instant.

    (Determine)
  7. [3 marks](g)

    Calculate the time, to the nearest minute, at which tank $B$ first contains $25$ kg of salt.

    (Calculate)

Total: 24 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) Salt enters at $5\times0.2=1$ kg per minute and leaves at $5\cdot\frac{x}{100}=\frac{x}{20}$ kg per minute, so

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}=1-\frac{x}{20}.$$

(A1)(A1)

(b) Separating variables, $\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{20-x}=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{20}$, so $-\ln|20-x|=\tfrac{t}{20}+c$ and $20-x=Ke^{-t/20}$. (M1)(A1) Since $x(0)=0$, $K=20$ and

$$x(t)=20\left(1-e^{-t/20}\right).$$

(A1)(A1)

(c) Salt enters $B$ from $A$ at $5\cdot\frac{x}{100}=\frac{x}{20}$ kg per minute and leaves at $5\cdot\frac{y}{150}=\frac{y}{30}$ kg per minute. (M1)(A1) Hence $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}=\dfrac{x}{20}-\dfrac{y}{30}$, and substituting $x=20\left(1-e^{-t/20}\right)$ gives

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}+\frac{y}{30}=1-e^{-t/20}.$$

(M1)(A1)(AG)

(d) The integrating factor is $e^{\int \mathrm{d}t/30}=e^{t/30}$, so

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(y\,e^{t/30}\right)=e^{t/30}-e^{t/30}e^{-t/20}=e^{t/30}-e^{-t/60}.$$

(M1)(A1) Integrating,

$$y\,e^{t/30}=30\,e^{t/30}+60\,e^{-t/60}+C,$$

so $y=30+60\,e^{-t/20}+C e^{-t/30}$. (M1)(A1) Since $y(0)=0$, $0=30+60+C$, giving $C=-90$ and

$$y(t)=30+60\,e^{-t/20}-90\,e^{-t/30}.$$

(A1)

(e) From (d), $30-y=90e^{-t/30}-60e^{-t/20}$. This is positive exactly when $90e^{-t/30}>60e^{-t/20}$, i.e. when $\tfrac32>e^{-t/30+t/20}=e^{t/60}$. (M1)(A1) Since $e^{t/60}\ge1$ for all $t\ge0$ and $\tfrac32>1$, the inequality holds for all $t\ge0$, so $y(t)<30$ for every finite $t$. (R1)

(f) $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}=-3e^{-t/20}+3e^{-t/30}$. Differentiating, $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^2y}{\mathrm{d}t^2}=\tfrac{3}{20}e^{-t/20}-\tfrac{3}{30}e^{-t/30}$, which vanishes when $\dfrac{e^{-t/20}}{20}=\dfrac{e^{-t/30}}{30}$, i.e. $e^{-t/20+t/30}=\tfrac{20}{30}$, so $e^{t/60}=\tfrac32$. (M1)(A1) Hence $t=60\ln\tfrac32=24.3$ minutes. At that instant $e^{-t/20}=\left(\tfrac23\right)^3=\tfrac{8}{27}$ and $e^{-t/30}=\left(\tfrac23\right)^2=\tfrac49$, so

$$y=30+60\cdot\frac{8}{27}-90\cdot\frac49=30+\frac{160}{9}-40=\frac{70}{9}=7.78\text{ kg}.$$

(A1)

(g) Put $u=e^{-t/60}$. Then $e^{-t/20}=u^3$ and $e^{-t/30}=u^2$, so $y=25$ becomes $30+60u^3-90u^2=25$, i.e. $12u^3-18u^2+1=0$. (M1)(A1) Solving numerically in $0<u<1$ gives $u=0.2591$, so $t=-60\ln(0.2591)=81.0$ minutes, i.e. $81$ minutes to the nearest minute. (A1)

Markscheme notes

In (a) award one mark for the inflow $1$ and one for the outflow $x/20$; a candidate who forgets that the outflow concentration is $x/100$ rather than $x$ loses the second. In (b) allow either separation of variables or the integrating factor. In (d) the integration of $e^{-t/60}$ is where marks are lost: $\int e^{-t/60}\,\mathrm{d}t=-60e^{-t/60}$, and the coefficient $60$ must survive into the final answer. In (e) accept any correct rearrangement; the mark is for reducing the claim to a single inequality between exponentials. In (f) the maximum must be justified by a sign change in the second derivative or by an equivalent argument, not simply assumed; allow $24$ minutes to $24.4$ minutes. In (g) allow $80$ to $82$ minutes, and allow a graphical or solver-based route with the substitution shown. Note that (b) and (d) are the two method-heavy parts and together carry nine of the twenty-four marks, so a candidate who sets up (a) and (c) correctly but cannot integrate $e^{-t/60}$ has not lost the item. Candidates using a calculator solver for (g) should still be required to show the substitution or an equivalent equation in a single variable; a bare answer with no working earns nothing, because the mark is for setting up the crossing condition rather than for pressing buttons.

Why this question is hard

The cascade is the point. Tank A is a standard single-compartment mixing problem and most candidates arrive able to do it; the novelty is that tank B's input is not constant but the time-dependent output of A, so the second equation is inhomogeneous with an exponential right-hand side and must be handled with an integrating factor rather than by separation. The physical readings in (e) to (g) are where understanding shows. The salt in B approaches $30$ kg from below and never reaches it, which follows from $e^{t/60}>1$ and surprises students who expect an overshoot; the rate of increase peaks at about $24$ minutes, long before B is anywhere near full, because B can only respond as fast as A delivers; and B takes $81$ minutes to reach $25$ kg even though A is essentially saturated by then. A candidate who finishes and checks that $y'(t)>0$ for all $t>0$ has understood the model. The item also rewards dimensional sense. Both time constants, $20$ minutes and $30$ minutes, are simply the tank volumes divided by the flow rate, and a candidate who notices that can predict the shape of the answer before doing any calculus: the response of B must be governed by the slower of the two constants at large times, which is why $e^{-t/30}$ dominates the tail. That observation also explains why the peak rate in (f) occurs so early, and it gives a candidate a way to catch a sign error in the integrating-factor integration without re-deriving the whole solution.
