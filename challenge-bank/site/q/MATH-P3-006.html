P3 extended problem: generating functions

Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL · MATH-P3-006

Topic 1: Number and algebraAHL 1.11 (partial fractions), SL 1.8 (sum of infinite convergent geometric sequences), AHL 1.10 (counting principles)P316 marksdifficulty 5technology: not allowedShow thatDetermineHence

Question

A strip consists of $n$ cells in a row. It is to be tiled using two kinds of tile: a square tile covering one cell, available in $2$ colours, and a domino covering two adjacent cells, available in $3$ colours. Let $u_n$ be the number of different tilings of a strip of $n$ cells, with $u_0 = 1$ corresponding to the empty strip.

  1. [4 marks](a)

    Show that $u_1 = 2$ and $u_2 = 7$, and show that $u_n = 2u_{n-1} + 3u_{n-2}$ for every integer $n \ge 2$.

    (Show that)
  2. [5 marks](b)

    Let $F(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} u_n x^n$. By multiplying the recurrence by $x^n$ and summing over $n \ge 2$, show that
    $$F(x) = \frac{1}{1 - 2x - 3x^{2}}.$$

    (Show that)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    Express $F(x)$ in partial fractions and hence determine a closed form for $u_n$.

    (Determine)
  4. [3 marks](d)

    Hence find $u_{10}$, and determine the limiting value of $\dfrac{u_{n}}{3^{n}}$ as $n \to \infty$.

    (Hence)

Total: 16 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

By multiplying the recurrence by $x^n$ and summing over $n \ge 2$, show that $$F(x) = \frac{1}{1 - 2x - 3x^{2}}.$$ [5 marks] (Show that) (c) Express $F(x)$ in partial fractions and hence determine a closed form for $u_n$. [4 marks] (Determine) (d) Hence find $u_{10}$, and determine the limiting value of $\dfrac{u_{n}}{3^{n}}$ as $n \to \infty$. [3 marks] (Hence)
Reveal the answer

(a) A strip of one cell can only be covered by a square, in $2$ colours, so $u_1 = 2$. (A1) A strip of two cells is either two squares ($2\times2 = 4$ ways) or one domino ($3$ ways), so $u_2 = 4+3 = 7$. (A1)

For $n\ge2$, look at the first cell. (M1) If it is covered by a square, there are $2$ choices of colour and the remaining $n-1$ cells can be tiled in $u_{n-1}$ ways, giving $2u_{n-1}$. If it is covered by a domino, that domino covers the first two cells, there are $3$ colour choices, and the remaining $n-2$ cells can be tiled in $u_{n-2}$ ways, giving $3u_{n-2}$. (A1) These cases are exhaustive and disjoint, so $u_n = 2u_{n-1} + 3u_{n-2}$. (AG)

(b) Multiply the recurrence by $x^n$ and sum from $n=2$ to $\infty$: (M1)
$$\sum_{n\ge2} u_n x^n = 2\sum_{n\ge2} u_{n-1}x^n + 3\sum_{n\ge2} u_{n-2}x^n$$
The left side is $F(x) - u_0 - u_1 x = F(x) - 1 - 2x$. (A1) The first sum on the right is
$$2x\sum_{n\ge2} u_{n-1}x^{n-1} = 2x\sum_{m\ge1} u_m x^m = 2x\bigl(F(x)-1\bigr)$$
and the second is
$$3x^{2}\sum_{n\ge2} u_{n-2}x^{n-2} = 3x^{2}F(x).$$ (A1)(A1)
Hence
$$F - 1 - 2x = 2x(F-1) + 3x^{2}F$$
$$F\bigl(1 - 2x - 3x^{2}\bigr) = 1 - 2x + 2x = 1$$
$$F(x) = \frac{1}{1-2x-3x^{2}}$$ (A1)(AG)

(c) Factorising, $1-2x-3x^{2} = (1-3x)(1+x)$. (M1) Write
$$\frac{1}{(1-3x)(1+x)} = \frac{A}{1-3x} + \frac{B}{1+x}$$
$$1 = A(1+x) + B(1-3x)$$
Putting $x = -1$: $1 = B(1+3) = 4B$, so $B = \tfrac14$. (A1)
Putting $x = \tfrac13$: $1 = A(1+\tfrac13) = \tfrac43 A$, so $A = \tfrac34$. (A1)
Therefore
$$F(x) = \frac{3/4}{1-3x} + \frac{1/4}{1+x} = \frac34\sum_{n\ge0}(3x)^{n} + \frac14\sum_{n\ge0}(-x)^{n}$$
and comparing coefficients, $u_n = \tfrac34\,3^{n} + \tfrac14(-1)^{n}$, that is (A1)
$$u_n = \frac{3^{n+1} + (-1)^{n}}{4}.$$

(d) $u_{10} = \dfrac{3^{11} + 1}{4} = \dfrac{177147 + 1}{4} = \dfrac{177148}{4} = 44287$. (M1)(A1)
$$\frac{u_n}{3^{n}} = \frac34 + \frac{(-1)^{n}}{4\cdot3^{n}} \to \frac34 \quad \text{as } n\to\infty$$ (M1)(A1)

Markscheme notes

(a) The recurrence mark is for the case analysis on the first cell; a candidate who merely asserts the recurrence without the two cases earns nothing. Award the $u_2$ mark even if obtained from the recurrence rather than directly, provided $u_0$ and $u_1$ were established first. (b) The index-shifting is the technical hurdle: award (M1) for setting up the summed equation and the (A1)s for correctly expressing each of the three sums in terms of $F$. A common error is to write the first right-hand sum as $2xF$ instead of $2x(F-1)$; condone only if corrected later. Convergence need not be discussed at this level, but a candidate who notes that the series is being treated formally should not be penalised. (c) Award (M1) for the factorisation, which is the step students most often fail to see. Accept any valid route to $A$ and $B$, including equating coefficients. The final (A1) is for reading off the coefficient, which requires recognising both geometric series. (d) Accept $44287$; the limit mark is for noticing that the sign-alternating term is divided by $3^n$ and so vanishes, leaving the coefficient of the dominant term.

Why this question is hard

The generating function is one of the few techniques that turns a recurrence into algebra, and this problem is built so that the technique does something a student cannot easily do by other means. The recurrence $u_n = 2u_{n-1}+3u_{n-2}$ is trivial to iterate for ten terms with a calculator, but the question asks for a closed form and for a limit, and iterated numbers give neither.

The move that matters is treating the entire sequence as one object. Multiplying by $x^n$ and summing over $n\ge2$ converts each shifted copy of the sequence into $F$ multiplied by a power of $x$, and the recurrence becomes a linear equation in $F$ that can be solved in three lines. The two places this goes wrong are both index shifts: $\sum_{n\ge2}u_{n-1}x^{n}$ is $x(F-1)$ and not $xF$, because the term $u_0$ is missing from the shifted sum. Getting that wrong produces a rational function with a spurious constant in the numerator and a closed form that fails at $n=0$, which is a useful thing for a student to notice.

The choice of $2$ square colours and $3$ domino colours is deliberate. The denominator factors as $(1-3x)(1+x)$, so one root is positive and one negative, and the closed form contains a term $(-1)^n/4$ that oscillates. That term is small compared with $3^{n+1}/4$ but it is not zero, and it is what makes $u_n$ alternately just above and just below $\tfrac14\,3^{n+1}$. Asking for the limit of $u_n/3^{n}$ as well as the closed form forces a student to confront it: the limit is $\tfrac34$, not $\tfrac34$ plus something, and the oscillating term dies because it is divided by $3^{n}$.
