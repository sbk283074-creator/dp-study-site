Why the conveyor motor needs twice the power you expect

Physics HL · PHYS-A.3-001

A.3 Work, energy and power (work done by a force, conservation of energy, power as the rate of energy transfer, efficiency)P2 · Section B16 marksdifficulty 5technology: permittedShow thatCalculateDetermine

Stimulus

Gravel falls vertically from a stationary hopper onto a horizontal conveyor belt that runs at a constant speed. The gravel arrives with no horizontal velocity and is carried away at the belt's speed. In the steady state the mass of gravel delivered to the belt is $12\ \mathrm{kg}$ each second. Treat the gravel as arriving with no horizontal motion, neglect the mass and any internal friction of the belt mechanism, and consider the horizontal motion only.

Question

The belt moves at a constant speed $v=2.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and the gravel is delivered at a constant rate of $12\ \mathrm{kg\,s^{-1}}$.

  1. [4 marks](a)

    Show that the horizontal force the belt must exert on the gravel is $F=v\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}$, and calculate the value of $F$.

    (Show that)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    Calculate the rate at which kinetic energy is supplied to the gravel as it is brought up to the belt's speed.

    (Calculate)
  3. [5 marks](c)

    Show that the power delivered by the belt's motor is twice the rate at which the gravel gains kinetic energy, identify where the remaining energy goes, and hence calculate the efficiency with which the motor's work becomes kinetic energy of the gravel.

    (Show that)
  4. [3 marks](d)

    The belt speed is increased to $4.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ while the mass flow rate is unchanged. Determine the new motor power and explain why it is not simply double the original power.

    (Determine)

Total: 16 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) In a time interval $\Delta t$ a mass $\Delta m=\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}\Delta t$ of gravel is brought from rest to the belt's speed $v$. Its horizontal momentum therefore changes by $\Delta m\,v$, so the required horizontal force is

$$F=\frac{\Delta(mv)}{\Delta t}=v\frac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}$$ (M1)(AG)

Numerically,

$$F=2.0\times 12=24\ \mathrm{N}$$ (A1)(A1)

(b) Each second a mass of $12\ \mathrm{kg}$ is accelerated from rest to $2.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, so the rate at which kinetic energy is supplied is

$$\frac{\Delta E_{k}}{\Delta t}=\tfrac12\frac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}v^{2}=\tfrac12(12)(2.0)^{2}$$ (M1)(A1)

$$=24\ \mathrm{W}$$ (A1)(A1)

(c) The belt moves at constant speed, so the motor's power equals the force it exerts multiplied by the belt's speed:

$$P=Fv=(24)(2.0)=48\ \mathrm{W}$$ (M1)(A1)

The ratio is

$$\frac{P}{\Delta E_{k}/\Delta t}=\frac{48}{24}=2$$ (A1)

so the motor supplies twice the rate at which the gravel gains kinetic energy. The remaining $24\ \mathrm{W}$ is dissipated as heat where the gravel slides against the belt while it is being accelerated: the friction force acts over the belt's displacement, which is larger than the gravel's displacement, and the difference appears as thermal energy at the sliding contact. (A1) The efficiency is therefore

$$\eta=\frac{24}{48}=0.50=50\%.$$ (A1)

(d) Since $F=v\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}$, doubling $v$ doubles the force to $48\ \mathrm{N}$. The power is $P=Fv$, and both the force and the belt speed have doubled, so

$$P=48\times 4.0=192\ \mathrm{W},$$ (M1)(A1)

which is four times the original $48\ \mathrm{W}$. Equivalently $P=\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}v^{2}\propto v^{2}$, because the belt must accelerate twice as much momentum each second and must do so through twice the distance per second. The mass flow rate has not changed, so the factor of four comes entirely from the doubling of both quantities in $P=Fv$, not from any change in the amount of gravel handled. (A1)

Markscheme notes

(a) The force must come from the rate of change of momentum, not from an energy argument. A candidate who writes $F=\tfrac12\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}v=12\ \mathrm{N}$ has used the kinetic-energy rate and has made the very error the question is designed to expose; award no method mark. Accept $F=v\Delta m/\Delta t$ with $\Delta m/\Delta t=12\ \mathrm{kg\,s^{-1}}$.

(b) Accept $24\ \mathrm{W}$, $24\ \mathrm{J\,s^{-1}}$ or $24\ \mathrm{N\,m\,s^{-1}}$. A candidate who uses $mv^{2}$ instead of $\tfrac12 mv^{2}$ obtains $48\ \mathrm{W}$ and will then report a 100% efficiency; that contradiction should prompt a re-check but earns nothing here.

(c) The essential reasoning is that the belt's force acts through the belt's displacement, not the gravel's. Simply stating 'half the energy is lost as heat' without identifying the sliding contact earns at most one mark. Award the efficiency mark for $0.50$ or $50\%$; a ratio written the other way round ($2$) with a clear statement is acceptable. Follow-through: if (a) or (b) is wrong but internally consistent, the ratio and efficiency marks remain available.

(d) The explanation must mention both that $F\propto v$ and that the belt speed is $v$, so that $P\propto v^{2}$. A candidate who says only 'because power depends on $v$ squared' earns the value mark but not the explanation mark. Accept $192\ \mathrm{W}$ and accept $190\ \mathrm{W}$.

Why this question is hard

This question is built on a result that contradicts the energy audit most students perform automatically. The gravel gains kinetic energy at $24\ \mathrm{W}$, so it is natural to conclude that the motor must supply $24\ \mathrm{W}$. It does not: the motor supplies $48\ \mathrm{W}$, and the other $24\ \mathrm{W}$ is dissipated as heat where the gravel slides against the belt.

The reason is that work is force multiplied by the displacement of the point of application. The belt's friction force acts on the gravel, but the belt itself moves further than the gravel does while the gravel is catching up. In one second the belt advances $2.0\ \mathrm{m}$, whereas the gravel being accelerated moves on average only $1.0\ \mathrm{m}$, so the motor does $24\ \mathrm{N}\times 2.0\ \mathrm{m}=48\ \mathrm{J}$ of work while the gravel's kinetic energy rises by only $24\ \mathrm{J}$. The difference is the frictional heating at the sliding interface.

This is the same mechanism that heats a car's clutch during a standing start, and it is why the efficiency of any device that picks up a fresh charge of mass from rest by dragging it along at a fixed speed is capped at 50%. It is not a machine fault; it is a consequence of the inelastic way the mass is acquired.

Part (d) turns the result into a scaling law. The force is $F=\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}v$, proportional to $v$, and the power is $Fv$, so $P=\dfrac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}v^{2}$. Doubling the belt speed therefore doubles the force and doubles the speed through which that force acts, giving four times the power — a trap for candidates who expect a linear response.

Classic wrong turns: using $\tfrac12 mv^{2}$ to find the force; forgetting that it is the belt's displacement, not the gravel's, that sets the work done by the motor; and assuming that a constant-speed belt does no net work because the gravel's kinetic energy ends up steady. That last error confuses a steady state with a closed system.

Challenge lever
The motor power is not the rate at which the gravel gains kinetic energy but twice that, because the belt's friction force acts through the belt's displacement while the gravel slides and lags behind, so exactly half of the energy supplied is dissipated as heat at the sliding surface and the efficiency is pinned at 50 per cent.

Adaptation
Built from the A.3 bullets on work, power and efficiency, combined with the A.2 idea of force as the rate of change of momentum. The design is original in its arc: the force is obtained from momentum, the kinetic-energy rate is then contrasted with the motor power to expose the factor of two, the missing energy is located at the sliding contact, and the final part converts the result into the scaling law $P\propto v^{2}$. The values $12\ \mathrm{kg\,s^{-1}}$ and $2.0\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ were chosen so that the force, the kinetic-energy rate and the motor power come out as $24\ \mathrm{N}$, $24\ \mathrm{W}$ and $48\ \mathrm{W}$ exactly.
Verification
Force, kinetic-energy rate and motor power recomputed from F = v dm/dt, dEk/dt = 0.5 (dm/dt) v^2 and P = Fv; the energy balance 48 W = 24 W (kinetic) + 24 W (heat) was checked, and the v^2 scaling re-derived at 4.0 m/s.
