The false root invented by multiplying through a vanishing denominator

Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL · MATH-AHL3.9-001

AHL 3.9 (definition of the reciprocal trigonometric ratios sec, csc, cot)P1 · Section B15 marksdifficulty 5technology: not allowedStateShow thatDetermineExplain

Question

For real $x$ where it is defined, let $$f(x)=\csc x-\cot x.$$

  1. [3 marks](a)

    State the values of $x$ in the interval $[0,2\pi)$ at which $f(x)$ is undefined, and write down the period of $f$.

    (State)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    Show that $f(x)=\tan\dfrac{x}{2}$ for every $x$ in the domain of $f$.

    (Show that)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    A student solves $f(x)=1$ by multiplying both sides by $\sin x$. Show that this produces the equation $\sin x+\cos x=1$, and hence find all solutions of that produced equation in $[0,2\pi)$.

    (Show that)
  4. [4 marks](d)

    Determine the number of solutions of $f(x)=k$ in $[0,2\pi)$ for $k>0$, for $k=0$ and for $k<0$, justifying your answers by reference to the graph of $f$.

    (Determine)

Total: 15 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) $f$ is undefined where $\sin x=0$, because $\csc x=\frac{1}{\sin x}$ and $\cot x=\frac{\cos x}{\sin x}$ both require $\sin x\ne0$. (A1) In $[0,2\pi)$ these are $x=0$ and $x=\pi$. (A1) Both reciprocal functions have period $2\pi$, so $f$ has period $2\pi$. (A1)

(b) Putting the two terms over the common denominator, $$\csc x-\cot x=\frac{1}{\sin x}-\frac{\cos x}{\sin x}=\frac{1-\cos x}{\sin x}.$$ (M1) The half-angle identity $\tan\frac{x}{2}=\frac{1-\cos x}{\sin x}$ holds for every $x$ with $\sin x\ne0$. (M1) Hence $f(x)=\tan\frac{x}{2}$ on the whole domain of $f$. (A1) *(AG)*

(c) Multiplying $f(x)=1$ by $\sin x$ gives $1-\cos x=\sin x$, that is $$\sin x+\cos x=1.$$ (M1)(A1) Writing the left side as $\sqrt2\sin\left(x+\frac{\pi}{4}\right)$ gives $\sin\left(x+\frac{\pi}{4}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}$, so $x+\frac{\pi}{4}=\frac{\pi}{4}$ or $\frac{3\pi}{4}$ in the first period. (M1) The solutions in $[0,2\pi)$ are $x=0$ and $x=\frac{\pi}{2}$. (A1) At $x=0$, however, $\sin x=0$, so $f$ is undefined and the multiplication by zero has created a false root; only $x=\frac{\pi}{2}$ solves the original equation. (R1)(A1)

(d) On the domain $f(x)=\tan\frac{x}{2}$ with $\frac{x}{2}\in\left(0,\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\cup\left(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi\right)$. (M1) On $\left(0,\frac{\pi}{2}\right)$ the tangent rises from $0$ to $+\infty$, so each $k>0$ is attained exactly once and no $k<0$ is attained. (A1) On $\left(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi\right)$ it rises from $-\infty$ to $0$, so each $k<0$ is attained exactly once and no $k>0$ is attained. (A1) For $k=0$, $\tan\frac{x}{2}=0$ would require $x=0$ or $x=2\pi$, both excluded, so there are no solutions. (R1) Hence $f(x)=k$ has exactly one solution for every $k\ne0$ and none for $k=0$. (A1)

Markscheme notes

(a) Both (A1) marks are for the two excluded values; accept $0$ and $\pi$, or $n\pi$ with $n=0,1$, within the interval. The third (A1) is for the period $2\pi$; a candidate who says $\pi$ has confused the period of $\tan\frac{x}{2}$ with that of $\tan x$.

(b) The first (M1) is for combining the reciprocals over the single denominator $\sin x$, the second (M1) for the half-angle form. A candidate who uses $\tan\frac{x}{2}=\frac{\sin x}{1+\cos x}$ should be fully credited, since the two forms agree on the domain. The (A1) requires the identity to be asserted on the domain, not at the excluded points.

(c) The (M1)(A1) are for the multiplication and the resulting linear equation, the next (M1) for solving it, and the (A1) for the two roots in the interval. The (R1) is for testing $x=0$ against the domain and naming it extraneous; merely listing $x=0$ earns nothing. The final (A1) requires the reduced solution set.

(d) The (M1) is for rewriting the equation as $\tan\frac{x}{2}=k$ on the two branches. Each branch contributes one (A1); the (R1) is for treating $k=0$ separately, since the tangent vanishes only at the excluded endpoints.

Why this question is hard

The stem is deliberately bare: the difficulty lies not in the algebra but in noticing where the algebra is legal. The reciprocal functions are defined by $\csc x=\frac{1}{\sin x}$ and $\cot x=\frac{\cos x}{\sin x}$, so both fail wherever $\sin x=0$. On $[0,2\pi)$ that is exactly $x=0$ and $x=\pi$, and every later part is governed by those two points.

Part (b) is the structural key. The combination $\csc x-\cot x$ collapses to $\frac{1-\cos x}{\sin x}$, which is precisely the half-angle expression for $\tan\frac{x}{2}$. Two reciprocal functions that look like a hard mixture are secretly a single tangent, and this turns the equation into a monotonic one.

Part (c) is where the trap bites. Multiplying by $\sin x$ clears the denominator and gives the clean equation $\sin x+\cos x=1$, whose solutions in the interval are $x=0$ and $x=\frac{\pi}{2}$. But the multiplication is not reversible at $x=0$, where $\sin x=0$: it multiplies by zero and manufactures a root the original equation never had, because $f$ is not even defined there. Each root must be tested against the domain.

Part (d) uses the reduced form as a graph. The domain splits into two open intervals, and on each the half-angle runs through a half-period of the tangent, so the branches together cover every nonzero real value exactly once and never cover zero. For $k=0$ the algebra suggests $x=0$ and $x=2\pi$, but both are excluded, so there are no solutions. The classic errors are accepting $x=0$ in (c), forgetting the excluded point $x=\pi$, and claiming two solutions for every $k$ by ignoring the split domain. A candidate who trusts the multiplied equation will report two answers where only one is genuine.

Challenge lever
The reciprocal functions are undefined wherever sin x or cos x vanishes, so the natural move of multiplying csc x minus cot x equals 1 through by sin x manufactures the extra equation sin x plus cos x equals 1, whose root x=0 lies where sin x=0; the student must notice that this multiplication is not reversible on the original domain and reject that root.
