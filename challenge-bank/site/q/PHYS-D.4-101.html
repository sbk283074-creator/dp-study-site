D.4 Induction

Physics HL · PHYS-D.4-101

Theme D: FieldsD.4 (Induction: Faraday's law, Lenz's law, motional emf, self-inductance)P1 · Section A5 marksdifficulty 5technology: allowedCalculateDetermine

Question

Five independent multiple-choice questions on electromagnetic induction. Each one stands alone. In every case the magnetic field is uniform over the region stated and the resistance given is the total circuit resistance.

  1. [1 mark](a)

    A rectangular coil of 200 turns and resistance $4.0\ \Omega$ has one side of length $5.0\ \mathrm{cm}$ inside a uniform magnetic field of $0.80\ \mathrm{T}$. The coil is pulled out of the field at a constant speed of $0.20\ \mathrm{m\ s^{-1}}$, with that side remaining perpendicular to the direction of motion. What is the current in the coil while it leaves the field?

    (Calculate)
  2. [1 mark](b)

    A coil of 500 turns and area $0.020\ \mathrm{m^2}$ rotates at a steady frequency of $50\ \mathrm{Hz}$ in a uniform magnetic field of $0.10\ \mathrm{T}$, with its axis of rotation perpendicular to the field. What is the maximum emf induced across the coil?

    (Calculate)
  3. [1 mark](c)

    An inductor of inductance $0.40\ \mathrm{H}$ carries a steady current of $3.0\ \mathrm{A}$. How much energy is stored in the magnetic field of the inductor?

    (Calculate)
  4. [1 mark](d)

    A square loop of side $0.10\ \mathrm{m}$ and resistance $2.0\ \Omega$ is pulled at constant speed completely through a region of uniform magnetic field of $0.50\ \mathrm{T}$ directed perpendicular to the plane of the loop. The region is wide enough that the loop enters and leaves separately. What total charge flows past a point in the loop?

    (Determine)
  5. [1 mark](e)

    A conducting rod of length $0.40\ \mathrm{m}$ rotates at a steady angular speed of $20\ \mathrm{rad\ s^{-1}}$ about one end, sweeping out a plane that is perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field of $0.25\ \mathrm{T}$. What is the emf between the two ends of the rod?

    (Calculate)

Total: 5 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) Only the side of length $L = 0.050\ \mathrm{m}$ that lies across the field boundary changes the flux, so $\varepsilon = NBLv = 200 \times 0.80 \times 0.050 \times 0.20 = 1.6\ \mathrm{V}$, and $I = \varepsilon/R = 1.6/4.0 = 0.40\ \mathrm{A}$. (A1) Option B reports the emf, option C puts the coil area where the side length belongs, and option D omits the turn count.

(b) The flux linkage is $N\Phi = NBA\cos(\omega t)$, so $\varepsilon = NBA\omega\sin(\omega t)$ with $\omega = 2\pi f = 314\ \mathrm{rad\ s^{-1}}$. Hence $\varepsilon_{\max} = 500 \times 0.10 \times 0.020 \times 314 = 314\ \mathrm{V}$. (A1) Option B uses $\pi f$ for $\omega$, option C uses $f$, and option D gives the flux linkage rather than its rate of change.

(c) $E = \tfrac{1}{2}LI^2 = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.40 \times 3.0^2 = 1.80\ \mathrm{J}$. (A1) Option B omits the half, option C uses $LI$, and option D halves $LI$.

(d) The charge is $Q = \int I\,dt = \dfrac{1}{R}\int \varepsilon\,dt = \dfrac{|\Delta\Phi|}{R}$ over each transition. Entry gives $Ba^2/R = 0.50 \times 0.010/2.0 = 2.5\ \mathrm{mC}$ and exit gives the same again, so $Q = 5.0\ \mathrm{mC}$. (A1) This is the item that separates a student who has memorised a formula from one who knows what charge is: the *net* flux change over the whole motion is indeed zero, but the two currents flow at different times and it is their magnitudes, not their signs, that add.

(e) An element at distance $r$ moves at $\omega r$, so $d\varepsilon = B\omega r\,dr$ and $\varepsilon = \int_0^L B\omega r\,dr = \tfrac{1}{2}B\omega L^2 = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.25 \times 20 \times 0.16 = 0.400\ \mathrm{V}$. (A1) Option B uses the tip speed throughout, option C is dimensionally inconsistent, and option D halves twice.

Markscheme notes

One mark per correct option, no negative marking. In (a) allow an answer reached via the rate of cutting of flux lines provided the length used is the one crossing the boundary. In (b) accept 314 V or 3.1 x 10^2 V, and condone a small discrepancy from rounding omega to 314. In (c) accept 1.8 J. In (d) do not credit an argument from the net flux change alone even if it reaches 5.0 mC by some other route, because the reasoning is what is being rewarded; equally, credit 5.0 mC reached by treating the two transitions separately as intended. In (e) accept 0.40 V and allow the integral form or the mean-speed argument. Note that in (d) the speed at which the loop is pulled does not appear in the answer at all, and that is the point of the item: the charge depends only on the change of flux linkage and on the resistance. In (e) credit the integral form and the mean-speed argument equally, but do not credit B L v applied with the tip speed, which is precisely the error option B exists to catch. In (b) the value 314 V depends on using an angular frequency in radians per second; a candidate working in degrees will reach none of the options, which is a useful diagnostic in itself.

Why this question is hard

The unifying idea is that an induced emf comes from a rate of change, never from a flux on its own, and that is what options D in (b), B in (a) and the whole of (d) are built to test. Item (d) is the hardest in the set and is the one that most reliably separates grade 6 from grade 7 candidates: the net change in flux linkage across the complete motion really is zero, and a candidate who has learned 'Q equals delta phi over R' without understanding that Q is a time integral will choose 0 mC with complete confidence. Item (e) rewards the same habit at the level of a single rod: because the speed varies along the rod, the emf has to be built up element by element, and the factor of one half is not a rule to remember but the result of that averaging. Across the cluster, dimensional checks eliminate at least one option in every item, which is the intended escape route for a candidate who runs short of time. Items (a) and (e) are deliberately paired: both ask for an emf from a conductor moving in a magnetic field, and both are answered by B L v only when the whole conductor moves at the same speed, which is true in (a) and false in (e). Deciding which of the two situations applies is the transferable skill the cluster is built around, and it is the same decision that separates (d) from a naive use of the flux formula.
