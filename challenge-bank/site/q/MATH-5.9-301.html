5.9 Two moving objects that are level twice: the second meeting is a repeated root, so the cubic has three roots and the objects meet on two occasions

Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL · MATH-5.9-301

Topic 5: CalculusSL 5.9 (kinematic problems involving displacement s, velocity v, acceleration a and time t) with SL 5.8 (local maximum and minimum points) and SL 2.7 (solution of quadratic equations)P1 · Section B15 marksdifficulty 5technology: not allowedFindShow thatDetermineDetermine

Question

Two objects $A$ and $B$ move along a straight line. The displacement of $A$ from a fixed origin at time $t$ seconds, $0 \le t \le 5$, is
$$s_{A}(t) = t^{3} - 6t^{2} + 9t \quad \text{metres}.$$
Object $B$ moves with constant velocity, so that $s_{B}(t) = \alpha + \beta t$ for constants $\alpha$ and $\beta$. Observations show that $A$ and $B$ are level when $t = 1$, and that they are level again when $t = \tfrac{5}{2}$ but that neither overtakes the other at that instant. The diagram shows both displacement-time graphs.

  1. [3 marks](a)

    Find the velocity of $A$ at time $t$, the two times at which $A$ reverses its direction of motion, and the displacement of $A$ at the first of those times.

    (Find)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    Show that the condition that neither object overtakes the other at $t = \tfrac{5}{2}$ is the condition that $t = \tfrac{5}{2}$ is a repeated root of $s_{A}(t) - s_{B}(t) = 0$, and hence determine $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

    (Show that)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    Determine the number of occasions in $0 \le t \le 5$ on which the two objects are level, and the distance between them when $t = 4$.

    (Determine)
  4. [4 marks](d)

    Determine the greatest distance between the two objects in $0 \le t \le 5$, and the time at which it occurs.

    (Determine)

Reveal the answer

(a) $v_{A}(t) = s_{A}'(t) = 3t^{2} - 12t + 9 = 3(t - 1)(t - 3)$ $(M1)$, so $v_{A}(t) = 0$ at $t = 1$ and $t = 3$; these are the two times at which $A$ reverses $(A1)$. $s_{A}(1) = 1 - 6 + 9 = 4$, so at the first reversal $A$ is $4$ m from the origin $(A1)$.\n(b) Let $D(t) = s_{A}(t) - s_{B}(t)$. Being level at $t = \tfrac{5}{2}$ means $D\left(\tfrac{5}{2}\right) = 0$. If the root were simple, $D$ would change sign there, so $A$ would pass $B$ or $B$ would pass $A$; since neither overtakes the other, $D$ keeps the same sign on both sides, which happens exactly when the root is of even multiplicity $(M1)$ — here a repeated (double) root, so also $D'\left(\tfrac{5}{2}\right) = 0$ $(A1)$. Now
$$D(t) = t^{3} - 6t^{2} + 9t - \alpha - \beta t, \qquad D'(t) = 3t^{2} - 12t + 9 - \beta.$$
The two conditions give $D\left(\tfrac{5}{2}\right) = \tfrac{125}{8} - \tfrac{75}{2} + \tfrac{45}{2} - \alpha - \tfrac{5\beta}{2} = \tfrac{5}{8} - \alpha - \tfrac{5\beta}{2} = 0$ and $D'\left(\tfrac{5}{2}\right) = \tfrac{75}{4} - 30 + 9 - \beta = -\tfrac{9}{4} - \beta = 0$, so $\beta = -\tfrac{9}{4} = -2.25$ $(M1)$, and then $\alpha = \tfrac{5}{8} - \tfrac{5}{2}\left(-\tfrac{9}{4}\right) = \tfrac{5}{8} + \tfrac{45}{8} = \tfrac{25}{4} = 6.25$ $(A1)$. Hence $s_{B}(t) = 6.25 - 2.25t$: $B$ starts $6.25$ m from the origin and moves towards it at $2.25$ m s$^{-1}$.\n(c) With these values,
$$D(t) = t^{3} - 6t^{2} + 11.25t - 6.25 = (t - 1)\left(t - \tfrac{5}{2}\right)^{2} \;(M1)(A1),$$
so the equation $D(t) = 0$ has roots $t = 1$ and $t = \tfrac{5}{2}$ (twice). The objects are therefore level on two occasions in $0 \le t \le 5$, not three: the double root is a single instant at which $B$ draws level with $A$ and immediately falls behind again $(A1)$. When $t = 4$, $s_{A}(4) = 64 - 96 + 36 = 4$ and $s_{B}(4) = 6.25 - 9 = -2.75$, so the distance between them is $4 - (-2.75) = 6.75$ m $(A1)$.\n(d) The separation is $|D(t)|$ with $D(t) = (t - 1)\left(t - \tfrac{5}{2}\right)^2$ $(M1)$. $D'(t) = 3t^{2} - 12t + 11.25 = 3(t - 1.5)(t - 2.5)$, so the only stationary points in the interval are $t = 1.5$ and $t = 2.5$ $(A1)$. Evaluating, $D(0) = -6.25$, $D(1.5) = 0.5$, $D(2.5) = 0$ and $D(5) = 125 - 150 + 56.25 - 6.25 = 25$, so $|D|$ attains its greatest value at the endpoint $t = 5$ $(M1)$, where the separation is $25$ m $(A1)$.

Markscheme notes

(a) (M1) for differentiating, (A1) for $t = 1$ and $t = 3$, (A1) for $4$ m. Accept the factorised velocity $3(t-1)(t-3)$ as the justification for the reversal times.\n(b) (M1) for explaining that 'no overtaking' forces the difference not to change sign, and hence a repeated root, (A1) for the condition $D'(5/2) = 0$ as well as $D(5/2) = 0$, (M1) for solving the two equations, (A1) for $\beta = -2.25$ and $\alpha = 6.25$. A candidate who uses only $D(5/2) = 0$ and the earlier observation $D(1) = 0$ obtains the same two equations by a different route, and must be credited fully for the algebra; but such a candidate earns the first mark only on confirming that $t = 5/2$ is in fact a double root of the difference they have found, since otherwise they have not shown that their motion is consistent with the observation they were given.\n(c) (M1) for forming $D$ and factorising, (A1) for the factorisation, (A1) for the answer two occasions with the reason that the double root is one instant, (A1) for $6.75$ m. Do not credit 'three times' however carefully the cubic is solved; the question asks for occasions, and this is the item's point.\n(d) (M1) for writing the separation as $|D(t)|$, (A1) for $D'(t) = 3(t-1.5)(t-2.5)$, (M1) for checking the endpoints as well as the stationary points, (A1) for $25$ m at $t = 5$. A candidate who maximises $D$ without the modulus and reports $25$ m by luck should be asked for the endpoint check; award the final mark only if $t = 0$ was considered.

Why this question is hard

Meeting problems are root-finding problems, and the habit they build is that the number of meetings is the number of roots. This item breaks that habit with the one case the habit cannot see: a repeated root is a meeting that is not a crossing, so the cubic has three roots and the objects meet twice.\n\nThe condition is doing double duty. 'They are level at t = 5/2 and neither overtakes the other' looks like a qualitative remark attached to a numerical one; in fact it is the second equation the candidate needs, because two unknowns ($\alpha$ and $\beta$) cannot be found from one observation. Turning the words into $D'(5/2) = 0$ is the whole of part (b), and it is the same move that later tells the candidate the root has multiplicity two.\n\nPart (d) closes the item on a different skill: the separation is a modulus, so its maximum over a closed interval need not be at a stationary point of $D$ — and here it is not, because the objects are still drawing apart when the motion stops at $t = 5$. A candidate who differentiates and compares stationary points alone will report $0.5$ m and miss the endpoint entirely. That reversal is the same lesson as part (b) in another form. The interesting instant in a meeting problem is not always a root of something: here one of them is a stationary point of the difference and the other is the end of the motion, and reading such a question as a root-count is precisely the habit the item is written to break.

Challenge lever
The condition that the two objects are level at t = 2.5 and that one does not overtake the other there is a condition on the derivative, not on the value: the difference of the displacements must vanish and must have a stationary point at the same instant, so t = 2.5 is a double root. That is the only way the two observations pin down both of B's unknowns, and it is also the reason the cubic equation for the meeting times has three roots but describes two meetings — the number of occasions is not the degree.
