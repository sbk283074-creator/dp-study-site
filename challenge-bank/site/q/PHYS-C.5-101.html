C.5 Doppler effect

Physics HL · PHYS-C.5-101

Question

Five independent multiple-choice questions on the Doppler effect. Each stands alone. Take the speed of sound in air as $340\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and the speed of ultrasound in soft tissue as $1540\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ where those are needed. For electromagnetic radiation use $c = 3.00 \times 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$.

  1. [1 mark](a)

    A siren emitting $500\ \mathrm{Hz}$ moves at $30\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ towards a stationary observer. What frequency does the observer hear?

    (Calculate)
  2. [1 mark](b)

    The same $500\ \mathrm{Hz}$ siren is now stationary and the observer moves towards it at $30\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. What frequency does the observer hear?

    (Calculate)
  3. [1 mark](c)

    A hydrogen line with laboratory wavelength $656\ \mathrm{nm}$ is measured in the spectrum of a galaxy at $689\ \mathrm{nm}$. What is the motion of the galaxy along the line of sight?

    (Determine)
  4. [1 mark](d)

    An ultrasound probe emits $5.0\ \mathrm{MHz}$ at $60^{\circ}$ to blood flowing at $0.40\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. What is the magnitude of the Doppler shift in the reflected signal?

    (Calculate)
  5. [1 mark](e)

    An aircraft flies at $510\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ in air where the speed of sound is $340\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. What is the half-angle of the Mach cone it produces?

    (Determine)

Take the speed of sound in air as $340\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and the speed of ultrasound in soft tissue as $1540\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ where those are needed. For electromagnetic radiation use $c = 3.00 \times 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. (a) A siren emitting $500\ \mathrm{Hz}$ moves at $30\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ towards a stationary observer. What frequency does the observer hear? [1 mark] (Calculate) (b) The same $500\ \mathrm{Hz}$ siren is now stationary and the observer moves towards it at $30\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. What frequency does the observer hear? [1 mark] (Calculate) (c) A hydrogen line with laboratory wavelength $656\ \mathrm{nm}$ is measured in the spectrum of a galaxy at $689\ \mathrm{nm}$. What is the motion of the galaxy along the line of sight? [1 mark] (Determine) (d) An ultrasound probe emits $5.0\ \mathrm{MHz}$ at $60^{\circ}$ to blood flowing at $0.40\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. What is the magnitude of the Doppler shift in the reflected signal? [1 mark] (Calculate) (e) An aircraft flies at $510\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ in air where the speed of sound is $340\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. What is the half-angle of the Mach cone it produces? [1 mark] (Determine)
Reveal the answer

(a) A. A moving source compresses the wavefronts ahead of it, so the wavelength in the medium is shortened while the observer samples at the usual rate: $f' = fv/(v-u_{s}) = 500 \times 340/310 = 548\ \mathrm{Hz}$. (A1) Note that the denominator contains the source speed, because it is the spacing of the crests that has changed.

(b) A. A moving observer meets unchanged wavefronts more often, so it is the numerator that changes: $f' = f(v+u_{o})/v = 500 \times 370/340 = 544\ \mathrm{Hz}$. (A1) The two answers differ by about $4\ \mathrm{Hz}$ for identical speeds, and that difference is the whole point of asking both questions: the situations are physically different even though the relative speed is the same.

(c) A. The wavelength is longer than the laboratory value, so the galaxy is receding. The redshift is $z = (689-656)/656 = 0.0503$, and since $z \ll 1$ the recession speed is $zc = 1.51 \times 10^{7}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. (A1) Direction first, magnitude second: the sign of the wavelength change carries the physics and is worth stating before any arithmetic.

(d) A. Reflection imposes the shift twice and only the along-beam component contributes, so $\Delta f = 2fv\cos 60^{\circ}/c = 2 \times 5.0\times10^{6} \times 0.40 \times 0.500/1540 = 1.30\ \mathrm{kHz}$. (A1) The factor of two is not a correction: the blood is first a moving observer and then a moving source.

(e) A. The wavelets emitted along the flight path superpose on a cone whose half-angle satisfies $\sin\theta = v_{\text{sound}}/v_{\text{source}} = 340/510 = 0.667$, giving $\theta = 41.8^{\circ}$. (A1) A faster aircraft makes the cone narrower, which is the check that rules out the cosine.

Markscheme notes

One mark per question, no negative marking. (a) Accept $548$ to $549\ \mathrm{Hz}$. (b) Accept $544\ \mathrm{Hz}$; if a candidate gives $548\ \mathrm{Hz}$ here, note that they have used the source formula and consider whether part (a) should also be reviewed, since the same error produces both. (c) Accept $1.5 \times 10^{7}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and require the direction; a magnitude alone is not the answer to 'what is the motion'. Accept the relativistic expression if offered, but note that it changes the third figure only. (d) Accept $1.29$ to $1.30\ \mathrm{kHz}$, and accept $1299\ \mathrm{Hz}$. Award nothing if the factor of two is missing, even if the cosine is handled correctly, because the reflection is the part of the physics that is specific to this technique. (e) Accept $42^{\circ}$ or the exact statement $\sin\theta = 2/3$; a candidate who writes $\sin\theta = 3/2$ and then notes the impossibility has understood the geometry better than one who produces $41.8^{\circ}$ by rote, and should be credited.

Why this question is hard

The first two items are deliberately the same question twice with the motion moved from one side to the other, and the intended lesson is that the answers are not the same. When the source moves, the wavelength in the medium is changed and the observer's sampling rate is not; when the observer moves, the reverse holds. The two expressions are $fv/(v-u_{s})$ and $f(v+u_{o})/v$, they give $548\ \mathrm{Hz}$ and $544\ \mathrm{Hz}$ for identical speeds, and a candidate who memorised a single 'Doppler formula' has no way to choose between them. That is why both numbers appear in both items: recognising the correct one is the test, not computing it.

Item (d) is the one where two independent factors have to be applied and neither is optional. The factor of two arises because the moving blood is first a moving observer of the incoming beam and then a moving source of the reflected one, so the shift is imposed twice. The cosine arises because only the component of velocity along the beam produces a shift at all, which is why clinical probes are held at a known angle and why $60^{\circ}$ rather than a rounder angle was chosen for the item. A candidate who gets one factor and not the other arrives at $2.6\ \mathrm{kHz}$ or $0.65\ \mathrm{kHz}$, both of which are offered, and both of which are wrong for a reason the candidate can be told.

Item (e) closes the cluster by asking for a decision about trigonometry rather than a substitution. Drawing the wavelets shows that the sine, not the cosine or tangent, relates the two speeds to the cone angle, and the physical check is the trend: a faster aircraft should give a narrower cone. A candidate who checks the trend catches the cosine error even if the diagram did not.
