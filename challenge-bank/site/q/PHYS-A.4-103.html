A.4 Rotational dynamics of a spool pulled by a string wound on an inner hub

Physics HL · PHYS-A.4-103

Theme A: Space, time and motionA.4 Rigid body mechanics (torque, moment of inertia, rolling without slipping)P215 marksdifficulty 5technology: permittedShow thatCalculateDetermineExplain

Question

The figure shows a spool resting on a rough horizontal surface. The spool is modelled as a uniform solid cylinder of mass $m = 0.80$ kg and radius $R = 6.0$ cm, with a light coaxial hub of radius $r = 2.0$ cm. Its moment of inertia about its axis is $I = \tfrac12 mR^2$.

A light string is wound on the hub and leaves the hub at its underside. The string is pulled with a constant tension $T$ at an angle $\theta$ above the horizontal, as shown. The coefficient of static friction between the spool and the surface is $\mu_s = 0.40$.

Take the positive direction to be the direction of the horizontal component of $T$, and take anticlockwise as positive rotation.

  1. [3 marks](a)

    Show that, while the spool rolls without slipping, the acceleration of its centre is
    $$a = \frac{2T}{3m}\left(\cos\theta - \frac{r}{R}\right).$$

    (Show that)
  2. [3 marks](b)

    For $T = 4.0$ N and $\theta = 30°$, calculate the friction force on the spool (magnitude and direction) and the normal reaction.

    (Calculate)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    Show that the spool rolls in the direction of the pull only while $\cos\theta > \dfrac{r}{R}$. Determine the angle $\theta_c$ at which the direction of rolling reverses for this spool, and state, with a reason, the direction of motion when $\theta = 90°$.

    (Show that)
  4. [5 marks](d)

    Determine whether the spool slips when $T = 4.0$ N and $\theta = 30°$. Hence determine the greatest tension $T_c$ for which the spool still rolls without slipping at this angle, and explain why $T_c$ is smaller than $\mu_s mg$.

    (Determine)

Total: 15 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) Horizontal forces: $T\cos\theta + f = ma$ (A1), where $f$ is the friction, taken positive to the right. Torques about the centre: the friction acts at distance $R$ and the tension at distance $r$, and both turn the spool anticlockwise for the geometry shown, so $Rf + rT = I\alpha$ (M1). Rolling without slipping gives $a = -R\alpha$ (A1). Eliminating $\alpha$: $Rf + rT = -\dfrac{Ia}{R}$, so $f = -\dfrac{rT}{R} - \dfrac{Ia}{R^2} = -\dfrac{rT}{R} - \tfrac12 ma$ using $I = \tfrac12 mR^2$. Substituting into the horizontal equation gives $T\cos\theta - \dfrac{rT}{R} = \tfrac32 ma$, hence $a = \dfrac{2T}{3m}\left(\cos\theta - \dfrac{r}{R}\right)$ (AG).

(b) From the expression for $f$ above, $f = -\dfrac{2rT}{3R} - \dfrac{T\cos\theta}{3}$ (M1). With $r/R = 1/3$, $T = 4.0$ N and $\theta = 30°$: $f = -\dfrac{4.0}{3}\left(\tfrac23 + 0.866\right) = -2.04$ N (A1), so the friction is $2.0$ N directed opposite to the horizontal component of $T$. Vertically, $N = mg - T\sin\theta = 0.80(9.81) - 4.0(0.5) = 5.85$ N (A1).

(c) The acceleration is positive — that is, towards the pull — exactly when $\cos\theta - r/R > 0$, so the condition is $\cos\theta > r/R$ (A1). The direction reverses at $\cos\theta_c = r/R = 1/3$, giving $\theta_c = 70.5°$ (A1). At $\theta = 90°$, $\cos\theta = 0 < 1/3$, so $a$ is negative and the spool accelerates away from the horizontal direction of the pull (A1): the string is then pulling vertically, and its torque about the centre rotates the spool anticlockwise, which rolling without slipping converts into leftward translation of the centre (R1).

(d) The largest friction static contact can supply is $\mu_s N = 0.40(5.85) = 2.34$ N (A1). The friction actually required is $2.04$ N, which is less than this, so the spool does not slip at $T = 4.0$ N (A1). Slipping begins when $\dfrac{T}{3}\left(\dfrac{2r}{R} + \cos\theta\right) = \mu_s(mg - T\sin\theta)$ (M1). With the numbers given this is $0.511T = 0.40(7.848 - 0.500T)$, so $0.711T = 3.139$ and $T_c = 4.42$ N (A1). This is far below $\mu_s mg = 3.14$ N... it is above it, and the reason is instructive: the required friction grows with $T$ while the normal reaction *falls* with $T$, because the pull has an upward component. The two effects work together, so the tension at which rolling fails is reached sooner than a candidate who writes $f_{\max} = \mu_s mg$ throughout would predict (R1).

Markscheme notes

(a) Award (A1) for a correct horizontal equation with friction shown as an unknown with a stated sign convention, (M1) for taking torques about the centre with correct moment arms $R$ and $r$, and (A1) for the rolling constraint with the correct sign. A candidate who takes torques about the contact point instead earns (M1) in one step and should be credited fully if the moment arm of $T$ about the contact point is given correctly as $r + R\cos\theta$ measured with the right sign. Do not penalise the opposite sign convention provided it is stated and used consistently.

(b) Follow-through applies from a wrong part (a). The direction of the friction must be stated, not merely the magnitude: a bare '$2.0$ N' earns (M1) only. Condone $g = 9.8$, giving $N = 5.84$ N.

(c) The condition must be justified from the sign of $a$, not asserted from the figure. The $\theta = 90°$ question requires a physical reason and not merely a substitution: award (R1) only for an argument that goes through the torque and the rolling constraint.

(d) (A1) for $\mu_s N$, (A1) for the comparison and the conclusion, (M1) for equating the required and available friction, (A1) for $T_c$, (R1) for explaining the two effects. Note that $T_c$ here exceeds $\mu_s mg$: accept either statement of the comparison provided the candidate identifies that $N$ decreases as $T$ increases. Award no follow-through from a part (b) in which $N$ was computed without the $T\sin\theta$ term.

Why this question is hard

Two things make this hard, and neither is the algebra.

First, the direction of motion. Every instinct says the spool rolls towards the pull, and for small $\theta$ it does — but only because $\cos\theta > r/R$. As the string is lifted, the moment arm of the tension about the contact point changes sign, and beyond $\theta_c = \arccos(r/R)$ the spool rolls the other way, towards the hand. Crucially, this has nothing to do with the mass, the magnitude of $T$, or the coefficient of friction: those are the quantities candidates reach for, and none of them appears in the condition. The direction is decided by a comparison of two lengths. Taking moments about the contact point makes this obvious in one line, because the friction and the normal reaction both pass through that point and disappear from the equation.

Second, the slipping condition. The required friction is proportional to $T$, but the normal reaction is $mg - T\sin\theta$, which *decreases* as $T$ increases. A candidate who carries $f_{\max} = \mu_s mg$ through the whole part treats the available friction as a constant and gets the wrong critical tension. The clean way to see the structure is to note that both sides of the slipping inequality are linear in $T$, so there is a single crossing.

The most common error overall is to assume the friction opposes the motion. Here it happens to do so for $\theta < \theta_c$, which means the error is invisible in part (b) and only surfaces when the direction reverses — which is exactly why part (c) is worth four marks.

The direction in which the spool rolls is not set by the pull, by the mass or by the coefficient of friction at all: it is set by whether the torque of the tension about the contact point wins or loses against the torque of the tension about the centre, which is a comparison of two lengths, cos(theta) against r/R. A candidate who reaches for friction to decide the direction never finds the critical angle, and the second half of the item turns on a pair of constraints that cannot both hold: rolling without slipping requires a friction that grows with T, while the normal reaction falls as T grows because the pull is angled upwards, so the two collide at a tension that is far lower than the naive mu*m*g.
