The indefinite integral that needs a logarithm and a cosine

Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL · MATH-AHL5.10-001

SL 5.10 (indefinite integral of x^n, n in Q, sin x, cos x, 1/x, e^x)P2 · Section B16 marksdifficulty 5technology: permittedFind

Question

Find the indefinite integral $\int\left(3x^{2}-\frac{2}{x}+6\mathrm{e}^{x}-4\sin x\right)\mathrm{d}x$.

  1. [4 marks](a)

    Find the general antiderivative, including the constant of integration.

    (Find)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    Find the derivative of your answer and confirm that it returns the original integrand.

    (Find)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    Find the value of the antiderivative at $x=1$, taking the constant of integration to be zero.

    (Find)
  4. [4 marks](d)

    Find the constant of integration if the antiderivative takes the value $7$ when $x=1$.

    (Find)

Total: 16 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) Integrate the expression term by term, because the integral of a sum is the sum of the separate integrals. The power term gives $\int 3x^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=x^{3}$ (M1). The reciprocal term gives $\int -\frac{2}{x}\,\mathrm{d}x=-2\ln|x|$ (A1), where the absolute value is required so the logarithm is defined. The exponential term gives $\int 6\mathrm{e}^{x}\,\mathrm{d}x=6\mathrm{e}^{x}$ and the sine term gives $\int -4\sin x\,\mathrm{d}x=4\cos x$ (A1), since the derivative of $\cos x$ is $-\sin x$. No term affects another, so the four rules can be applied in any order, and adding the constant of integration gives the general antiderivative $F(x)=x^{3}-2\ln|x|+6\mathrm{e}^{x}+4\cos x+C$ (A1)(AG). Each standard rule is applied to one term independently, so the final result is the sum of four antiderivatives.

(b) Differentiate the answer to check it. We have $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(x^{3})=3x^{2}$ (M1), $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(-2\ln|x|)=-\frac{2}{x}$ (A1), $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(6\mathrm{e}^{x})=6\mathrm{e}^{x}$ and $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}(4\cos x)=-4\sin x$ (A1). Adding these derivatives gives exactly the original integrand $3x^{2}-\frac{2}{x}+6\mathrm{e}^{x}-4\sin x$ (A1)(AG). This reverse check is valuable, because it catches any single sign slip instantly, and the derivative undoes the integration term by term, which is why the check confirms the antiderivative is correct.

(c) With $C=0$ and $x=1$, substitute to obtain $F(1)=1-2\ln 1+6\mathrm{e}^{1}+4\cos 1$ (M1). Because $\ln 1=0$, this simplifies to $1+6\mathrm{e}+4\cos 1$, which evaluates to $19.470900$ to six decimal places (A1). The logarithm contributes nothing at $x=1$, so the value is dominated by the exponential and the cosine term, and those two terms together account for almost the whole magnitude of the result.

(d) The condition $F(1)=7$ gives $19.470900+C=7$ (M1), so the constant is $C=7-19.470900=-12.470900$ (A1)(AG). This constant shifts the whole antiderivative downwards so that its graph passes through the point $(1,7)$. Choosing the constant in this way fixes a unique member of the family of antiderivatives, and the negative sign is required because the value without the constant already exceeds $7$, which is why $C$ must be negative.

Markscheme notes

(a) Award (M1) for integrating the power term to $x^{3}$ and (A1) for $-2\ln|x|$; the absolute value bars are required for full credit. Award (A1) for $6\mathrm{e}^{x}$ and (A1) for $4\cos x$, noting the sign change that comes from integrating $-4\sin x$. The final (A1)(AG) is for the combined antiderivative $x^{3}-2\ln|x|+6\mathrm{e}^{x}+4\cos x+C$. A candidate who writes $\ln x$ without bars earns the term mark only, not full credit.

(b) Award (M1) for the derivative of the power term and (A1) for $-2/x$ from the logarithm. Award (A1) for $6\mathrm{e}^{x}$ and (A1) for $-4\sin x$, with (A1)(AG) for recovery of the original integrand. The confirmation must show the derivative equals the given integrand term by term.

(c) Award (M1) for substituting $x=1$ with $C=0$ and (A1) for the numerical value $19.470900$ to six decimal places. Accept $19.47$ if rounded, but the exact form $1+6\mathrm{e}+4\cos 1$ is preferred in the markscheme.

(d) Award (M1) for forming $19.470900+C=7$ and (A1)(AG) for $C=-12.470900$. Follow through from an incorrect part (c) value is allowed provided the subtraction is consistent with it. A candidate who misses the negative sign earns no credit for the final answer.

Why this question is hard

The item is built on the indefinite integral of a sum that deliberately spans four different standard forms: a power, a reciprocal, an exponential and a sine. The antiderivative must combine a logarithm with a cosine, because integrating $-2/x$ gives $-2\ln|x|$ and integrating $-4\sin x$ gives $+4\cos x$, since the derivative of $\cos x$ is $-\sin x$. Combining a logarithm and a cosine in one antiderivative is unusual enough that candidates often second-guess the sign of the cosine term. The trap is to mishandle one of these signs, most often writing $-4\cos x$ instead of $+4\cos x$, or dropping the absolute value on the logarithm. Part (b) is the built-in self-check: differentiating the answer must return the integrand term by term, and it exposes any sign error at once. The evaluation at $x=1$ is chosen because $\ln 1$ vanishes, isolating the exponential and cosine contributions, so the value is $1+6\mathrm{e}+4\cos 1\approx 19.470900$. Part (d) then pins the curve by requiring $F(1)=7$, so $C=7-19.470900=-12.470900$, and requiring an initial condition turns the family of antiderivatives into a single curve. The classic error is to integrate $-4\sin x$ as $-4\cos x$, which fails the part (b) check, or to forget that $\ln 1=0$ and keep an erroneous constant term. The numbers were chosen so the part (c) value is not an integer, forcing a calculator evaluation, while the part (d) constant is a clean subtraction from it. The interplay of the four forms is the point of the exercise, and the logarithm-plus-cosine combination is the feature most likely to be mis-remembered under pressure. A calculator is genuinely needed only at the final numerical evaluation, since every algebraic step is exact.

Built from the SL 5.10 bullet on the indefinite integral of x^n, sin x, cos x, 1/x and e^x. The lever is the integrand 3x^2 - 2/x + 6e^x - 4 sin x, which mixes four standard forms so that the antiderivative must contain both a logarithm and a cosine; those coefficients were chosen so that the power integrates to x^3, the reciprocal to minus 2 ln|x|, the exponential to 6e^x and the sine to plus 4 cos x, and so that evaluating at x = 1 gives 1 + 6e + 4 cos 1 is approximately 19.470900, from which the initial condition F(1) = 7 yields the clean constant minus 12.470900. The item is not a reskin because the four-form mix forces a sign-sensitive logarithm-plus-cosine antiderivative and then an initial-value correction, which a single-form integral never requires.
