Related rates on a sliding ladder, solved by implicit differentiation

Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL · MATH-AHL5.14-001

Topic 5: CalculusAHL 5.14 (implicit differentiation; related rates of change; optimisation, including an end-point optimum)P2 · Section B16 marksdifficulty 4technology: permittedShow thatFindFind

Question

A $13$ m ladder rests against a vertical wall. The foot is pulled horizontally away from the wall at a constant rate of $0.5$ m s$^{-1}$. Let $x$ be the distance of the foot from the wall and $y$ the height of the top above the ground, so that $x^{2}+y^{2}=169$.

  1. [5 marks](a)

    By differentiating $x^{2}+y^{2}=169$ with respect to time $t$, show that $\frac{dy}{dt}=-\frac{x}{y}\frac{dx}{dt}$.

    (Show that)
  2. [5 marks](b)

    Find the rate at which the top of the ladder is descending when the foot is $5$ m from the wall.

    (Find)
  3. [6 marks](c)

    Find the distance of the foot from the wall when the top of the ladder is descending at exactly $1$ m s$^{-1}$.

    (Find)

Total: 16 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

Differentiate $x^{2}+y^{2}=169$ implicitly with respect to $t$: $2x\frac{dx}{dt}+2y\frac{dy}{dt}=0$, so $\frac{dy}{dt}=-\frac{x}{y}\frac{dx}{dt}$ (M1)(A1)(A1)(AG). This is the related-rates equation that links the two speeds through the geometry of the ladder.

When $x=5$ we have $y=\sqrt{169-25}=\sqrt{144}=12$ (M1). With $\frac{dx}{dt}=0.5$, $\frac{dy}{dt}=-\frac{5}{12}(0.5)=-\frac{5}{24}$ m s$^{-1}$ (A1)(AG), so the top of the ladder descends at $\frac{5}{24}$ m s$^{-1}$, approximately $0.208$ m s$^{-1}$.

Set $\frac{dy}{dt}=-1$. Then $-\frac{x}{y}(0.5)=-1$, giving $\frac{x}{y}=2$ and hence $x=2y$ (M1)(A1). Substitute into the constraint: $(2y)^{2}+y^{2}=5y^{2}=169$, so $y^{2}=\frac{169}{5}$ and $y=\sqrt{\frac{169}{5}}$. Therefore $x=2\sqrt{\frac{169}{5}}\approx 11.63$ m (M1)(A1)(AG). As a check, with $x=2\sqrt{169/5}$ we have $x^{2}+y^{2}=4\cdot\frac{169}{5}+\frac{169}{5}=\frac{5\cdot 169}{5}=169$, confirming the constraint. The foot lies between $0$ and $13$, so the configuration is physically valid. The sign convention is that a negative rate of change denotes descent of the top, which is why the requested answer is reported as a positive speed of descent. Markscheme notes

(a) Award (M1) for differentiating the constraint implicitly with respect to $t$, (A1) for $2x\,dx/dt+2y\,dy/dt=0$, (A1) for isolating $dy/dt$, and (A1)(AG) for the stated formula $-(x/y)\,dx/dt$. A candidate who differentiates with respect to $x$ instead of $t$ has not treated $x$ and $y$ as functions of time and forfeits the first two marks.

(b) Award (M1) for finding $y=12$ at $x=5$ from the constraint, (A1) for substituting both values into the related-rates equation, and (A1)(AG) for $-5/24$. The sign is part of the answer: a positive value describing the top as rising does not earn the final mark. Accept $-0.208\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ or the exact fraction.

(c) Award (M1) for setting $dy/dt=-1$ and forming the equation, (A1) for deducing $x=2y$, (A1) for solving $5y^{2}=169$, and (A1)(AG) for $x=2\sqrt{169/5}$. Award (M1) for a verification that the values satisfy the original constraint $x^{2}+y^{2}=169$. Note the foot must lie between $0$ and $13$; $11.63$ does, so accept it. A candidate who stops at $x=2y$ without substituting into the constraint earns only the (M1)(A1), not the final (A1)(AG) for the numerical distance. The numerical answer 11.63 m follows by rounding the exact form $2\sqrt{169/5}$. Watch for the common slip of writing $y=2x$ rather than $x=2y$, which is an inverted ratio: it produces $x=\sqrt{169/5}=5.81$, the height rather than the distance from the wall, and is follow-through only as far as the substitution mark.

Why this question is hard

The ladder, wall and ground form a right triangle whose hypotenuse is fixed at $13$, so $x$ and $y$ are not independent: $x^{2}+y^{2}=169$. The chain rule applied to time turns this algebraic constraint into a differential one linking the two rates, which is the essence of related-rates problems. Part (b) is the standard forward direction: given the position, find the rate. Part (c) reverses it, asking for the position that produces a given rate, which most candidates only practise forwards and so often mishandle the algebra that leads to $x=2y$. The negative sign throughout means the top moves down exactly as the foot moves out, and the magnitude of the rate blows up as $x\to 13$ (where $y\to 0$), a nice reminder that the formula breaks exactly when the ladder is flat against the wall. The implicit-differentiation step is the lever: without differentiating the constraint with respect to time there is no way to connect the two speeds, and substituting the known rate too early loses the general relationship needed for part (c). Keeping the symbolic relation until the end is what makes part (c) tractable rather than a guessing game, and it is the habit the question is meant to build.
