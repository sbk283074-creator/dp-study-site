Two solutions in one period, ten in five

Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL · MATH-AHL3.5-001

SL 3.5 (definition of cos theta and sin theta in terms of the unit circle) and SL 3.6 (the Pythagorean identity cos^2 theta + sin^2 theta = 1) and SL 3.7 (the circular functions sin x, cos x and tan x: their domains, ranges and periodicity) and SL 3.8 (solving trigonometric equations in a finite interval)P1 · Section B15 marksdifficulty 4technology: not allowedFindShow thatDetermineSolve

Question

The angle $\theta$ satisfies $\sin\theta=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\cos\theta<0$. All angles in this question are measured in radians.

  1. [3 marks](a)

    Find the exact values of $\cos\theta$ and of $\tan\theta$, giving each as a simplified fraction or surd.

    (Find)
  2. [3 marks](b)

    Show that $(\sin\theta+\cos\theta)^{2}+(\sin\theta-\cos\theta)^{2}=2$ for every value of the angle $\theta$.

    (Show that)
  3. [5 marks](c)

    Determine the exact value of $\sin\theta\cos\theta$, and hence find the exact value of $\sin^{4}\theta+\cos^{4}\theta$.

    (Determine)
  4. [4 marks](d)

    Solve $\sin x=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ for $x$ in the interval $[0,2\pi)$, and hence state the number of solutions in $[0,10\pi)$.

    (Solve)

Total: 15 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) Since $\sin\theta=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ is negative and $\cos\theta<0$, the angle $\theta$ lies in the third quadrant (M1). The Pythagorean identity gives $\cos^{2}\theta=1-\sin^{2}\theta=1-\frac{3}{4}=\frac{1}{4}$, so $\cos\theta=\pm\frac{1}{2}$ (A1); the third-quadrant condition selects $\cos\theta=-\frac{1}{2}$. Therefore $\tan\theta=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}=\frac{-\sqrt{3}/2}{-1/2}=\sqrt{3}$ (A1).

(b) Expanding, $(\sin\theta+\cos\theta)^{2}=\sin^{2}\theta+2\sin\theta\cos\theta+\cos^{2}\theta$ and $(\sin\theta-\cos\theta)^{2}=\sin^{2}\theta-2\sin\theta\cos\theta+\cos^{2}\theta$ (M1). Adding the two expressions, the cross terms $+2\sin\theta\cos\theta$ and $-2\sin\theta\cos\theta$ cancel (A1), leaving $2\sin^{2}\theta+2\cos^{2}\theta=2(\sin^{2}\theta+\cos^{2}\theta)$ (A1) $=2$ (AG). The identity holds for every angle because it never uses the particular values from part (a); the cross terms cancel in exactly the same way in every quadrant.

(c) From part (a), $\sin\theta\cos\theta=\left(-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$ (M1)(A1). For the fourth powers, write $\sin^{4}\theta+\cos^{4}\theta=(\sin^{2}\theta+\cos^{2}\theta)^{2}-2\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\theta$ (M1). The first bracket equals $1$, and $\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\theta=\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\right)^{2}=\frac{3}{16}$ (A1), so the expression is $1-2\cdot\frac{3}{16}=1-\frac{3}{8}=\frac{5}{8}$ (A1). This identity is the standard companion of the Pythagorean relation.

(d) In the interval $[0,2\pi)$ the equation $\sin x=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ has one solution in the third quadrant and one in the fourth (M1). The reference angle is $\frac{\pi}{3}$, so the solutions are $x=\pi+\frac{\pi}{3}=\frac{4\pi}{3}$ (A1) and $x=2\pi-\frac{\pi}{3}=\frac{5\pi}{3}$ (A1). Since $\sin x$ has period $2\pi$, the interval $[0,10\pi)$ contains $\frac{10\pi}{2\pi}=5$ complete periods (M1), and each contributes two solutions, giving $2\times 5=10$ solutions in total (A1).

Markscheme notes

(a) The (M1) is for locating the angle in the third quadrant from the two sign conditions. The (A1) is for $\cos^{2}\theta=\frac{1}{4}$ and the second (A1) for selecting the negative root; a candidate who gives $+\frac{1}{2}$ forfeits the second mark.

(b) The marks are for structure. The (M1) is for expanding at least one square correctly; the (A1) is for the cancellation of the cross terms; the final (A1) requires the explicit use of $\sin^{2}\theta+\cos^{2}\theta=1$. A candidate who substitutes the numerical values from part (a) instead of proving the identity earns no marks here.

(c) The (M1)(A1) pair is for $\sin\theta\cos\theta=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$; accept a direct product from part (a). The second (M1) is for the identity $\sin^{4}\theta+\cos^{4}\theta=1-2\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\theta$, and the final (A1) is for $\frac{5}{8}$. Follow through from an incorrect product is allowed.

(d) Award (M1) for identifying the two quadrants and (A1) for each exact solution. The final marks require the count of five periods and the product $2\times 5=10$; a candidate who lists the ten solutions explicitly earns full credit. Do not accept $x=\frac{\pi}{3}$ or $x=\frac{2\pi}{3}$, which solve the equation with the positive value $+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

Why this question is hard

The item links four syllabus bullets around a single angle. The angle is fixed by two sign conditions, $\sin\theta<0$ and $\cos\theta<0$, which together place it in the third quadrant and determine $\cos\theta$ and $\tan\theta$ without any ambiguity. Parts (b) and (c) then test whether the candidate can move between the squared identities and the values themselves, rather than treating the angle as a black box.

Part (b) is a proof that should be done symbolically. Substituting the numbers from part (a) is tempting because they are available, but it does not establish the identity for every $\theta$ and earns nothing. The cross terms $+2\sin\theta\cos\theta$ and $-2\sin\theta\cos\theta$ are the whole content of the proof, and recognising that they cancel is the point.

Part (c) is where the two threads meet. The product $\sin\theta\cos\theta=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$ comes from the values in part (a), and the identity $\sin^{4}\theta+\cos^{4}\theta=(\sin^{2}\theta+\cos^{2}\theta)^{2}-2\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\theta$ converts the fourth powers into that product. Candidates who try to compute $\sin^{4}\theta$ and $\cos^{4}\theta$ separately make heavy work of a simple substitution.

Part (d) moves to the general solution and asks for a count over five periods. The two solutions in $[0,2\pi)$ are $\frac{4\pi}{3}$ and $\frac{5\pi}{3}$, and periodicity multiplies them by five. The common error is to use the positive value $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and obtain $\frac{\pi}{3}$ and $\frac{2\pi}{3}$, which satisfy the wrong equation. The value $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ was chosen so that the reference angle is $\frac{\pi}{3}$ and every solution is an exact multiple of $\frac{\pi}{3}$. A candidate who counts the periods carefully will see that the count depends only on the number of complete periods in the interval, not on the particular value of the sine.

Adaptation
Built from the SL 3.5 to 3.8 bullets on the unit circle, the Pythagorean identity, the circular functions and solving trigonometric equations in a finite interval. The lever is a pair of sign conditions, sin theta < 0 and cos theta < 0, that selects one of the two angles with sin theta = -sqrt 3 / 2 and therefore makes cos theta = -1/2 and tan theta = sqrt 3 unique; the value sqrt 3 / 2 was chosen so that sin^4 theta + cos^4 theta collapses to 5/8 and so that the solutions in [0, 2 pi) are the exact multiples 4 pi / 3 and 5 pi / 3, giving ten solutions over five periods.
Verification
Every value was recomputed in Python: sin, cos and tan at 4 pi / 3 and 5 pi / 3, the identities (sin + cos)^2 + (sin - cos)^2 = 2 and sin^4 + cos^4 = 5/8, the product sqrt 3 / 4 and the period count 10 pi / (2 pi) = 5.
