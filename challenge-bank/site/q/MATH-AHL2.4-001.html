The zeros and the poles swap places

Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL · MATH-AHL2.4-001

SL 2.4 (determining key features of graphs) and SL 2.8 (the reciprocal function f(x) = 1/x)P2 · Section B16 marksdifficulty 5technology: permittedDetermineFindExplainSolve

Question

The function $f$ is defined by $f(x) = \frac{2x + 3}{x - 1}$.

  1. [4 marks](a)

    Determine the equations of the asymptotes and the coordinates of the intercepts of the graph of $y = f(x)$.

    (Determine)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    Find an expression for $\frac{1}{f(x)}$ in the form $\frac{x+a}{bx+c}$, and state the equations of its asymptotes and the coordinates of its intercepts.

    (Find)
  3. [3 marks](c)

    Explain how the asymptotes and intercepts of $y = \frac{1}{f(x)}$ relate to those of $y = f(x)$.

    (Explain)
  4. [5 marks](d)

    Solve the inequality $f(x) \ge \frac{1}{f(x)}$, giving your answer in interval notation.

    (Solve)

Total: 16 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) A vertical asymptote occurs where the denominator vanishes, so $x - 1 = 0$ gives $x = 1$. (A1) The function is undefined there, so $x = 1$ is excluded from the domain. Dividing, $\frac{2x+3}{x-1} = 2 + \frac{5}{x-1}$, so as $x \to \pm\infty$ the graph approaches $y = 2$; this is the horizontal asymptote. (A1) The $y$-intercept is $f(0) = 3/(-1) = -3$, giving $(0, -3)$. (A1) The $x$-intercept solves $2x + 3 = 0$, so $x = -3/2$, giving $(-3/2, 0)$. (A1)

(b) Taking the reciprocal, $\frac{1}{f(x)} = \frac{x-1}{2x+3}$. (M1) This has the form $\frac{x+a}{bx+c}$ with $a = -1$, $b = 2$, $c = 3$. (A1) Its vertical asymptote is $x = -3/2$, where the denominator vanishes, and its horizontal asymptote is $y = 1/2$, the ratio of the leading coefficients. (A1) The $y$-intercept is $(0, -1/3)$ and the $x$-intercept is $(1, 0)$. (A1)

(c) The $x$-intercept of $f$ at $x = -3/2$ becomes the vertical asymptote of $1/f$, and the vertical asymptote of $f$ at $x = 1$ becomes the $x$-intercept of $1/f$; in other words, the zeros and the poles swap roles. (R1) The horizontal asymptote changes from $y = 2$ to its reciprocal $y = 1/2$. (R1) The $y$-intercept changes from $-3$ to its reciprocal $-1/3$, since $1/f(0) = 1/(-3)$. (R1)

(d) The inequality is $\frac{2x+3}{x-1} \ge \frac{x-1}{2x+3}$. (M1) Subtracting the right-hand side and combining over the common denominator $(x-1)(2x+3)$ gives $\frac{(2x+3)^2 - (x-1)^2}{(x-1)(2x+3)} \ge 0$. (M1) The numerator factorises as $\left[(2x+3) - (x-1)\right]\left[(2x+3) + (x-1)\right] = (x+4)(3x+2)$. (A1) So the inequality is $\frac{(x+4)(3x+2)}{(x-1)(2x+3)} \ge 0$. (A1) The critical values are $-4$, $-3/2$, $-2/3$ and $1$, and a sign table gives the solution $(-\infty, -4] \cup (-3/2, -2/3] \cup (1, \infty)$; the endpoints $-4$ and $-2/3$ are included because the numerator vanishes there, while $-3/2$ and $1$ are excluded because the expression is undefined there. (A1)

Markscheme notes

Part (a) awards (A1) for the vertical asymptote $x = 1$, (A1) for the horizontal asymptote $y = 2$, and (A1) for each intercept, giving $(0, -3)$ and $(-3/2, 0)$. Writing the function as $2 + 5/(x-1)$ is the cleanest route to the horizontal asymptote and should be credited. In part (b) the (M1) is for taking the reciprocal, and the (A1) marks are for the form $(x-1)/(2x+3)$, the two asymptotes $x = -3/2$ and $y = 1/2$, and the two intercepts $(0, -1/3)$ and $(1, 0)$; a candidate who inverts only the numerator or only the denominator earns nothing. Part (c) awards three (R1) marks, one for the interchange of zeros and poles, one for the horizontal asymptote becoming the reciprocal $1/2$, and one for the $y$-intercept becoming $-1/3$. In part (d) the (M1) marks are for writing the inequality with a common denominator and for expanding $(2x+3)^2 - (x-1)^2$, and the (A1) is for the factorisation $(x+4)(3x+2)$; the final two (A1) marks are for the correct critical values and the interval solution. Endpoint inclusion is essential: $-4$ and $-2/3$ are included because the numerator vanishes, while $-3/2$ and $1$ are excluded because the expression is undefined. The two excluded endpoints must be written with open brackets, since the expression is not defined there.

Why this question is hard

Reciprocating a function rearranges its graph in a very specific way, and the item is designed so that this rearrangement is the object of study rather than a by-product. In part (a) the rational function is read for its asymptotes and intercepts; rewriting it as $2 + 5/(x-1)$ makes the horizontal asymptote immediate and shows that the graph is a shifted hyperbola.

Part (b) asks for $1/f$ explicitly, and the key observation is that the reciprocal of a rational function is obtained by swapping numerator and denominator. The consequence is a complete exchange of roles: the zero of $f$ at $x = -3/2$ becomes the pole of $1/f$, and the pole of $f$ at $x = 1$ becomes the zero of $1/f$. The horizontal asymptote changes to the reciprocal of the original one, and the $y$-intercept changes to the reciprocal of the original intercept. Part (c) asks the candidate to state this pattern, which is where the structural understanding is assessed.

Part (d) turns the comparison $f(x) \ge 1/f(x)$ into a rational inequality. Collecting terms over the common denominator $(x-1)(2x+3)$ produces a difference of two squares, which factorises into a product of two linear factors over a product of two linear factors. The four critical values split the line into five intervals, and a sign table decides each one. The subtlety is endpoint behaviour: where the numerator vanishes the inequality is satisfied, but where the denominator vanishes it is undefined, so two endpoints are included and two are excluded. The comparison also shows why the horizontal asymptotes are reciprocals rather than equal: the behaviour at large $x$ of $1/f$ is the reciprocal of the behaviour of $f$, so $y = 2$ becomes $y = 1/2$, while the two finite critical values simply exchange their roles as roots and poles.
