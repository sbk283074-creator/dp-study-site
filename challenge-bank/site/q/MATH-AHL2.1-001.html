A family of lines with one member the gradient formula cannot reach

Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL · MATH-AHL2.1-001

SL 2.1 (different forms of the equation of a straight line) and SL 2.4 (determining key features of graphs)P1 · Section B15 marksdifficulty 4technology: not allowedShow thatFind

Question

For each real number $k$, the line $\ell_k$ has equation $(k + 2)x + (k - 1)y = 2k + 7$.

  1. [4 marks](a)

    Show that every line $\ell_k$ passes through a single fixed point $P$, and state the coordinates of $P$.

    (Show that)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    Find the value of $k$ for which $\ell_k$ is perpendicular to the line joining $P$ to the origin, giving your answer in the form $ax + by = c$ with integers $a$, $b$, $c$.

    (Find)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    Find the value of $k$ for which $\ell_k$ is parallel to the $y$-axis, and explain why the expression $-(k+2)/(k-1)$ for the gradient cannot be used to find it.

    (Find)
  4. [3 marks](d)

    The line $\ell_k$ meets the coordinate axes at $A$ and $B$; show that the area of triangle $OAB$ is $\frac{(2k+7)^2}{2|(k+2)(k-1)|}$, and hence find the value of $k$ with $-2 < k < 1$ for which this area is exactly $6$.

    (Find)

Total: 15 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) Substituting $x = 3$ and $y = -1$ into the left-hand side gives $(k+2)(3) + (k-1)(-1) = 3k + 6 - k + 1 = 2k + 7$. (M1) This equals the right-hand side for every value of $k$, so $(3, -1)$ lies on every line of the family. (A1) Hence the fixed point is $P = (3, -1)$. (A1)(R1)

(b) The gradient of $PO$ is $(-1 - 0)/(3 - 0) = -1/3$. (M1) A line perpendicular to $PO$ therefore has gradient $3$. (A1) The gradient of $\ell_k$ is $-(k+2)/(k-1)$, so $-(k+2)/(k-1) = 3$. (M1) This gives $-k - 2 = 3k - 3$, so $k = 1/4$. (A1) Substituting, $\ell_{1/4}$ is $(9/4)x - (3/4)y = 15/2$, which simplifies to $3x - y = 10$. (AG)

(c) When $k = 1$ the equation becomes $3x + 0y = 9$, that is $x = 3$, a vertical line parallel to the $y$-axis. (A1) The expression $-(k+2)/(k-1)$ was obtained by dividing by the coefficient of $y$, namely $k - 1$. (R1) At $k = 1$ that coefficient is zero, so the division is not defined. (R1) The formula therefore does not apply, and the vertical line must be read directly from the equation. (A1)

(d) The $x$-intercept is $A = ((2k+7)/(k+2), 0)$ and the $y$-intercept is $B = (0, (2k+7)/(k-1))$. (M1) The area of triangle $OAB$ is $\frac{1}{2}\left|\frac{2k+7}{k+2}\right|\left|\frac{2k+7}{k-1}\right| = \frac{(2k+7)^2}{2|(k+2)(k-1)|}$. (A1) Setting this equal to $6$ and using that $(k+2)(k-1) < 0$ on $-2 < k < 1$ gives $(2k+7)^2 = -12(k+2)(k-1)$. (M1) Expanding, $4k^2 + 28k + 49 = -12k^2 - 12k + 24$, so $16k^2 + 40k + 25 = 0$. (A1) This is $(4k+5)^2 = 0$, so $k = -5/4$. (A1) Then $\ell_{-5/4}$ is $x - 3y = 6$, with intercepts $6$ and $-2$, and the area is $\frac{1}{2}(6)(2) = 6$. (R1)

Markscheme notes

Part (a) awards (M1) for substituting $(3, -1)$ and (A1) for verifying that the identity holds for all $k$, with a second (A1) for stating $P = (3, -1)$; the (R1) recognises the alternative grouping $k(x+y-2) + (2x-y-7) = 0$. A candidate who finds $P$ by solving two particular members earns the same credit. In part (b) the (M1) is for the gradient $-1/3$ of $PO$ and the (A1) for the perpendicular gradient $3$; the second (M1) is for solving $-(k+2)/(k-1) = 3$ and the (A1) for $k = 1/4$, with the (AG) confirming $3x - y = 10$. In part (c) the (A1) is for identifying $k = 1$ and the vertical line $x = 3$, while the two (R1) marks are for the observation that the formula divides by $k - 1$ and that this coefficient is zero at $k = 1$; the final (A1) states the conclusion. Part (d) awards (M1) for the two intercepts and (A1) for the area expression. The (M1) is for using $(k+2)(k-1) < 0$ on the given interval to remove the modulus with a sign change, and the (A1) marks are for the quadratic $16k^2 + 40k + 25 = 0$ and for the double root $k = -5/4$; the (R1) is for the numerical check that the area is $6$.

Why this question is hard

The family of lines is controlled by one parameter, and the whole item is organised around the single value $k = 1$ at which the usual description of the line breaks down. Part (a) is a fixed-point argument: because the coefficients of $k$ can be collected, every member passes through the point where $x + y - 2 = 0$ and $2x - y - 7 = 0$ meet, and that point is $P = (3, -1)$. Recognising the family as a pencil through a fixed point is the organising idea for the rest of the question.

Parts (b) and (c) sit on either side of the degeneracy. For part (b) the gradient formula $-(k+2)/(k-1)$ is valid, because $k = 1/4$ keeps the coefficient of $y$ away from zero, and the perpendicularity condition reduces to a linear equation. Part (c) asks for the one member that the formula cannot see: at $k = 1$ the coefficient of $y$ is zero, the line is vertical, and the division that produced the gradient is undefined. The candidate has to abandon the formula and read the vertical line directly from the equation.

Part (d) adds the area of the triangle cut off by the axes. The modulus is essential, but on the interval $-2 < k < 1$ the product $(k+2)(k-1)$ is negative, so removing the modulus reverses a sign and produces the perfect square $(4k+5)^2 = 0$. The double root is a feature, not an accident: the area function is tangent to the level $6$ at that parameter value, so the condition has exactly one solution in the interval. The fixed point found in part (a) is what ties the three later parts together, because every geometric statement about $\ell_k$ can be translated into an equation involving $k$ and that common point.
