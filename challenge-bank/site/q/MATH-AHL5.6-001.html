The product of a power and a logarithm whose stationary point is irrational

Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL · MATH-AHL5.6-001

SL 5.6 (derivative of x^n, n in Q; derivative of sin x, cos x, e^x and ln x)P2 · Section B16 marksdifficulty 5technology: permittedShow thatFindDetermineFind

Question

Consider the function $f(x)=x^{4}\ln x$ for $x>0$.

  1. [4 marks](a)

    By using the product rule on the two factors, show that $f'(x)=x^{3}(4\ln x+1)$.

    (Show that)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    Find the values of $f'(1)$ and $f'(e)$.

    (Find)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    Determine the exact x-coordinate of the stationary point of $f$.

    (Determine)
  4. [4 marks](d)

    Find the value of $f$ at this stationary point.

    (Find)

Total: 16 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) By the product rule, $f'(x)=4x^{3}\ln x+x^{4}\cdot\frac{1}{x}=4x^{3}\ln x+x^{3}=x^{3}(4\ln x+1)$ (M1)(A1)(A1)(AG). The derivative of $\ln x$ is $\frac{1}{x}$, which cancels one factor of $x$ in $x^{4}$ and leaves the $x^{3}$ term (R1). The factorised derivative is therefore confirmed as a product of $x^{3}$ and a logarithmic bracket. This structure is what makes the stationary point easy to locate once the factorisation is seen.

(b) At $x=1$, we have $\ln 1=0$, so $f'(1)=1^{3}(4\cdot0+1)=1$ (M1)(A1). At $x=e$, we have $\ln e=1$, so $f'(e)=e^{3}(4\cdot1+1)=5e^{3}$ (A1). The two gradient values are therefore $1$ and $5e^{3}$ (R1). The logarithm evaluates to $0$ at $1$ and to $1$ at $e$, which is exactly why these two inputs give such clean results.

(c) A stationary point satisfies $f'(x)=0$, so $x^{3}(4\ln x+1)=0$ (M1). Since the domain is $x>0$, the factor $x^{3}$ is never zero, leaving $4\ln x+1=0$, hence $\ln x=-\frac{1}{4}$ and $x=e^{-1/4}$ (A1)(A1). The coordinate is irrational because it is a non-integer power of $e$ (R1). The exact $x$-coordinate is therefore $e^{-1/4}$, a value that cannot be found by simple inspection.

(d) Substituting $x=e^{-1/4}$ gives $f(e^{-1/4})=(e^{-1/4})^{4}\ln(e^{-1/4})=e^{-1}\cdot(-\frac{1}{4})=-\frac{1}{4e}$ (M1)(A1), so the value is $-\frac{1}{4e}$ (A1). The power and the logarithm combine to produce a single negative fraction (R1). The value is therefore $-\frac{1}{4e}$, which is approximately $-0.09197$ and confirms the stationary point lies below the axis.

Markscheme notes

(a) Award (M1) for applying the product rule to $x^{4}$ and $\ln x$, (A1) for $4x^{3}\ln x+x^{3}$, and (A1)(AG) for the factorised $x^{3}(4\ln x+1)$. The (R1) is for explaining that the $1/x$ from $\ln x$ cancels one power of $x$. A candidate who writes $x^{4}(1/x)$ as $x^{3}$ earns the simplification mark. The logarithm must remain inside the bracket for the stationary point to be found.

(b) The (M1) is for substituting $x=1$, (A1) for $f'(1)=1$, (A1) for $f'(e)=5e^{3}$, and (R1) for the logarithm values at $1$ and $e$. Accept $5e^{3}$ in exact form or as a decimal approximation.

(c) Award (M1) for setting the derivative to zero, (A1) for $4\ln x+1=0$, (A1) for $x=e^{-1/4}$, and (R1) for noting the coordinate is irrational. Follow through from an incorrect derivative is allowed.

(d) Award (M1) for substituting $x=e^{-1/4}$, (A1) for $e^{-1}(-1/4)$, (A1) for $-1/(4e)$, and (R1) for the simplified negative fraction. A candidate who gives only the decimal forfeits the exact-value mark. The exact form $-1/(4e)$ is required at HL.

Why this question is hard

This item combines the product rule with the derivative of $\ln x$ and then forces the candidate to solve for a stationary point that is not a nice rational number. The function $x^{4}\ln x$ differentiates, by the product rule, to $x^{3}(4\ln x+1)$, where the $1/x$ from $\ln x$ cancels one power of $x$. The intended route is to factor the derivative, evaluate it at $x=1$ and $x=e$ to get $1$ and $5e^{3}$, then set the derivative to zero; because $x^{3}$ is never zero for $x>0$, the stationary point comes from $4\ln x+1=0$, giving the irrational $x=e^{-1/4}$. The final value is $e^{-1}$ times $-1/4$, a negative fraction. The classic wrong turn is to mis-apply the product rule, writing $4x^{3}\ln x+4x^{3}$ or forgetting the $1/x$ factor, which destroys the factorisation. Candidates also try to factor out $x^{4}$ or to solve $x^{3}=0$ and claim a stationary point at $x=0$, ignoring the domain $x>0$. Another error is to evaluate $\ln(e^{-1/4})$ incorrectly, or to stop at $\ln x=-1/4$ without exponentiating to recover $x$. The numbers were chosen so that $x=1$ and $x=e$ give clean values and the stationary coordinate is a pure power of $e$, isolating the logarithm-solving skill as the genuine difficulty. The irrational answer is the feature that separates this item from a routine product-rule exercise, and the requirement to express the final value exactly as $-1/(4e)$ rather than as a rounded decimal is the final test of precision.
