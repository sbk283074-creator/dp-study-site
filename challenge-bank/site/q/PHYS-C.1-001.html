The coupling spring that the period ignores

Physics HL · PHYS-C.1-001

C.1 Simple harmonic motion (defining equation a = -omega^2 x, period of a mass-spring oscillator, energy in SHM) and C.2 Wave model (travelling waves, the wave equation v = f lambda, phase difference and the propagation delay)P2 · Section B16 marksdifficulty 5technology: permittedShow thatDetermineCalculateExplain

Question

Two identical gliders, each of mass $m=0.020\ \mathrm{kg}$, rest on a frictionless horizontal air track. Each glider is held to a fixed post by a spring of stiffness $k=2.0\times10^{4}\ \mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$, and the two gliders are joined to each other by a coupling spring of stiffness $k_{c}=3.0\times10^{4}\ \mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$ (see the figure). The left glider carries a light piston that closes the near end of a long straight tube; the far end of the tube is lined with an absorber, so a travelling wave rather than a standing wave is set up, and when the glider moves along the track the piston drives a longitudinal compression wave along the tube. The speed of sound in the air in the tube is $340\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$. Friction, the mass of the springs and the mass of the piston are all negligible.

  1. [4 marks](a)

    The two gliders are displaced by equal amounts in the same direction and released from rest. Show that the coupling spring is neither stretched nor compressed at any instant, deduce that the period of this motion is $T_{s}=2\pi\sqrt{m/k}$, and calculate the value of $T_{s}$.

    (Show that)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    The gliders are now displaced by equal amounts in opposite directions and released from rest. Determine the period $T_{a}$ of this motion, showing how the coupling spring now contributes, and calculate the ratio $T_{s}/T_{a}$.

    (Determine)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    In the antisymmetric motion the piston drives the wave in the tube at the frequency $f=1/T_{a}$. Calculate the frequency, the wavelength of the wave in the tube, and the phase difference between two points on the tube axis that are $0.534\ \mathrm{m}$ apart. State which of the two points is ahead in phase.

    (Calculate)
  4. [4 marks](d)

    Show that $T_{s}/T_{a}=\sqrt{1+2k_{c}/k}$, and hence determine the value of this ratio in the limits $k_{c}\to0$ and $k_{c}\to\infty$. Explain what each limit means physically for the motion of the two gliders.

    (Determine)

Total: 16 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) In this motion the two gliders have equal displacements in the same direction at every instant, so $x_{1}=x_{2}$ and their separation never changes; the coupling spring therefore remains at its natural length and exerts no force on either glider. (M1) Each glider is then acted on only by its own wall spring, so Newton's second law for either glider gives

$$m\ddot{x}=-kx\quad\Rightarrow\quad\ddot{x}=-\frac{k}{m}x.$$ (A1)

Comparing with the defining equation of simple harmonic motion, $a=-\omega^{2}x$, shows that $\omega_{s}=\sqrt{k/m}$ and hence

$$T_{s}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.020}{2.0\times10^{4}}}=2\pi\sqrt{1.0\times10^{-6}}$$ (A1)

$$T_{s}=6.28\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{s}=6.28\ \mathrm{ms}.$$ (A1)

The coupling stiffness $k_{c}$ does not appear: in this mode the coupling spring is inert.

(b) In the antisymmetric motion $x_{1}=-x_{2}$, so the separation of the gliders changes by $2x_{1}$ and the coupling spring is stretched or compressed by twice the displacement of either glider. (M1) Its force on a glider is therefore $-k_{c}(2x_{1})$, and combining this with the wall spring gives

$$m\ddot{x}_{1}=-kx_{1}-2k_{c}x_{1}=-(k+2k_{c})x_{1},$$ (A1)

so $\omega_{a}=\sqrt{(k+2k_{c})/m}$ and

$$T_{a}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k+2k_{c}}}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.020}{2.0\times10^{4}+2(3.0\times10^{4})}}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.020}{8.0\times10^{4}}}=2\pi\sqrt{2.5\times10^{-7}}$$

$$T_{a}=3.14\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{s}=3.14\ \mathrm{ms}.$$ (A1)

The ratio of the two periods is

$$\frac{T_{s}}{T_{a}}=\sqrt{\frac{k+2k_{c}}{k}}=\sqrt{\frac{8.0\times10^{4}}{2.0\times10^{4}}}=2.00.$$ (A1)

The antisymmetric mode is therefore the faster of the two.

(c) The piston is driven by the glider that moves in the antisymmetric mode, so the frequency of the wave in the tube is fixed by $T_{a}$:

$$f=\frac{1}{T_{a}}=\frac{1}{3.1416\times10^{-3}}=318\ \mathrm{Hz}.$$ (M1)(A1)

Using the wave equation $v=f\lambda$,

$$\lambda=\frac{v}{f}=\frac{340}{318.3}=1.07\ \mathrm{m}.$$ (A1)

The phase difference between two points separated by $\Delta x=0.534\ \mathrm{m}$ is

$$\Delta\varphi=\frac{2\pi\,\Delta x}{\lambda}=\frac{2\pi(0.534)}{1.068}=3.14\ \mathrm{rad}=\pi.$$ (A1)

The two points are in antiphase. The point nearer the piston is ahead in phase: the disturbance reaches it first, and the farther point lags by the propagation delay $\Delta x/v=1.57\ \mathrm{ms}$, which is exactly half a period.

(d) Dividing the two periods,

$$\frac{T_{s}}{T_{a}}=\sqrt{\frac{k+2k_{c}}{k}}=\sqrt{1+\frac{2k_{c}}{k}}.$$ (M1)

As $k_{c}\to0$ the ratio tends to $1$, so $T_{a}\to T_{s}$. The coupling spring has gone, the two gliders are independent identical oscillators, and every possible motion is a superposition of one single frequency; the distinction between the symmetric and the antisymmetric mode therefore disappears. (A1)

As $k_{c}\to\infty$ the ratio diverges and $T_{a}\to0$. An infinitely stiff coupling spring holds the gliders a fixed distance apart, so they can no longer move in opposite directions and the antisymmetric mode is suppressed. (A1)

For any finite $k_{c}$ the ratio is greater than one, so the antisymmetric mode is always the faster of the two, while the symmetric period is completely independent of $k_{c}$. (A1)

Markscheme notes

(a) The (M1) requires the explicit statement that equal displacements in the same direction leave the coupling spring at its natural length. A candidate who writes $T=2\pi\sqrt{m/(k+k_{c})}$ or $T=2\pi\sqrt{m/(k+2k_{c})}$ has assumed that the coupling spring acts and forfeits the mark; this is the error the question is built to expose. The two (A1)s are for obtaining $\omega_{s}=\sqrt{k/m}$ by comparison with $a=-\omega^{2}x$, and for the numerical period. Accept $6.3\ \mathrm{ms}$, $2\pi\ \mathrm{ms}$ or $6.28\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{s}$.

(b) The (M1) is for recognising that the coupling spring's extension is twice the individual displacement; a candidate who uses $k_{c}x_{1}$ instead of $2k_{c}x_{1}$ obtains $\omega_{a}=\sqrt{(k+k_{c})/m}=1.6\times10^{3}\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$ and a ratio of $1.58$, which forfeits the last two marks but should still be followed through in (c) and (d). Accept the ratio as $2$, $2.0$ or $2.00$.

(c) Follow-through from (b) is expected: if the candidate's $T_{a}$ differs, the frequency, the wavelength and the phase difference must be recomputed consistently with it and are then credited. The phase-difference mark requires the substitution $\Delta\varphi=2\pi\Delta x/\lambda$, not a quoted $\pi$; accept $3.1\ \mathrm{rad}$, $180^{\circ}$ or $\pi$. The statement of which point leads carries no separate mark, but a candidate who says the farther point leads has reversed the propagation direction and should be corrected.

(d) The (M1) requires the ratio to be written as $\sqrt{1+2k_{c}/k}$; a candidate who states the two limits without deriving the ratio earns at most one mark. Both limits and both physical explanations are needed. Do not accept 'the ratio becomes zero' for $k_{c}\to0$; it becomes one.

Why this question is hard

The question is built on a single point of physics: in the symmetric normal mode of this system the coupling spring does not change length, so it cannot affect the period. A candidate who has met a coupled oscillator reaches for the coupling stiffness, and the natural guess is that the period must involve $k+k_{c}$ or $k+2k_{c}$. In the symmetric mode it involves neither; only the wall springs and the mass appear, and the period is exactly that of a single glider on its own wall spring.

The reason is that the two gliders move as one. Their separation is constant, the coupling spring stays at its natural length, and it pulls on neither glider. The wall springs are the only sources of restoring force, and each acts on its own glider, so the two oscillators are independent and identical. The coupling spring matters only in the antisymmetric mode, where the gliders move towards and away from each other: the separation changes by twice the displacement of one glider, so the spring's extension is doubled and its effective stiffness is $2k_{c}$. This is the familiar result that a spring pulled from both ends behaves as though it were twice as stiff.

Part (c) forces the SHM result to do work. The wave frequency is not chosen by the candidate; it is fixed by $T_{a}$, and the wavelength then follows from $v=f\lambda$. The phase difference is the wave-model consequence: two points half a wavelength apart are in antiphase, and the point nearer the source leads. A candidate who uses the naive $f=\sqrt{k/m}/(2\pi)$, the symmetric frequency, will obtain a wavelength twice too large and a phase difference half the correct value; the question separates those two frequencies deliberately.

Part (d) turns the result into a limit. Writing the ratio as $\sqrt{1+2k_{c}/k}$ makes both limits visible. As the coupling stiffness vanishes the two frequencies coalesce, which is the signature of a degeneracy: without coupling there is no way to tell the 'symmetric' mode from the 'antisymmetric' one, because the two gliders are simply independent. As the coupling stiffness becomes very large the gliders are locked a fixed distance apart and the antisymmetric motion is frozen out.

Classic wrong turns: assuming that the coupling spring contributes to the symmetric mode; treating the two gliders as a single mass $2m$ on a single spring, which happens to give the right answer here only because of the factor of two; and using the symmetric frequency rather than the antisymmetric one to drive the wave.

Metadata

Challenge lever
In the symmetric normal mode the two gliders keep a constant separation, so the coupling spring is never stretched and its stiffness cancels completely from the equation of motion; the period is fixed by the wall springs alone, so the quantity the student instinctively reaches for is not the quantity that sets the frequency of the wave that is launched into the tube.

Adaptation
Built from the C.1 bullets on the defining equation of SHM and the period of a mass-spring oscillator, combined with the C.2 bullets on travelling waves, the wave equation and phase difference. The design is original in its arc: the two normal modes of a symmetrically anchored pair of gliders are contrasted so that the coupling stiffness cancels in one of them, and that same system then drives a longitudinal wave whose frequency, wavelength and phase structure are read off from the SHM result. The values $m=0.020\ \mathrm{kg}$, $k=2.0\times10^{4}\ \mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$ and $k_{c}=3.0\times10^{4}\ \mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}$ were chosen so that the two angular frequencies are exactly $1000$ and $2000\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$, making the period ratio exactly $2$ and the wavelength $0.34\pi\ \mathrm{m}$, and so that the two probe points are separated by exactly half a wavelength.
Verification
Both normal-mode frequencies were obtained from the equations of motion and re-checked by substituting the modal forms x1 = x2 and x1 = -x2; the antisymmetric effective stiffness k + 2k_c was verified; the wave frequency was taken as 1/T_a and the wavelength and phase difference recomputed from lambda = v/f and delta_phi = 2 pi delta_x / lambda; the propagation delay was checked to equal half of T_a.
