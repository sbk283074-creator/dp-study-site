The bend that is set by a ratio, not a field

Physics HL · PHYS-D.2-001

D.2 Electric and magnetic fields and D.3 Motion in electromagnetic fields (AHL): uniform electric field between parallel plates E = V/d, force on a charge F = qE and F = qvB, crossed electric and magnetic fields, the velocity selector condition qE = qvB, circular motion of a charged particle in a magnetic field r = mv/(qB) and period T = 2 pi m/(qB)P2 · Section B15 marksdifficulty 5technology: permittedShow thatDetermineExplain

Stimulus

A beam of singly ionised atoms of one unknown isotope travels along the axis of a velocity selector. The selector is formed by two parallel plates separated by $d=8.0\ \mathrm{mm}$, with the upper plate at the higher potential and a potential difference $V=288\ \mathrm{V}$ between them; the field between the plates is uniform. The same region contains a uniform magnetic field of flux density $B_{1}=0.60\ \mathrm{T}$, directed perpendicular to both the plates and the beam axis. Ions that emerge along the axis enter a second region, longer than $2R$, that contains only a uniform magnetic field of flux density $B_{2}=0.40\ \mathrm{T}$ directed into the page, with no electric field. Each ion then follows a semicircular path of radius $R=0.15\ \mathrm{m}$ and strikes a detector. Each ion carries charge $+e=+1.60\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}$; gravitational and relativistic effects are negligible.

Question

The beam enters the selector from the left, travelling along the axis, and the ions are positively charged. The second region is long enough for every ion to complete its semicircular path. Answer all parts using $e=1.60\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}$ and $1\ \mathrm{u}=1.66\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg}$.

  1. [4 marks](a)

    Show that the electric field between the plates has magnitude $E=3.6\times10^{4}\ \mathrm{V\,m^{-1}}$, state the direction of $\mathbf{E}$, and determine the direction of $\mathbf{B}_{1}$ for which a positive ion travelling along the axis passes through the selector undeflected. Explain the relationship between the electric and magnetic forces on such an ion.

    (Show that)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    Show that an ion crosses the selector undeflected only if its speed is $v=\dfrac{E}{B_{1}}$, calculate the value of $v$, and explain why this measurement on its own cannot give the mass of the ion.

    (Show that)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    Each ion that leaves the selector enters the second region and moves along a semicircular path of radius $R$ to the detector. Determine the mass of the ion in kilograms and in atomic mass units, and hence state its approximate mass number.

    (Determine)
  4. [3 marks](d)

    The flux density of the selector's magnetic field is now doubled to $B_{1}'=1.20\ \mathrm{T}$, while $E$ and $B_{2}$ are unchanged. Determine the new radius of the semicircular path and the time the ion spends in the second region, and explain why this time is unchanged even though the radius has altered.

    (Determine)

Total: 15 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) The field between parallel plates is uniform, so its magnitude is the potential difference divided by the plate separation:

$$E=\frac{V}{d}=\frac{288}{8.0\times10^{-3}}=3.6\times10^{4}\ \mathrm{V\,m^{-1}}$$ (M1)(A1)

The upper plate is the positive one, so $\mathbf{E}$ is directed vertically downwards, from the upper plate towards the lower plate. (A1)

A positive ion travelling along the axis feels an electric force $q\mathbf{E}$ downwards. For it to cross the selector undeflected the magnetic force $q\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}_{1}$ must be equal in magnitude and opposite in direction, that is upwards. With $\mathbf{v}$ directed to the right and the required force upwards, the right-hand rule gives $\mathbf{B}_{1}$ directed into the page. (A1)

(b) Undeflected motion requires the two forces to balance:

$$qE=qvB_{1}$$ (M1)

Dividing by $qB_{1}$,

$$v=\frac{E}{B_{1}}$$ (AG)(A1)

$$v=\frac{3.6\times10^{4}}{0.60}=6.0\times10^{4}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$$ (A1)

The balance $qE=qvB_{1}$ contains neither the mass nor the charge of the ion, because both forces are proportional to $q$. The undeflected setting therefore fixes only the speed $v=E/B_{1}$; it carries no information about the mass, and a second measurement that does depend on the mass is needed. (A1)

(c) In the second region the only force is magnetic, and it provides the centripetal force:

$$qvB_{2}=\frac{mv^{2}}{R}\quad\Rightarrow\quad m=\frac{qB_{2}R}{v}$$ (M1)(AG)

$$m=\frac{(1.60\times10^{-19})(0.40)(0.15)}{6.0\times10^{4}}=\frac{9.60\times10^{-21}}{6.0\times10^{4}}=1.60\times10^{-25}\ \mathrm{kg}$$ (A1)

Converting with $1\ \mathrm{u}=1.66\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg}$,

$$m=\frac{1.60\times10^{-25}}{1.66\times10^{-27}}=96.4\ \mathrm{u}$$ (A1)

so the isotope has a mass number of about $96$. (A1)

(d) If $B_{1}$ is doubled to $1.20\ \mathrm{T}$ while $E$ is unchanged, the transmitted speed becomes

$$v'=\frac{E}{B_{1}'}=\frac{3.6\times10^{4}}{1.20}=3.0\times10^{4}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$$ (M1)

and the new radius is

$$R'=\frac{mv'}{qB_{2}}=\frac{(1.60\times10^{-25})(3.0\times10^{4})}{(1.60\times10^{-19})(0.40)}=0.075\ \mathrm{m}=\frac{R}{2}$$ (A1)

The ion still travels through half a circle, so the time in the second region is half a period:

$$t=\frac{T}{2}=\frac{\pi m}{qB_{2}}=\frac{\pi(1.60\times10^{-25})}{(1.60\times10^{-19})(0.40)}=7.9\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{s}$$ (A1)

This is the same as before the change. The period of circular motion in a magnetic field is $2\pi m/(qB_{2})$, which contains no speed, so the ion moves round a smaller semicircle at a proportionally smaller speed and arrives at the detector at exactly the same instant. (A1)

Markscheme notes

(a) The (M1) is for using $E=V/d$ with the separation in metres; a candidate who leaves $d=8.0$ and obtains $36\ \mathrm{V\,m^{-1}}$ loses the value mark but may still earn the direction marks. The direction of $\mathbf{E}$ must be stated as from the upper (positive) plate to the lower plate. The final (A1) requires the magnetic force to be equal and opposite to the electric force and a right-hand-rule argument; merely writing 'into the page' with no reasoning earns only the direction mark. Accept 'into the paper' or a correct $\otimes$ symbol.

(b) The (AG) requires the balance $qE=qvB_{1}$ to be stated before dividing. The value mark is for $6.0\times10^{4}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$; accept $6.0\times10^{4}$ or $60\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. The final (A1) is for the observation that both forces contain $q$, so the balance is independent of both $q$ and $m$. A candidate who says only 'the mass is not measured' has not explained the insensitivity and forfeits that mark.

(c) Follow-through applies from (b): a candidate whose $v$ is wrong but who uses $m=qB_{2}R/v$ correctly earns the method mark and the conversion mark. Accept $1.6\times10^{-25}\ \mathrm{kg}$ and a mass number between $96$ and $97$. Do not accept a mass given without a unit.

(d) The key mark is the last one, for the reason that the period is independent of speed. A candidate who finds the new radius as $R/2$ but claims that the time halves has missed the lever and forfeits that mark. Accept $0.075\ \mathrm{m}$ and $7.9\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{s}$, and accept the time quoted symbolically as $\pi m/(qB_{2})$.

Why this question is hard

This question is built around a measurement that cannot see the fields which produce it. The undeflected condition $qE=qvB_{1}$ fixes the speed as $E/B_{1}$, so the selector responds only to the ratio of the two fields. Doubling $E$ and $B_{1}$ together leaves the speed, the straight path and the downstream radius exactly as they were; there is no reading anywhere in the apparatus that would reveal the change. That is the lever: the quantity the student wants to attribute to a single field is in fact set by a ratio.

The second half of the lever is the time spent in the magnetic region. A candidate who has just seen the radius halve when $B_{1}$ doubles naturally expects the ion to reach the detector in half the time. It does not. The period $T=2\pi m/(qB_{2})$ contains no speed, so the ion crawls round a smaller semicircle at a proportionally smaller speed and arrives at the same instant. This is the same reason a cyclotron can accelerate particles to high energy without the orbit period drifting.

The two unknowns in the problem, the speed and the mass, are deliberately split across two measurements. The selector is sensitive only to $v$; the magnet is sensitive to the product $mv$, and therefore, once $v$ is known, to $m$ alone. Neither reading by itself is enough, and the markscheme rewards the recognition that the selector is blind to mass.

Classic wrong turns: treating the selector balance as $qvB=mv^{2}/r$, which confuses the two regions; forgetting that the electric force is also proportional to $q$ and concluding that the selector measures a charge-to-mass ratio; and using the full period rather than half a period for a semicircular path. A useful sanity check is dimensional: $E/B_{1}$ has units of $\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, and $qB_{2}R/v$ has units of kilograms.

Adaptation
Built from the D.2 and D.3 bullets on uniform fields, forces on charges and the motion of a charged particle in crossed and magnetic fields. The design was constructed backwards from two levers: the undeflected condition fixes only the ratio $E/B_{1}$, so the apparatus is blind to a common scaling of the two fields, and the semicircle time depends only on $m$, $q$ and $B_{2}$. The values $d=8.0\ \mathrm{mm}$, $V=288\ \mathrm{V}$, $B_{1}=0.60\ \mathrm{T}$, $B_{2}=0.40\ \mathrm{T}$ and $R=0.15\ \mathrm{m}$ were chosen so that $E=3.6\times10^{4}\ \mathrm{V\,m^{-1}}$, $v=6.0\times10^{4}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, $m=1.60\times10^{-25}\ \mathrm{kg}$ (mass number $96$) and $T/2=7.9\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{s}$ all come out cleanly, and so that doubling $B_{1}$ gives exactly $R/2$. The item is not a reskin of any existing one: the bank's D.4 item is about motional emf and terminal speed, and its D.1 item is about orbital transfers.
Verification
Electric field from E = V/d = 288/8.0e-3 = 3.6e4 V/m; transmitted speed from v = E/B1 = 6.0e4 m/s; ion mass from m = q B2 R / v = 1.60e-25 kg = 96.4 u; period T = 2 pi m/(q B2) = 1.5708e-5 s so the semicircle time is 7.854e-6 s; after doubling B1 the speed is 3.0e4 m/s, the radius is 0.075 m and the semicircle time is unchanged. The scaled-field case was re-checked by confirming that 2E/(2B1) = E/B1.
