1.13 Loci in the complex plane: a circle and a half-line

Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL · MATH-AHL1.13-101

AHL 1.13 (modulus-argument form; loci in the complex plane, |z - z0| = r and arg z = theta)P1 · Section B12 marksdifficulty 4technology: not allowedShow thatDetermineCalculate

Question

The complex number $z$ satisfies $|z-(3+2i)|=2$. The circle and the half-line $\arg z = \frac{\pi}{4}$ are drawn on the Argand diagram in the figure. Answer all parts without using a calculator and give every answer in exact form.

  1. [2 marks](a)

    Show that the locus of $z$ in the complex plane is a circle, and state the centre and the radius of this circle.

    (Show that)
  2. [5 marks](b)

    The half-line $\arg z = \frac{\pi}{4}$ meets the circle at the two points $P$ and $Q$ shown in the diagram. Determine the exact value of $|z|$ at each of $P$ and $Q$.

    (Determine)
  3. [2 marks](c)

    Calculate the exact distance $PQ$ between the two points found in part (b), giving your answer in the form $\sqrt{k}$ where $k$ is an integer.

    (Calculate)
  4. [3 marks](d)

    The point $W$ lies on the circle, and $|W|$ is smaller than the modulus of every other point of the circle. Determine $W$ in the form $a+bi$ and state the exact value of $|W|$.

    (Determine)

Total: 12 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) Put $z = x + iy$. Then $z - (3+2i) = (x-3) + i(y-2)$, and the condition $|z-(3+2i)| = 2$ says that $\sqrt{(x-3)^2 + (y-2)^2} = 2$. Squaring both sides gives $(x-3)^2 + (y-2)^2 = 4$. (M1) This is the equation of the circle with centre $3 + 2i$ and radius $2$. (A1)(AG)

(b) Every point of the half-line $\arg z = \frac{\pi}{4}$ has equal real and imaginary parts, so write $z = t(1+i)$ with $t > 0$. (M1) Substituting into the circle equation gives $(t-3)^2 + (t-2)^2 = 4$. (M1) Expanding, $2t^2 - 10t + 9 = 0$. (A1) The quadratic formula gives $t = \frac{10 \pm \sqrt{100-72}}{4} = \frac{10 \pm \sqrt{28}}{4} = \frac{5 \pm \sqrt{7}}{2}$. For a point $z = t(1+i)$ the modulus is $|z| = t|1+i| = t\sqrt{2}$, so the two moduli are $\frac{(5+\sqrt{7})\sqrt{2}}{2} = \frac{5\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{14}}{2}$ and $\frac{(5-\sqrt{7})\sqrt{2}}{2} = \frac{5\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{14}}{2}$. (A1)(A1) Numerically these are $5.41$ and $1.66$, and the diagram confirms that both lie in the first quadrant.

(c) The two points are $z_1 = t_1(1+i)$ and $z_2 = t_2(1+i)$, and from the quadratic $t_1 - t_2 = \sqrt{7}$. (M1) Because both points lie on the same ray, $PQ = |z_1 - z_2| = (t_1-t_2)|1+i| = \sqrt{7}\,\sqrt{2} = \sqrt{14}$. (A1)

(d) The point of a circle closest to a given point lies on the line joining that point to the centre, so $W$ lies on the line from the origin through $3+2i$. (M1) The distance from the origin to the centre is $|3+2i| = \sqrt{13}$, and the origin is outside the circle because $\sqrt{13} > 2$, so $|W| = \sqrt{13} - 2$. (A1) Since $W$ lies on that line, $W = \frac{\sqrt{13}-2}{\sqrt{13}}(3+2i) = \frac{3(\sqrt{13}-2)}{\sqrt{13}} + \frac{2(\sqrt{13}-2)}{\sqrt{13}}i \approx 1.336 + 0.891i$. (A1)

Markscheme notes

(a) Award M1 for translating the modulus condition into an algebraic equation, either by writing $z = x+iy$ and squaring, or by quoting the circle form directly. Award A1 for centre $3+2i$ and radius $2$. Accept $(x-3)^2+(y-2)^2=4$ as an equivalent statement of the locus. (b) M1 for the parameterisation $z = t(1+i)$ or for any equivalent use of $y = x$ with $x > 0$; M1 for substituting into the circle equation; A1 for the reduced quadratic $2t^2-10t+9=0$; A1 for $t = \frac{5 \pm \sqrt{7}}{2}$; A1 for both moduli in exact form. A candidate who obtains $t$ correctly but reports $\frac{5 \pm \sqrt{7}}{2}$ as $|z|$ has omitted the factor $\sqrt{2}$ and scores 4 of the 5 marks. Do not award the final A1 for decimal answers alone. (c) M1 for $PQ = (t_1-t_2)|1+i|$, A1 for $\sqrt{14}$. Follow through from the candidate's own $t_1, t_2$ provided the method is sound. (d) M1 for placing $W$ on the line joining the origin to the centre, A1 for $|W| = \sqrt{13}-2$, A1 for the exact complex form. Accept $1.34 + 0.89i$ only when the exact form is also given, since the question asks for exact values. Do not accept $W$ found by scaling the centre by any other factor.

Why this question is hard

This item is built on one idea: a modulus condition is a distance, and a distance is geometry before it is algebra. Reading $|z-(3+2i)| = 2$ as a circle of radius $2$ about the point $3+2i$ is the whole of part (a), and a candidate who recognises that can answer it without writing anything down. A candidate who reaches for $x + iy$ still gets there, but pays for it in algebra.

Part (b) is where the item bites. The half-line $\arg z = \frac{\pi}{4}$ is the ray $y = x$ restricted to $x > 0$, so every point on it is $t(1+i)$ for a single positive real parameter $t$. That turns a two-variable problem into a quadratic in $t$, which is the intended route. The commonest error is to solve that quadratic correctly and then report $t$ as the modulus, forgetting that $|t(1+i)| = t\sqrt{2}$. The second commonest is to keep the negative root: the full line $y = x$ does meet the circle twice more on the far side of the origin, but those points have argument $-\frac{3\pi}{4}$, which is not the half-line the question names. The figure makes both traps visible, because both intersections $P$ and $Q$ are drawn in the first quadrant, so a candidate who finds a negative $t$ has contradicted the diagram.

Part (c) rewards seeing that $P$ and $Q$ lie on the same ray, so the chord between them is just the difference of the two parameters multiplied by $\sqrt{2}$. No coordinates are needed. It also offers an independent check, because the chord of a circle cut by a line at perpendicular distance $d$ from the centre is $2\sqrt{r^2 - d^2}$, and here $d = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, which returns $\sqrt{14}$ again.

Part (d) cannot be reached by the algebra of parts (b) and (c), because the closest point is not on the ray at all. It lies on the line joining the origin to the centre, at distance $\sqrt{13} - 2$ from the origin. A candidate who answers $\sqrt{13} + 2$ has taken the far side of the circle, and one who answers $\sqrt{13}$ has forgotten the radius entirely.

Metadata

Challenge lever
The substitution the half-line invites returns the ray parameter t rather than the modulus the question asks for, so both answers in part (b) come out too small by a factor of sqrt(2) unless the candidate notices that |t(1+i)| = t*sqrt(2). Part (c) then rewards the same observation in reverse, because PQ is (t1 - t2)|1+i| and not t1 - t2, and part (d) asks for a point the algebra never produces: it can only be found by knowing that the point of a circle closest to the origin lies on the line joining the origin to the centre.

Adaptation
Original item. The pairing of a circle locus with a half-line is standard, but the outputs were chosen to punish the two errors that the parameterisation invites: quoting the ray parameter as the modulus, and keeping the root that lies on the opposite half-line. The final part deliberately moves off the ray so that no answer from the earlier parts can be reused.
Verification
Both intersection points recomputed from the quadratic and each checked to lie on the circle at full precision; the chord length cross-checked against the perpendicular-distance form 2*sqrt(r^2 - d^2) with d = 1/sqrt(2); the minimum-modulus point verified to be the centre scaled by (|c|-r)/|c| and to have modulus |c|-r.
