2.12 Cubic graphs: the three-real-root interval and the width of that interval

Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL · MATH-AHL2.12-101

Topic 2: FunctionsAHL 2.12 (polynomial functions, their graphs and equations)P1 · Section B13 marksdifficulty 4technology: not allowedFindStateShow thatHence

Question

The figure shows part of the graph of $y = x^3 - 3x + 1$ together with three horizontal lines $y = a$.

Let $f(x) = x^3 - 3x + 1$ and consider the equation $f(x) = a$.

  1. [3 marks](a)

    Find the coordinates of the two stationary points of $f$ and state the nature of each.

    (Find)
  2. [2 marks](b)

    State the set of values of $a$ for which the equation $f(x) = a$ has three distinct real roots.

    (State)
  3. [5 marks](c)

    For $a$ in this set, let the three roots be $x_1 < x_2 < x_3$. Show that $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 = 0$, that $x_1 x_3 = x_2^2 - 3$, and hence that $x_3 - x_1 = \sqrt{12 - 3x_2^2}$.

    (Show that)
  4. [3 marks](d)

    Hence find the greatest possible value of $x_3 - x_1$, and explain why this is larger than the value at either end of the range found in part (b).

    (Hence)

Total: 13 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) $f'(x) = 3x^2 - 3 = 3(x-1)(x+1)$ (M1), so $f'(x) = 0$ at $x = \pm 1$. $f(-1) = -1 + 3 + 1 = 3$ and $f(1) = 1 - 3 + 1 = -1$ (A1). $f''(x) = 6x$, so $f''(-1) = -6 < 0$ and $(-1, 3)$ is a local maximum, while $f''(1) = 6 > 0$ and $(1, -1)$ is a local minimum (A1).

(b) Three distinct real roots occur exactly when the horizontal line $y = a$ cuts the curve between the two turning values, so $-1 < a < 3$ (A1)(A1) — the inequality is strict, since at $a = 3$ and at $a = -1$ two of the roots coincide.

(c) The equation $f(x) = a$ is $x^3 - 3x + 1 - a = 0$, a cubic with roots $x_1, x_2, x_3$. By Vieta, the sum of the roots is minus the coefficient of $x^2$, which is zero, so $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 = 0$ (M1)(A1). The sum of pairwise products is the coefficient of $x$, so $x_1x_2 + x_2x_3 + x_3x_1 = -3$ (M1). Hence $x_2(x_1 + x_3) + x_1x_3 = -3$, and since $x_1 + x_3 = -x_2$ this gives $-x_2^2 + x_1x_3 = -3$, that is $x_1x_3 = x_2^2 - 3$ (A1). Finally $(x_3 - x_1)^2 = (x_1 + x_3)^2 - 4x_1x_3 = x_2^2 - 4(x_2^2 - 3) = 12 - 3x_2^2$ (M1), and $x_3 - x_1 > 0$, so $x_3 - x_1 = \sqrt{12 - 3x_2^2}$ (A1) (AG).

(d) $x_3 - x_1 = \sqrt{12 - 3x_2^2}$ is greatest when $x_2 = 0$ (M1), giving $x_3 - x_1 = \sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt3 \approx 3.46$ (A1). This exceeds $3$, the width at each end of the range: at $a = 3$ the roots are $-1$ (repeated) and $2$, and at $a = -1$ they are $-2$ and $1$ (repeated), so both extreme levels give a width of exactly $3$ (R1). The reason is that the width is governed by $x_2$, not by $a$: as $a$ moves away from the value that puts the middle root at the origin, $|x_2|$ grows and the width falls, so the ends of the parameter range give the narrowest intervals rather than the widest.

Markscheme notes

(a) Award (M1) for differentiating and factorising. The nature may be established by the second derivative or by a sign table; condone a correctly argued sign table. A candidate who writes only '$(-1,3)$ max, $(1,-1)$ min' with no working earns (A1) only if both coordinates are correct.

(b) One mark for each endpoint; the strictness is worth a mark in its own right, so $-1 \le a \le 3$ earns (A1)(A0). Accept equivalent notation.

(c) Vieta must be seen, not merely asserted: award (M1) for writing the monic cubic and identifying its coefficients, (A1) for the sum, (M1) for the pairwise-product relation and (A1) for eliminating $x_1 + x_3$ inside it. The final square root requires the positive branch to be justified from $x_3 > x_1$; a candidate who omits that justification forfeits the last (A1). Follow-through applies throughout from a wrong value of $x_1x_3$.

(d) (M1) for recognising the expression is maximised at $x_2 = 0$; (A1) for $2\sqrt3$; (R1) for the comparison with $3$ and an explanation in terms of $x_2$ rather than $a$. A candidate who simply reads $2\sqrt3$ off the figure without the algebraic argument earns no marks in this part.

Why this question is hard

This is a parameter-range question wearing the clothes of a graph question, and the figure is there to make the wrong answer look right. Every candidate finds the turning values in (a) and the interval $-1 < a < 3$ in (b) without difficulty; the item is decided by (d).

The natural, and wrong, move is to assume the three roots are furthest apart when the horizontal line is as far as possible from the middle of the range — that is, at the two tangency levels $a = 3$ and $a = -1$, where one pair of roots has just merged. Both those levels give a width of exactly $3$. The truth is the opposite: the width is $\sqrt{12 - 3x_2^2}$, a function of the middle root alone, so it is largest when the middle root passes through the origin — at $a = 1$, in the centre of the range — where it is $2\sqrt3 \approx 3.46$.

The step that unlocks this is Vieta. The cubic is monic with no $x^2$ term, so the roots sum to zero and the quantity $x_3 - x_1$ can be rewritten entirely in terms of $x_2$; once that is done the maximisation is one line. Candidates who try to work with $a$ directly have no handle, because $a$ and $x_2$ are related by $x_2^3 - 3x_2 + 1 = a$, which cannot be inverted by hand. The second common error is to confuse the width with the difference of the outer roots computed at a single convenient value of $a$ and generalised from one case.

Challenge lever
The width of the three-root interval is not governed by the ends of the parameter range, which is what the figure appears to show and what every candidate reaches for: at both tangency levels the interval has width exactly 3, while at the parameter value that puts the middle root on the axis of symmetry it is 2 sqrt(3), which is larger. The width has to be expressed in terms of the middle root using Vieta before it can be maximised at all, and the extremes of the parameter are where the width is smallest, not largest.
