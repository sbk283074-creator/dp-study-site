The inverse normal value that scales with the standard deviation

Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL · MATH-AHL4.12-001

SL 4.12 (standardization of normal variables, z-values; inverse normal calculations)P2 · Section B16 marksdifficulty 4technology: requiredFindDetermine

Question

The random variable $X$ is normally distributed with mean $150$ and standard deviation $12$. A calculator is required throughout.

  1. [4 marks](a)

    Find the probability that a value of $X$ chosen at random is less than $138$.

    (Find)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    Find the probability that a value of $X$ lies between $144$ and $168$.

    (Find)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    Find the value $x$ such that $P(X<x)=0.90$.

    (Find)
  4. [4 marks](d)

    The standard deviation is later found to be $15$ rather than $12$. Determine the new value of $x$ for which $P(X<x)=0.90$ and explain how the answer changes.

    (Determine)

Total: 16 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) Standardising gives $z=\frac{138-150}{12}$ (M1) $=-1$ (A1), so $P(X<138)=P(Z<-1)$ (M1) $=0.158655$ (A1). Standardising expresses the value as a number of standard deviations below the mean, after which the standard normal distribution supplies the probability directly. The value $0.158655$ is the area of the standard normal curve to the left of minus one, and a value below the mean therefore gives a negative standardised score.

(b) The two standardised values are $z_{1}=\frac{144-150}{12}=-0.5$ (M1) and $z_{2}=\frac{168-150}{12}=1.5$ (A1). Hence $P(144<X<168)=P(-0.5<Z<1.5)$ (M1) $=0.933193-0.308539=0.624655$ (A1). Subtracting the two cumulative probabilities removes the lower tail, which is correct because the required region is bounded on both sides and the lower tail must not be counted twice.

(c) The standard normal value satisfying $P(Z<z)=0.90$ is $z=1.281552$ (M1)(A1). Reversing the standardisation gives $x=150+1.281552\times 12$ (M1) $=165.379$ (A1). The inverse normal returns the number of standard deviations above the mean, so multiplying by the standard deviation and adding the mean returns to the original scale of the measurement.

(d) The required standardised value is unchanged at $1.281552$ (M1), so the new value is $x=150+1.281552\times 15$ (A1) $=169.223$ (A1). The increase is exactly $1.281552\times 3=3.845$ (R1), since the answer moves by the fixed $z$-value multiplied by the change in the standard deviation. The same standardised position therefore corresponds to a larger distance from the mean when the spread is wider, so the new value of $x$ is greater than the value found in part (c). The standardised value is a property of the ninety per cent tail alone and is completely independent of the units in which $X$ is measured.

Markscheme notes

(a) The (M1) is for standardising with the mean and standard deviation; the (A1) is for $z=-1$. The second (M1) is for evaluating the standard normal probability and the final (A1) for $0.158655$. Accept four significant figures.

(b) Award (M1) for the first $z$-value and (A1) for both $-0.5$ and $1.5$. The (M1) is for expressing the interval probability as a difference of cumulative values and the (A1) for $0.624655$. A candidate who adds the two tail probabilities instead of subtracting earns the method mark only.

(c) The (M1)(A1) pair is for the inverse normal value $1.281552$ and the (M1) for reversing the standardisation with the mean and standard deviation. The (A1) is for $x=165.379$. Do not accept a $z$-value taken from a two-tailed table.

(d) The (M1) is for recognising that the $z$-value is unchanged. The (A1) is for the substitution with $15$ and the second (A1) for $169.223$. The (R1) is for explaining that the increase equals the $z$-value times the change in the standard deviation. A candidate who recomputes the $z$-value from scratch earns no credit for the first mark, because the point is that it is fixed.

Why this question is hard

The item is about the inverse normal calculation and how its answer depends on the standard deviation. Part (a) standardises $138$ and reads a lower-tail probability, part (b) converts an interval into the difference of two cumulative probabilities, and part (c) inverts the relationship to find the value below which ninety per cent of the distribution lies, giving $z=1.281552$ and $x=165.379$.

Part (d) is the lever. The standard deviation is changed from $12$ to $15$, and the value of $x$ for the same ninety per cent point must be recomputed. The standardised value is a property of the probability alone, so it does not change; only the multiplier does. The new answer is $150+1.281552\times 15=169.223$, and the increase over the earlier answer is exactly $1.281552\times 3=3.845$.

The common error is to believe that a larger standard deviation requires a new $z$-value, or to rescale the earlier answer by the ratio of the standard deviations while forgetting that the mean stays fixed. A candidate who does this will obtain a different and incorrect value. The numbers were chosen so that $138$ is exactly one standard deviation below the mean and $144$ and $168$ are exactly half a standard deviation below and one and a half above, so that every $z$-value is exact, and so that the two answers for $x$ differ by the clean amount $1.281552\times 3$. The point of the final part is that the standardised value belongs to the probability, not to the distribution, so it survives the change untouched while every value on the original scale is stretched.
