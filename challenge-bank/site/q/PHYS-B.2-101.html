B.2 Greenhouse effect

Physics HL · PHYS-B.2-101

Theme B: The particulate nature of matterB.2 (Greenhouse effect)P216 marksdifficulty 5technology: allowedShow thatDetermineCalculateEvaluate

Question

The solar constant at the Earth's orbit is $S = 1361\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$, the planetary albedo is $\alpha = 0.30$, and the Stefan–Boltzmann constant is $\sigma = 5.67 \times 10^{-8}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,K^{-4}}$. The measured mean surface temperature of the Earth is $288\ \mathrm{K}$. Treat the Earth as a sphere in a steady state, so that the mean power absorbed equals the mean power radiated to space.

  1. [4 marks](a)

    Show that the mean power absorbed per unit area of the Earth's surface, averaged over the whole sphere, is $\dfrac{S(1-\alpha)}{4}$, and calculate its value. Explain in one sentence why the factor of four appears.

    (Show that)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    Hence determine the effective radiating temperature of the Earth, that is, the temperature of a black body that would radiate that power per unit area to space.

    (Determine)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    A simple model treats the atmosphere as a single layer that is transparent to solar radiation and completely opaque to infrared. In this model the atmosphere absorbs all the surface's infrared emission and re-radiates equally upwards and downwards. Show that this leads to a surface temperature of $2^{1/4}$ times the effective temperature, calculate that temperature, and state by how much it exceeds the measured value.

    (Calculate)
  4. [4 marks](d)

    Suppose instead that a fraction $f$ of the surface's infrared emission escapes directly to space through the atmospheric window, and that the remaining fraction is absorbed by the atmosphere and re-radiated equally up and down. Determine the value of $f$ for which the balance gives the measured surface temperature of $288\ \mathrm{K}$, and evaluate whether the single-layer model in part (c) is a fair description of the real atmosphere.

    (Evaluate)

Total: 16 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) The Earth intercepts solar radiation over its cross-sectional area $\pi R^{2}$, so the power intercepted is $S\pi R^{2}$ and the fraction absorbed is $(1-\alpha)$ of that. (M1) This is spread over the whole surface area $4\pi R^{2}$, so the mean absorbed power per unit area is (M1)
$$\frac{S(1-\alpha)\pi R^{2}}{4\pi R^{2}} = \frac{S(1-\alpha)}{4} = \frac{1361 \times 0.70}{4} = 238\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$$ (A1)
The factor of four appears because a sphere presents a disc of area $\pi R^{2}$ to a parallel beam but radiates, and here absorbs on average, over an area four times as large. (A1)

(b) In steady state the mean emitted power per unit area equals the absorbed value, so $\sigma T_{e}^{4} = 238$. (M1)
$$T_{e} = \left(\frac{238}{5.67 \times 10^{-8}}\right)^{1/4} = (4.20 \times 10^{9})^{1/4} = 255\ \mathrm{K}$$ (A1)(A1)
which is about $-18\ \mathrm{^{\circ}C}$. (A1) This is the temperature the Earth would have with no greenhouse effect at all, and it is well below the freezing point of water.

(c) Let the surface emit $\sigma T_{s}^{4}$. The atmosphere absorbs all of it and re-radiates $\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma T_{s}^{4}$ upwards and the same downwards. (M1) The balance at the top of the atmosphere is therefore
$$\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma T_{s}^{4} = 238 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \sigma T_{s}^{4} = 476$$
while $\sigma T_{e}^{4} = 238$, so $\sigma T_{s}^{4} = 2\,\sigma T_{e}^{4}$ and (M1)
$$T_{s} = 2^{1/4}\,T_{e} = 1.189 \times 255 = 303\ \mathrm{K}$$ (A1)
This exceeds the measured $288\ \mathrm{K}$ by about $15\ \mathrm{K}$. (A1) The model therefore predicts a stronger greenhouse effect than the Earth actually has.

(d) The surface emits $\sigma T_{s}^{4}$ with $T_{s} = 288\ \mathrm{K}$, which is $5.67 \times 10^{-8} \times 288^{4} = 390\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$. (A1) Of this, $f$ escapes directly and $(1-f)$ is absorbed by the atmosphere, which re-radiates half of that upwards. The total escaping to space is therefore (M1)
$$f\,\sigma T_{s}^{4} + \frac{(1-f)}{2}\,\sigma T_{s}^{4} = \sigma T_{s}^{4}\,\frac{(1+f)}{2}$$
Setting this equal to the absorbed $238\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$: (M1)
$$\frac{(1+f)}{2} = \frac{238}{390} = 0.611 \quad\Rightarrow\quad f = 2(0.611) - 1 = 0.22$$
So about $22\%$ of the surface's infrared emission escapes directly to space. (A1) The single-layer model of part (c) is therefore not a fair description: it assumes the atmosphere is completely opaque to infrared, which would set $f = 0$ and give $303\ \mathrm{K}$, whereas the real atmosphere is partially transparent in the infrared and the direct escape is roughly a fifth of the surface emission. The model is still useful, because it produces the right order for the greenhouse warming and it identifies the mechanism — absorption and re-radiation rather than reflection — but it over-predicts the magnitude because it ignores the window and because it treats the atmosphere as a single isothermal layer when in reality it radiates to space from a range of altitudes.

Markscheme notes

(a) Award (M1) for the intercepted area $\pi R^{2}$ and (M1) for dividing by the surface area $4\pi R^{2}$; the verbal mark is for saying that the beam is intercepted by a disc while the average is taken over a sphere, and 'the Sun only shines on one side' is not sufficient on its own. (b) Accept $254$ to $256\ \mathrm{K}$. A candidate who forgets the factor of four in part (a) gets $360\ \mathrm{K}$ here and should be given follow-through only if the error is consistent. (c) The mark is for the factor of two in the balance; accept $302$ to $304\ \mathrm{K}$. A candidate who writes $T_{s} = 2T_{e}$ has used a factor of sixteen in the emission rather than two, and should lose the temperature mark but can still earn the comparison on follow-through. (d) Accept $f$ from $0.20$ to $0.24$. The evaluation must name the window or the partial transparency of the atmosphere; a candidate who says only that the model is wrong has not said why, and the remark about the isothermal layer is the strongest available point but is not required.

Why this question is hard

This question is arranged so that a model a student can build turns out to be wrong in an informative way, and the size of the error tells them something about the atmosphere. Part (b) gives $255\ \mathrm{K}$, which is a genuinely startling number the first time it appears: with no greenhouse effect at all the Earth sits eighteen degrees below the freezing point of water, and the habitability of the planet is entirely a consequence of the infrared opacity of its atmosphere. That single comparison is worth more than any amount of description.

Part (c) then makes the simplest possible greenhouse model and it overshoots. The single fully-opaque layer re-radiates half of what it absorbs downwards, so the surface has to emit twice what escapes to space, and the fourth root turns that factor of two into only $18\%$ on the temperature: $303\ \mathrm{K}$ against the measured $288\ \mathrm{K}$. The model is not useless — it gets the magnitude of the greenhouse warming roughly right and it shows that the mechanism is re-radiation rather than trapping, which is the misconception the word 'greenhouse' invites. But it is $15\ \mathrm{K}$ too warm, and part (d) asks where the extra heat got to.

The answer is the atmospheric window. Setting up the balance with a fraction $f$ escaping directly is a small piece of algebra that most candidates can do once they see it, and it produces $f \approx 0.22$. That number has a physical meaning: roughly a fifth of the surface's infrared emission leaves the planet without ever being absorbed, in the wavelength range between about $8$ and $13\ \mathrm{\mu m}$ where water vapour and carbon dioxide are comparatively transparent. It is also the reason the model in part (c) fails, and it is the reason adding carbon dioxide warms the surface at all: the gas narrows the window. A student who has derived $f$ themselves is in a much better position to understand that than one who has been told it.
