The derivative that keeps a negative power

Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL · MATH-AHL5.3-001

SL 5.3 (derivative of f(x)=ax^n is f'(x)=anx^{n-1}, n in Z; negative integer powers included)P1 · Section B16 marksdifficulty 4technology: not allowedShow thatFindFindDetermine

Question

Consider the function $f(x)=3x^{4}-5x^{2}+\frac{2}{x^{3}}-7$.

  1. [4 marks](a)

    By applying the power rule to each term, show that $f'(x)=12x^{3}-10x-6x^{-4}$.

    (Show that)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    Find the gradient of the curve at $x=1$.

    (Find)
  3. [4 marks](c)

    Find the gradient of the curve at $x=2$.

    (Find)
  4. [4 marks](d)

    Find the gradient of the curve at $x=-1$ and determine whether $f$ is increasing or decreasing there.

    (Determine)

Total: 16 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) Writing $\frac{2}{x^{3}}=2x^{-3}$, the power rule gives $\frac{d}{dx}(3x^{4})=12x^{3}$, $\frac{d}{dx}(-5x^{2})=-10x$ and $\frac{d}{dx}(2x^{-3})=-6x^{-4}$, while the constant $-7$ differentiates to zero (M1)(A1). Adding these three pieces yields $f'(x)=12x^{3}-10x-6x^{-4}$ (A1)(AG). The negative power remains because the index of $2x^{-3}$ drops from $-3$ to $-4$ when differentiated (R1). The derivative is therefore confirmed with the negative power retained rather than converted back to a fraction. This is the form that must be used for every later substitution.

(b) Substituting $x=1$ into the derivative gives $f'(1)=12(1)-10(1)-6(1)=12-10-6=-4$ (M1)(A1), so the gradient at $x=1$ is $-4$ (A1). The value is negative because the $-6$ term dominates the other two contributions at this point (R1). The gradient is therefore $-4$, meaning the curve is sloping downwards as it passes through $x=1$.

(c) Substituting $x=2$ gives $f'(2)=12(8)-10(2)-6(2^{-4})=96-20-\frac{6}{16}=76-0.375=75.625$ (M1)(A1), so the gradient at $x=2$ is $75.625$ (A1). The fractional subtraction must be carried through exactly, since $6/16=0.375$, to reach this precise decimal (R1). The gradient is therefore $75.625$, a steep positive slope that contrasts sharply with the negative slope at $x=1$.

(d) Substituting $x=-1$ gives $f'(-1)=12(-1)-10(-1)-6(1)=-12+10-6=-8$ (M1)(A1), so the gradient is $-8$ (A1). Because the gradient is negative, the function is decreasing at $x=-1$ (R1). The negative gradient confirms the function falls there, just as it does at $x=1$, although the magnitude of the slope is larger. The negative gradient at both $x=1$ and $x=-1$ shows the curve is sloping downwards at those two points.

Markscheme notes

(a) Award (M1) for rewriting $2/x^{3}$ as $2x^{-3}$ and differentiating each term with the power rule, (A1) for the three differentiated pieces, and (A1)(AG) for the combined derivative $12x^{3}-10x-6x^{-4}$. The (R1) is for noting the negative index is retained. A candidate who writes $6/x^{4}$ instead of $-6x^{-4}$ loses the final mark. The negative index is the single most tested detail and must not be dropped.

(b) The (M1) is for substituting $x=1$, (A1) for $12-10-6$, and (A1) for $-4$; the (R1) is for the negative gradient conclusion and its meaning for the slope of the curve.

(c) Award (M1) for substituting $x=2$, (A1) for $96-20-6/16$, (A1) for $75.625$, and (R1) for noting the exact fractional subtraction. A candidate who rounds $6/16$ to $0.4$ earns the value mark only if the final answer is $75.6$. Exact arithmetic is expected at HL.

(d) Award (M1) for substituting $x=-1$, (A1) for $-12+10-6$, (A1) for $-8$, and (R1) for stating the function is decreasing because the gradient is negative. Follow through from an incorrect derivative is allowed.

Why this question is hard

This item drills the power rule when the index is a negative integer, the case the syllabus explicitly includes but students routinely mishandle. The function looks like a polynomial until the final term $2/x^{3}$, which must be rewritten as $2x^{-3}$ before differentiating; its derivative keeps a negative power, $-6x^{-4}$, rather than reverting to a fraction. The intended route is to differentiate each term separately, substitute the three given $x$-values, and interpret the sign of the resulting gradient. The gradient at $x=2$ is the trap: it is $96-20-6/16$, and the subtraction of $6/16=0.375$ must be carried out exactly to give $75.625$, a value that looks suspicious next to the tidy integers elsewhere. The classic wrong turn is to leave $2/x^{3}$ as a fraction and attempt the quotient rule, or to differentiate it as though the index were positive and write $+6x^{-4}$ or $-6x^{-2}$. Another error is to treat $x^{-4}$ at $x=2$ as $1/16$ but then add it instead of subtracting, giving $76.375$. Candidates also sometimes state that a negative gradient means the function is increasing, reversing the sign interpretation entirely. The numbers were chosen so that $x=1$ gives a clean $-4$, $x=-1$ gives $-8$, and only $x=2$ demands careful fraction arithmetic, isolating the negative-power skill and rewarding the candidate who keeps the algebra exact rather than approximate. The deliberate contrast between the three gradients is what makes the negative-power rule the unmistakable focus of the item. Keeping the algebra exact is the difference between the correct $75.625$ and a rounded error.
