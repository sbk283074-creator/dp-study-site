E.3 Radioactive decay

Physics HL · PHYS-E.3-001

E.3 Radioactive decay (AHL): decay constant, activity, half-life, exponential decayP215 marksdifficulty 5technology: permittedWrite downDetermineCalculate

Question

A sample contains a mixture of two radioactive isotopes, P and Q. Isotope P has a half-life of $4.0\ \mathrm{h}$ and isotope Q has a half-life of $32\ \mathrm{h}$.

At time $t=0$ the total activity of the sample is $1200\ \mathrm{kBq}$. At $t=16\ \mathrm{h}$ the total activity is $300\ \mathrm{kBq}$.

Assume that the decay products are stable and that the two isotopes decay independently.

  1. [2 marks](a)

    Write down an expression for the activity of each isotope at time $t$, in terms of its initial activity.

    (Write down)
  2. [6 marks](b)

    Determine the initial activity of each isotope, showing the pair of simultaneous equations you solve. Give both values in $\mathrm{kBq}$.

    (Determine)
  3. [3 marks](c)

    Calculate the activity of each isotope at $t=16\ \mathrm{h}$ and hence verify that the total is $300\ \mathrm{kBq}$.

    (Calculate)
  4. [4 marks](d)

    Determine the time at which the two isotopes contribute equally to the total activity.

    (Determine)

Total: 15 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) Using $A=A_{0}2^{-t/T_{1/2}}$:

$$A_{P}(t)=A_{P0}\,2^{-t/4.0},\qquad A_{Q}(t)=A_{Q0}\,2^{-t/32}$$ (A1)(A1)

(with $t$ in hours)

Equivalently $A(t)=A_{0}e^{-\lambda t}$ with $\lambda=\ln2/T_{1/2}$; the two forms are identical because $2^{-t/T_{1/2}}=e^{-(\ln2)t/T_{1/2}}$. For P, $\lambda_{P}=\ln2/4.0=0.173\ \mathrm{h^{-1}}$; for Q, $\lambda_{Q}=\ln2/32=0.0217\ \mathrm{h^{-1}}$. (R1)

(b) Let $A_{P0}=a$ and $A_{Q0}=b$.

At $t=0$: $a+b=1200$ (A1)

At $t=16\ \mathrm{h}$: P has undergone $\dfrac{16}{4}=4$ half-lives and Q has undergone $\dfrac{16}{32}=0.5$ half-lives:

$$a\,2^{-4}+b\,2^{-0.5}=300$$ (M1)(A1)

$$\frac{a}{16}+0.7071\,b=300$$

Substituting $b=1200-a$:

$$0.0625a+0.7071(1200-a)=300$$ (M1)

$$0.0625a+848.5-0.7071a=300 \Rightarrow -0.6446a=-548.5$$

$$a=851\ \mathrm{kBq},\qquad b=1200-851=349\ \mathrm{kBq}$$ (A1)(A1)

Because the decay factors are known numbers, these two equations are linear in $a$ and $b$, which is why two total-activity readings suffice to recover both initial activities. If the two half-lives were equal the equations would be linearly dependent and no unique solution would exist; the eightfold separation of the half-lives is what makes the recovery possible. (R1)

(c) At $t=16\ \mathrm{h}$:

$$A_{P}=851\times2^{-4}=\frac{851}{16}=53.2\ \mathrm{kBq}$$ (A1)

$$A_{Q}=349\times2^{-0.5}=\frac{349}{1.414}=246.8\ \mathrm{kBq}$$ (A1)

$$\text{Total}=53.2+246.8=300\ \mathrm{kBq}\ \checkmark$$ (A1)

(d) Set the two contributions equal:

$$a\,2^{-t/4}=b\,2^{-t/32}$$ (M1)

$$\frac{a}{b}=2^{\,t/4-t/32}=2^{\,7t/32}$$ (A1)

$$\frac{851}{349}=2.438=2^{\,7t/32}$$

$$\frac{7t}{32}=\log_{2}(2.438)=\frac{\ln 2.438}{\ln 2}=\frac{0.8913}{0.6931}=1.286$$ (M1)

$$t=\frac{32}{7}(1.286)=5.88\ \mathrm{h}\approx5.9\ \mathrm{h}$$ (A1)

Check: $A_{P}=851\times2^{-5.88/4}=851\times0.361=307\ \mathrm{kBq}$ and $A_{Q}=349\times2^{-5.88/32}=349\times0.880=307\ \mathrm{kBq}$ ✓

As a limiting check, the crossover must fall after both isotopes are still appreciable and before the fast isotope dominates: $t=5.9\ \mathrm{h}$ lies between $T_{1/2,P}=4.0\ \mathrm{h}$ and $T_{1/2,Q}=32\ \mathrm{h}$, as required. Physically this is the time at which the sample stops behaving like P and starts behaving like Q. (R1)

Markscheme notes

(a) Accept the equivalent exponential forms $A=A_{0}e^{-\lambda t}$ with $\lambda_{P}=\ln2/4.0=0.173\ \mathrm{h^{-1}}$ and $\lambda_{Q}=\ln2/32=0.0217\ \mathrm{h^{-1}}$.

(b) Award (M1) for recognising 4 and 0.5 half-lives respectively — a candidate who uses 16/32 = 0.5 correctly but writes $2^{-16/32}$ as $2^{-2}$ has mis-handled the exponent. Follow through on small rounding differences; accept $a$ in the range 845–855 kBq and $b$ in 345–355 kBq. The pair must sum to 1200.

(c) Follow through on the candidate's values from (b); the verification is the point of this part, so a total that does not come to 300 kBq signals an error in (b) and should not be credited here either.

(d) The discriminating step is combining the exponents: $2^{-t/4}/2^{-t/32}=2^{-7t/32}$. Candidates who write $2^{-t/4-t/32}$ (adding instead of subtracting) will get a negative time. Award (M1) for equating the two expressions, (A1) for the correct exponent arithmetic, (M1) for taking logs, (A1) for 5.9 h. Allow 5.8–6.0 h.

Why this question is hard

Single-isotope decay questions are routine; this one is not, because there are two unknowns and only one decay curve's worth of data — the *total* activity at two times. That forces a simultaneous solution, and the exponential terms cannot be combined, so there is no shortcut.

The two places where work collapses:

1. Part (b) is really a pair of simultaneous equations. Recognising that 16 h is 4 half-lives for P but only half a half-life for Q is straightforward; the difficulty is then carrying $2^{-0.5}=0.7071$ through the algebra without rounding it to 0.7 (which shifts the answer by about 20 kBq).
2. Part (d) needs the exponent law applied to a difference. $\dfrac{2^{-t/4}}{2^{-t/32}}=2^{-t/4+t/32}=2^{-7t/32}$ — most candidates either add the exponents or subtract them the wrong way round and obtain a negative time, which they then either report or 'fix' by changing a sign without understanding why.

Worth noticing afterwards: the fast isotope P starts with the larger activity (851 vs 349 kBq) but by 16 h it contributes almost nothing (53 kBq against 247 kBq). That is what the crossover time of 5.9 h means physically — the mixture's behaviour is dominated by P early on and by Q later, and any measurement of 'the' half-life of the sample would give a value that drifts continuously from near 4 h towards 32 h. That is why mixed sources are awkward in practice, and why a single half-life measurement on an unknown sample is not enough to identify it.

Classic wrong turns: treating the sample as having a single effective half-life; using $A=A_{0}e^{-\lambda t}$ with $\lambda$ in hours but $t$ in seconds; and rounding $2^{-0.5}$ prematurely.
