3.17 Shortest distance from a point to a line segment, where the perpendicular foot falls outside the segment

Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL · MATH-AHL3.17-102

Topic 3: Geometry and trigonometryAHL 3.17 (vector equations of a plane; angles between lines and planes); AHL 3.12 (concept of a vector; displacement vectors)P2 · Section B14 marksdifficulty 4technology: not allowedFindCalculateShow thatDetermine

Question

A monitoring cable is stretched taut between two pylons. Relative to axes measured in
metres, with the $z$-axis vertical, the ends of the cable are at $A(0,\, 2,\, 1)$ and
$B(4,\, 2,\, 4)$. A drone hovers at $P(7,\, 6,\, 7)$ and a second drone hovers at
$Q(1,\, 6,\, 2)$.

(a) Find a vector equation of the infinite line passing through $A$ and $B$. \tag{3}

(b) Find the value of the parameter $t$ at which the point of this line closest to $P$
occurs. \tag{3}

(c) Hence find the shortest distance from $P$ to the cable. \tag{5}

(d) Show that for the drone at $Q$ the closest point of the infinite line does lie on the
cable, and find the shortest distance from $Q$ to the cable. \tag{3}

  1. [3 marks](a)

    Find a vector equation of the infinite line through $A$ and $B$.

    (Find)
  2. [3 marks](b)

    Find the value of $t$ at which the point of this line closest to $P$ occurs.

    (Find)
  3. [5 marks](c)

    Hence find the shortest distance from $P$ to the cable.

    (Calculate)
  4. [3 marks](d)

    Show that for $Q$ the closest point lies on the cable, and find the shortest distance from $Q$ to the cable.

    (Show that)

Total: 14 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) $\overrightarrow{AB} = (4-0,\, 2-2,\, 4-1) = (4,\, 0,\, 3)$ (A1), so a vector equation of
the line is

$$\mathbf{r} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}
+ t\begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 0 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad t \in \mathbb{R} \tag{M1}$$

The cable itself is the portion with $0 \le t \le 1$ (A1). This restriction is the one that
matters later and it is worth stating explicitly at the start.

(b) Let $X$ be the point of the line with parameter $t$. Then
$\overrightarrow{AX} = t(4,0,3)$ and $\overrightarrow{AP} = (7, 4, 6)$ (A1). At the closest
point, $\overrightarrow{XP}$ is perpendicular to the direction of the line (M1), so

$$(\overrightarrow{AP} - t\overrightarrow{AB}) \cdot \overrightarrow{AB} = 0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t = \frac{\overrightarrow{AP}\cdot\overrightarrow{AB}}
{\overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{AB}} \tag{M1}$$

Now $\overrightarrow{AP}\cdot\overrightarrow{AB} = 7(4) + 4(0) + 6(3) = 28 + 18 = 46$ and
$\overrightarrow{AB}\cdot\overrightarrow{AB} = 16 + 0 + 9 = 25$, so $t = \frac{46}{25} = 1.84$
(A1).

(c) Since $t = 1.84 > 1$, the closest point of the *infinite line* lies beyond $B$, on the
extension of the cable rather than on the cable itself (A1). The constrained minimum over
$0 \le t \le 1$ is therefore attained at an endpoint (R1), and because the parameter is
already past the end, that endpoint is $B$. Hence the shortest distance from $P$ to the cable
is simply $|BP|$ (M1):

$$\overrightarrow{BP} = (7-4,\, 6-2,\, 7-4) = (3,\, 4,\, 3) \tag{A1}$$

$$|BP| = \sqrt{3^{2} + 4^{2} + 3^{2}} = \sqrt{34} = 5.83 \text{ m (3 s.f.)} \tag{A1}$$

For comparison, the perpendicular distance to the infinite line is
$\frac{|\overrightarrow{AP} \times \overrightarrow{AB}|}{|\overrightarrow{AB}|}
= \frac{\sqrt{409}}{5} = 4.04$ m, which is $31\%$ too small — and is the answer a candidate
who never checks where the foot lands will give.

(d) $\overrightarrow{AQ} = (1, 4, 1)$ (A1), so
$\overrightarrow{AQ}\cdot\overrightarrow{AB} = 1(4) + 4(0) + 1(3) = 7$ and
$t = \frac{7}{25} = 0.28$ (A1). Since $0 \le 0.28 \le 1$, the closest point of the infinite
line does lie on the cable, so the perpendicular distance formula applies directly:

$$\overrightarrow{AQ} \times \overrightarrow{AB} = (12,\, 3,\, -16), \qquad
|\overrightarrow{AQ} \times \overrightarrow{AB}| = \sqrt{144 + 9 + 256} = \sqrt{401}$$

$$d = \frac{\sqrt{401}}{5} = 4.01 \text{ m (3 s.f.)} \tag{A1}$$

Markscheme notes

(a) (A1) for $\overrightarrow{AB}$, (M1) for the vector form with a parameter, (A1) for
stating the range $0 \le t \le 1$ that defines the cable. Any correct direction vector earns
the (A1), including a scalar multiple of $(4,0,3)$, but the parameter range must then be
stated consistently.

(b) (A1) for $\overrightarrow{AP}$. (M1) for the perpendicularity condition. (M1) for the
projection formula. (A1) for $t = 1.84$; accept $\frac{46}{25}$.

(c) (A1) for observing that $t > 1$ puts the foot outside the segment. (R1) for the inference
that the minimum is then at an endpoint — this is the conceptual mark and should not be given
for simply writing "$t > 1$". (M1) for using $|BP|$. (A1) for $\overrightarrow{BP}$. (A1) for
$\sqrt{34} = 5.83$ m. Follow-through: a candidate who obtains a different $t > 1$ in (b) but
draws the correct conclusion about the endpoint may earn everything after the (R1).

Forfeiture: a candidate who applies $\frac{|\overrightarrow{AP}\times\overrightarrow{AB}|}{|\overrightarrow{AB}|}$
without comment earns zero in this part, even if the arithmetic is correct, because the method
answers a different question.

(d) (A1) for $\overrightarrow{AQ}$, (A1) for $t = 0.28$ together with the observation that it
lies in $[0,1]$, (A1) for $4.01$ m. Condone a candidate who computes the distance via the foot
$F = A + 0.28\,\overrightarrow{AB}$ and then $|QF|$, which gives the same value.

Why this question is hard

The formula for the distance from a point to a line is one of the most reliably remembered
results in the vectors topic, and it is quietly the wrong formula here — not because it is
wrong in general, but because it answers a question that was not asked. It gives the distance
to the infinite line; the cable is a segment.

The distinction only matters when the perpendicular foot falls outside the segment, and the
way to find out is to compute the parameter $t$ and look at it. Here $t = 1.84$, so the foot
sits well past $B$. Once that is known, the geometry changes character: on a closed interval,
a minimum that is not attained at an interior stationary point is attained at an endpoint, and
there is nothing left to differentiate. The distance is $|BP|$.

This is the general shape of a constrained optimisation. The unconstrained minimum is
stationary; the constrained minimum is stationary only if the constraint happens not to bind,
and when it does bind the minimum sits on the boundary and the stationarity condition simply
fails. Candidates meet this idea in one-variable calculus as "check the endpoints", meet it
again in linear programming as a vertex solution, and rarely connect the two. The cable
question is the same idea in vector form.

Part (d) is not decoration. A candidate who has just decided in (c) that the foot lands
outside might reasonably start assuming that always happens, and the second drone is there to
disconfirm it: $t = 0.28$, comfortably inside, and the perpendicular formula applies. Being
asked to handle both cases in one item is what forces the check to become a habit rather than
a one-off insight.

Numerically, the error is large enough to matter. The perpendicular distance is
$\frac{\sqrt{409}}{5} \approx 4.04$ m and the true distance is $\sqrt{34} \approx 5.83$ m. In
the setting the question describes — a drone near a cable — a $31\%$ understatement of a
clearance is the difference between a safe separation and a collision.

The source items are set up so that the foot of the perpendicular always falls on the segment, which means the perpendicular-distance formula is always the right tool and the check is never needed.
