The instalment that is smaller than all the others

Mathematics: analysis and approaches HL · MATH-AHL1.4-001

SL 1.4 and AHL 1.4 (financial applications of geometric sequences and series, including annuities and amortisation)P2 · Section B16 marksdifficulty 5technology: permittedShow thatDetermineCalculate

Question

A loan of €45 000 is taken at a nominal annual rate of 6.0%, compounded monthly, so the monthly rate is 0.5%. It is repaid by equal monthly payments of €900, the first payment being made one month after the loan is taken.

  1. [4 marks](a)

    Show that the outstanding balance immediately after the $n$th payment is $B_n = 45000(1.005)^n - 180000\left((1.005)^n - 1\right)$.

    (Show that)
  2. [4 marks](b)

    Determine the number of full payments of €900 that are required to repay the loan.

    (Determine)
  3. [5 marks](c)

    Determine the amount of the final, smaller payment and the total interest paid over the life of the loan.

    (Determine)
  4. [3 marks](d)

    A second borrower makes $58$ payments of €900 and then receives a refund of the overpayment. Calculate the size of that refund.

    (Calculate)

Total: 16 marks. Try the question before revealing anything below.

Reveal the answer

(a) After one month the balance is $45000(1.005) - 900$. (M1) Each subsequent month multiplies the previous balance by $1.005$ and subtracts $900$, so after $n$ payments $B_n = 45000(1.005)^n - 900(1 + 1.005 + \cdots + 1.005^{n-1})$. (M1) The bracket is a geometric series with common ratio $1.005$, so it equals $\frac{(1.005)^n - 1}{0.005}$. (M1) Since $900 / 0.005 = 180000$, this gives $B_n = 45000(1.005)^n - 180000\left((1.005)^n - 1\right)$. (AG) The derivation shows that the balance equals the accumulated value of the loan less the accumulated value of the payments.

(b) Substituting $n = 57$ gives $B_{57} = 45000(1.005)^{57} - 180000\left((1.005)^{57} - 1\right) = 609.5627\ldots$, which is positive. (M1)(A1) Substituting $n = 58$ gives $B_{58} = -287.3895\ldots$, which is negative. (M1) Hence $57$ full payments are made and the loan is cleared by the next payment. (A1) The small positive balance signals that the end of the loan is close.

(c) The final payment is one further month of interest on the balance after the $57$th payment, so it equals $B_{57} \times 1.005 = 612.610485\ldots$, that is €612.61 to the nearest cent. (M1)(A1) This is smaller than €900, as required. (R1) The final payment is not €900; it is the balance remaining after the fifty-seventh payment together with one further month of interest. The total repaid is $57 \times 900 + 612.61 = 51912.61$ euro. (M1) The total interest is therefore $51912.61 - 45000 = 6912.61$ euro. (A1)

(d) If $58$ payments of €900 are made, the total paid is $58 \times 900 = 52200$ euro. (M1) The overpayment is $52200 - 51912.61 = 287.39$ euro. (A1) This agrees with $-B_{58} = 287.3895\ldots$, which confirms the refund. (R1)

Markscheme notes

Part (a): the first (M1) is for writing the balance after one month, the second for recognising the accumulated payments as a geometric series, and the third for summing it with common ratio $1.005$. The (AG) requires the printed form, so the step $900 / 0.005 = 180000$ must appear or be clearly implied; a candidate who writes the sum as $\frac{(1.005)^n - 1}{0.005}$ and then substitutes is acceptable. Part (b): the (M1)(A1) pair is for evaluating $B_{57}$ and showing it is positive, and the second (M1)(A1) for $B_{58} < 0$ and the conclusion of $57$ full payments. Part (c): the (M1)(A1) are for multiplying $B_{57}$ by $1.005$ and rounding to cents, and the (R1) for noting that the payment is below €900. The total (M1) is for summing the payments, and the final (A1) for the interest, which is total paid minus the principal. Accept €6912.61 or €6912.60 from intermediate rounding, but penalise an answer that treats the final payment as €900. Part (d): the (M1) is for the total of $58$ payments, the (A1) for the refund and the (R1) for linking it to $-B_{58}$. Follow-through from a wrong $B_{57}$ is allowed throughout part (c).

Why this question is hard

The question is an amortisation problem, but its twist is the final instalment. The standard treatment assumes every payment is €900; here the loan is not cleared exactly, so after some number of full payments a residue remains and the last payment is smaller. The candidate has to find where that residue sits rather than force the payments to fit.

Part (a) builds the balance from first principles. Each month the debt grows by the factor $1.005$ and then loses €900, and unrolling the recursion leaves a geometric series of payments. Summing that series and using $900 / 0.005 = 180000$ produces the closed form $B_n = 45000(1.005)^n - 180000((1.005)^n - 1)$. Everything after this is arithmetic on that expression.

Part (b) is the locating step. At $n = 57$ the balance is still positive at €609.56, but at $n = 58$ it has overshot to €-287.39. So $57$ full payments are possible and the $58$th cannot be made in full, which is exactly the situation the question is built around.

Part (c) computes the final payment correctly by allowing the outstanding balance one more month of interest: $B_{57} \times 1.005 = 612.61$, which is smaller than €900. The total interest is then the sum of all payments minus the principal, €6912.61. A larger instalment would clear the debt sooner, while a smaller one would never clear it. Part (d) contrasts this with a borrower who simply makes $58$ full payments and is refunded the excess; the refund is the same €287.39 that appears as $-B_{58}$. The classic errors are assuming the $58$th payment is €900, rounding the balance to cents before multiplying by $1.005$, and computing interest as a percentage of the principal rather than as total payments minus the loan.
