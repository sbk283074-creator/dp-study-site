5.8 A stationary value that is exactly zero, so a crossing of the axis becomes a touching point and the root count drops by one

Question

The figure shows the graph of $y = f'(x)$ for $0 \leq x \leq 9$. It is made up of the three straight line segments joining $(0,\,0)$ to $(3,\,3)$, $(3,\,3)$ to $(6,\,-3)$ and $(6,\,-3)$ to $(9,\,3)$.

It is given that $f(0) = -2.25$.

(a) Show that $f(7.5) = 0$.

(b) Find the exact value of the other solution of the equation $f(x) = 0$ in the interval $0 \leq x \leq 9$.

(c) State the number of distinct solutions of $f(x) = 0$ in $0 \leq x \leq 9$, and explain why the graph of $y = f(x)$ does not cross the $x$-axis at $x = 7.5$.

  1. [2 marks](a)

    Using the areas between the graph of $y = f'(x)$ and the $x$-axis, show that $f(7.5) = 0$.

    (Show that)
  2. [2 marks](b)

    Find the exact value of the other solution of $f(x) = 0$ in the interval $0 \leq x \leq 9$.

    (Find)
  3. [2 marks](c)

    State how many distinct solutions $f(x) = 0$ has in $0 \leq x \leq 9$ and explain why the graph of $y = f(x)$ touches rather than crosses the $x$-axis at $x = 7.5$.

    (Explain)

Reveal the answer

(a) The area between the graph of $f'$ and the axis from $0$ to $3$ is the triangle $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 3 \times 3 = 4.5$, and from $3$ to $4.5$, where $f'$ is still positive, it is $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 1.5 \times 3 = 2.25$. So $f(4.5) = -2.25 + 4.5 + 2.25 = 4.5$ (M1). From $4.5$ to $6$ the signed area is $-\tfrac{1}{2} \times 1.5 \times 3 = -2.25$ and from $6$ to $7.5$ it is another $-2.25$, so $f(7.5) = 4.5 - 2.25 - 2.25 = 0$ (A1).

(b) On $0 \leq x \leq 3$ the graph of $f'$ is the line $f'(x) = x$, so $f(x) = -2.25 + \tfrac{x^{2}}{2}$ on that interval (M1). Setting $f(x) = 0$ gives $x^{2} = 4.5$, so $x = \sqrt{4.5} = \tfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{2} \approx 2.12$, which lies in $[0,\,3]$. Since $f' \geq 0$ throughout $[0,\,4.5]$ the function is increasing there and this is the only solution in that interval; on $[4.5,\,7.5]$ the function falls from $4.5$ to $0$ without reaching zero again before the endpoint, and on $[7.5,\,9]$ it rises from $0$. The other solution is therefore $x = \tfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}$ (A1).

(c) There are two distinct solutions (A1): $x = \tfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}$, where the graph crosses the axis, and $x = 7.5$. At $x = 7.5$ the graph of $f'$ crosses the axis, so $f'(7.5) = 0$ and $x = 7.5$ is a stationary point of $f$; part (a) shows that the value there is exactly $0$. The function is decreasing immediately before $7.5$ and increasing immediately after it, so the graph comes down to the axis, touches it at its minimum and turns back up. It does not cross, the root is a repeated one, and the three roots that two turning points suggest never materialise (A1).

Markscheme notes

(a) (M1) for an area calculation that reaches $f(4.5) = 4.5$; (A1) for the two negative contributions and the conclusion $f(7.5) = 0$. A candidate who rounds the areas to $4.5$, $2$, $-2$, $-2$ and obtains $f(7.5) = 0.5$ has done the right mathematics on the wrong numbers and cannot be credited: the whole part turns on the value being *exactly* zero, and the gradient of the segment is $-2$, so the areas are exact halves and there is nothing to round.

(b) (M1) for identifying $f'(x) = x$ on the first segment and integrating to $-2.25 + \tfrac{x^{2}}{2}$; (A1) for $\tfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}$ together with a statement that it lies in $[0,\,3]$. Decimal $2.12$ is acceptable only if the exact form is also given, since the part says *exact*.

(c) (A1) for the answer two; (A1) for linking $f'(7.5) = 0$ with $f(7.5) = 0$ to conclude that the axis is touched rather than crossed. Credit should not be withheld from a candidate who says the root is repeated instead of using the words touch and cross.

Why this question is hard

This is a question about whether zero is a number. The candidate obtains $f(7.5) = 0$ by adding and subtracting areas that are all exact halves, and the temptation at that point is to treat the result as an indication that the arithmetic has gone wrong, because a stationary point that sits exactly on the axis feels like a coincidence. It is not a coincidence: it is the design. The initial value $-2.25$ is chosen to be exactly the negative of the area accumulated by $x = 4.5$ minus the area given back by $x = 7.5$.

Once $f(7.5) = 0$ is established, the root count follows from the sign of $f'$: the function rises through zero once, reaches a maximum, falls to touch the axis at its minimum, and rises again. The answer two is smaller than the three that two turning points suggest, and the missing root is not missing at all -- it has merged with the stationary point. That is the kind of thing a candidate can check: a sketch of $f$ built from the accumulated area makes the touching obvious, and a candidate who sketches it before answering will not need to be told.
