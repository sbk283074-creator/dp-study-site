A mean that says grow and a spread that says do not

Management's decision tree uses a 0.6 probability of high demand that the twelve monthly observations contradict, because the mean of 77 customers a day conceals a bimodal split between a cluster near 42 and a cluster near 125, so the empirical probability of 5/12 reverses which option has the higher expected value once the loan interest is deducted.

A flow line that is cheaper per bench and dearer per year

A strength the outside world is quietly converting into a threat

Management's SWOT lists the eighteen-year leaflet contract as its leading strength and recommends defending it, but STEEPLE shows the decline in printed leaflets is driven by social and technological change outside the firm, so the same contract is a threat the internal analysis cannot see and the admitted weakness in packaging design is where the opportunity actually lies.

Two matrices, one product, opposite instructions

The BCG matrix tells Brackenwood to harvest its cash cow and starve its dog, but the Ansoff matrix shows that the only low-risk growth open to the firm is market development of exactly that cash-cow product, and that the product BCG calls a star can only be grown by diversification the firm cannot fund, so the two tools give opposite instructions for the same product.

A circular plan that pays back only at a reuse rate the trial never reached

The business plan's payback of 3.8 years rests on an assumed twenty uses per glass bottle, but the three-month trial achieved only six and the break-even is seven, so the circular model the plan presents as a cost saving is dearer per delivery than the plastic it replaces, while the bank's four-year payback rule and the founders' zero-waste objective pull in opposite directions.
