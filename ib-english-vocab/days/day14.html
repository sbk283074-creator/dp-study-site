✍️ Model Paragraph — All Five Layers in One Passage

Notice how the five layers interlock. Highlighted = Week 2 vocabulary.

The oppressive manor of Wuthering Heights — its labyrinthine staircases and sepulchral chill — frames a mercurial Heathcliff whose saturnine brooding grows suffused with a visceral hunger for revenge. Brontë's sparse, almost laconic dialogue renders his taciturn cruelty with palpable menace, while the haunting moor outside carries an elegiac sense of the love that might have been. The effect is jarring yet luminous: a world where even tenderness feels incongruous, and every ephemeral peace is redolent of the violence waiting to return.

That paragraph uses 14 distinct Week 2 words across all five layers — place (oppressive, labyrinthine, sepulchral), person (mercurial, saturnine, taciturn), mood (visceral, haunting, elegiac), voice (sparse, laconic), texture (suffused, palpable, jarring, luminous, incongruous, ephemeral, redolent). That is what "precise description" looks like at the top of the markband.

✏️ Your Task — Write the Paragraph

Choose one set text you know well. Write a single analytical paragraph (120–180 words) that:

  1. Names the setting with ≥2 place adjectives (Day 8).
  2. Characterises ≥1 figure with ≥2 character adjectives (Day 9).
  3. Names the mood with ≥2 mood adjectives (Day 10).
  4. Names the voice/register with ≥2 tone/register words (Day 11).
  5. Describes texture with ≥2 texture words (Day 12).
  6. Uses at least one correct distinction pair (e.g., choose saturnine not sanguine; sparse not laconic).

Starter: "The [PLACE] world of [text] encloses a [PERSON] protagonist whose [MOOD] is rendered in a [VOICE] style; the [TEXTURE] detail makes the text's [theme] unavoidable."

Then self-mark using the rubric above. Target: 7–9 on every row. If any row is below 4, revisit that layer in Day 13's grids.

🎯 Assessment Tips

  1. Application, not recognition, is the goal. You "know" a word only when you can use it correctly under timed conditions. If the paragraph felt hard, that's data — those are the words to drill in Week 3.
  2. One paragraph, all five layers. The model shows the shape: place frames, person acts, mood colours, voice speaks, texture lands. Reaching for all five is what separates a 5 from a 7.
  3. The pair is the differentiator. Anyone can write "sad." Choosing "elegiac" over "wistful" (or defending why) is the IB move. The 15 pairs are your edge — keep them alive.
  4. Let verbs and adjectives cooperate. Pair an analysis verb (foreground, suffuse, juxtapose) with a descriptive adjective in the same clause for maximum density and a top-band effect claim.
