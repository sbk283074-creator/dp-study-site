🗂️ The Week 1 Toolkit at a Glance

WEEK 1 CONSOLIDATION — REVIEW DAY

Five conceptual families, built in order: what texts do (analysis, Days 1–2) → how texts are built (craft, Days 3–4) → how we judge texts (evaluation, Day 5). Re-read each card and ask: Can I use this word in a sentence about a text I'm reading right now?

Group A — Core Analysis Verbs & First Adjectives (Day 1)
The verbs that name what a text does to its reader and its ideas — plus the two adjectives (nuanced, didactic) that opened the evaluative register.
subvertverb

To undermine or overthrow established ideas/authority from within.

"Atwood subverts the fairy-tale form to expose its complicity in patriarchy."

juxtaposeverb

To place elements side by side to highlight contrast.

"The novel juxtaposes wealth and decay within a single street."

foregroundverb

To bring an element to the centre of attention.

"The opening foregrounds the theme of unreliable memory."

undermineverb

To weaken an idea or position gradually or deliberately.

"The ending undermines the narrator's claimed reliability."

evokeverb

To call forth a feeling, memory, or image in the reader.

"The imagery evokes a quiet sense of displacement."

elucidateverb

To make an idea or theme clear or explicit.

"The symbol elucidates the novel's central conflict."

problematizeverb

To expose the complexity of an assumed-certain issue.

"The text problematizes the good/evil binary."

conflateverb

To wrongly merge two distinct things into one.

"Critics often conflate the author's voice with the narrator's."

obfuscateverb

To deliberately obscure or cloud meaning.

"The bureaucratic prose obfuscates the truth."

interrogateverb

To critically examine or question a text or assumption.

"We must interrogate the text's claim to objectivity."

nuancedadj

Showing subtle, fine-grained distinction; holding complexity.

"Her nuanced reading attends to the contradiction."

didacticadj

Intended to teach or instruct, especially morally.

"The fable is overtly didactic, yet quietly moving."

Group B — Analysis & Effect/Causation Verbs (Day 2)
Verbs that name emphasis, dismantling, causation, and exemplification — the analytical machinery for how an argument or effect is produced.
underscoreverb

To emphasise or stress.

"The repetition underscores the theme of isolation."

illuminateverb

To shed light on or clarify.

"The subplot illuminates the character's hidden motive."

dismantleverb

To take apart an argument or structure critically.

"The essay dismantles the myth of meritocracy."

deconstructverb

To break a text into parts to reveal hidden assumptions.

"The critic deconstructs the nature/culture opposition."

destabiliseverb

To unsettle or undermine the stability of meaning or position.

"The unreliable narrator destabilises our sense of truth."

engenderverb

To give rise to or cause (a feeling or condition).

"The claustrophobic setting engenders paranoia."

provokeverb

To deliberately stimulate reaction or thought.

"Kafka's opening provokes a creeping unease."

elicitverb

To draw out (a response or feeling) from the reader.

"The final line elicits reluctant sympathy."

exemplifyverb

To serve as a clear example of.

"The scene exemplifies the novel's governing irony."

instantiateverb

To make an abstract idea concrete in a specific instance.

"The character instantiates the theme of alienation."

perpetuateverb

To cause (often harmfully) to continue or persist.

"The narrative perpetuates a colonial stereotype."

vindicateverb

To justify or prove right in hindsight.

"The twist vindicates the earlier ambiguity."

Group C — Writing-Method Verbs I (Day 3)
The shift to how texts are built: verbs of deliberate making, integration, and artistry (textile, sculptural, musical metaphors).
craftverb

To construct with deliberate skill and artistry.

"The author crafts the voice with flat restraint."

deployverb

To position and use a technique strategically.

"Atwood deploys biblical naming with precision."

weaveverb

To interlace multiple strands into a unified whole.

"He weaves myth and history into one fabric."

renderverb

To represent or translate into a perceivable form.

"Chopin renders awakening as a tactile process."

orchestrateverb

To coordinate elements with deliberate precision.

"Miller orchestrates the time transitions."

sculptverb

To shape by careful, refining craftsmanship.

"Morrison sculpts memory into nonlinear form."

layerverb

To build up meaning through superimposed levels.

"Fitzgerald layers the green light with resonance."

embedverb

To fix firmly within a surrounding context.

"Orwell embeds politics in domestic routine."

frameverb

To present within a perspective that shapes meaning.

"Brontë frames the story through double narration."

threadverb

To run one element continuously through a text.

"Shakespeare threads the blood motif throughout."

infuseverb

(Author) to instil a quality into the text.

"Achebe infuses the prose with oral tradition."

suffuseverb

(Atmosphere) to spread through completely.

"An atmosphere of dread suffuses the scene."

Group D — Writing-Method Verbs II (Day 4)
Completing the craft toolkit: saturation, integration, control, and refinement verbs.
permeateverb

To spread throughout every part of something.

"The theme of surveillance permeates 1984."

saturateverb

To fill completely, to capacity.

"Colour imagery saturates the opening chapter."

interlaceverb

To bind distinct elements so tightly they're interdependent.

"McEwan interlaces writing with atonement."

inscribeverb

To fix permanently into a text (monumental marking).

"Morrison inscribes slavery into the novel's body."

constructverb

To build methodically; assemble structure with design.

"Austen constructs the plot around irony."

arrangeverb

To order or sequence elements deliberately for effect.

"Bishop arranges the losses in ascending order."

calibrateverb

To adjust precisely toward a fixed target.

"Ishiguro calibrates the revelation with care."

modulateverb

To vary or shift tone, pace, or register.

"Woolf modulates between thought and description."

attenuateverb

To weaken or diminish intensity deliberately.

"McCarthy's prose attenuates all comfort."

distilverb

To extract and concentrate the essential essence.

"Plath distils anguish into the bell-jar image."

honeverb

To refine or perfect through repeated effort over time.

"Hemingway hones his economy across stories."

sharpenverb

To make more acute or intense at a single moment.

"The tense shift sharpens the trauma's immediacy."

Group E — Evaluative Adjectives (Day 5)
The vocabulary for judging texts: stance words, caution words, and aspiration words. The language of critical appraisal.
polemicaladj

Openly argumentative or combative in tone.

"Swift's polemical essay attacks the policy head-on."

subversiveadj

Working to undermine authority, often covertly.

"The Handmaid's Tale is fundamentally subversive."

satiricaladj

Using wit/irony to expose folly (a mode).

"Animal Farm is a sustained satirical allegory."

allegoricaladj

Carrying a second symbolic level of meaning.

"The Faerie Queene is an allegorical romance."

tendentiousadj

Biased while disguised as neutral.

"The prosecutor's tendentious framing manipulates."

reductiveadj

Oversimplifying; flattening complexity.

"Reading it merely as X is reductive."

insidiousadj

Harmful in a subtle, gradual, unnoticed way.

"The insidious appeal of the Party lies in its banality."

transgressiveadj

Crossing accepted boundaries or taboos.

"The transgressive desire of Gothic heroines."

provocativeadj

Deliberately stimulating reaction or thought.

"Kafka's provocative opening implicates the reader."

ambivalentadj

Holding mixed or contradictory feelings.

"The play is deeply ambivalent about empire."

didacticadj

Intended to teach; weigh intent against effect.

"A Christmas Carol is didactic yet moving."

nuancedadj

Subtle, complexity-holding — the IB gold standard.

"Morrison's nuanced portrayal of Sethe."

🔗 The 9 Near-Synonym Clusters

These are the pairs examiners probe — and the pairs students most often blur. Each cluster below restates the distinction box from its original day. By the end of today, choosing the right word in each pair should be instinctive.

⚠️ Cluster 1: weave vs. thread (Day 3)

weave — Many strands interlaced into a unified whole (you build the cloth).
→ "He weaves myth and history into one fabric."

thread — One continuous strand run through an existing text (you run a line through).
→ "Shakespeare threads the blood motif throughout."

Trick: weave = build from many; thread = one line through.

⚠️ Cluster 2: infuse vs. suffuse (Day 3)

infuse — The author puts a quality into the text (author is the active agent).
→ "Achebe infuses the prose with oral tradition."

suffuse — The atmosphere spreads through on its own (atmosphere is the subject).
→ "Dread suffuses the scene."

Trick: infuse = author in; suffuse = mood spreads.

⚠️ Cluster 3: permeate vs. saturate (Day 4)

permeate — Spread through every part (presence throughout).
→ "Surveillance permeates every level of the novel."

saturate — Fill completely, to capacity (degree of fullness).
→ "Colour imagery saturates the chapter."

Trick: permeate = everywhere; saturate = filled up.

⚠️ Cluster 4: calibrate vs. modulate (Day 4)

calibrate — Precise tuning toward a fixed target.
→ "Ishiguro calibrates the revelation to the exact degree."

modulate — Variation or shift of tone, pace, or register.
→ "Woolf modulates between thought and description."

Trick: calibrate = tune to a point; modulate = vary across range.

⚠️ Cluster 5: construct vs. arrange (Day 4)

construct — Build the whole structure methodically.
→ "Austen constructs the plot around irony."

arrange — Order or sequence the parts deliberately for effect.
→ "Bishop arranges the losses in ascending order."

Trick: construct = make the building; arrange = order the rooms.

⚠️ Cluster 6: hone vs. sharpen (Day 4)

hone — Perfect over time through repeated effort.
→ "Hemingway hones his economy across stories."

sharpen — Intensify at a single moment.
→ "The tense shift sharpens the trauma's immediacy."

Trick: hone = slow polish over years; sharpen = sudden edge now.

⚠️ Cluster 7: polemical vs. tendentious (Day 5)

polemical — Wears its bias openly; picks a fight in plain sight.
→ "Her polemical essay attacks the policy head-on."

tendentious — Hides its bias behind a mask of neutrality.
→ "The documentary's tendentious editing makes its case seem inevitable."

Trick: polemical = honest about its stance; tendentious = deceptive about it.

⚠️ Cluster 8: satirical vs. subversive (Day 5)

satirical — The method: mocks its target through wit, irony, exaggeration.
→ "The satirical portrait exposes vanity through comic exaggeration."

subversive — The aim: works to undermine or topple power, with or without humour.
→ "The subversive framing erodes the reader's faith in the present order."

Trick: satire holds up a fun-house mirror; subversion quietly loosens the foundations.

⚠️ Cluster 9: reductive vs. nuanced (Day 5)

reductive — Flattens complexity; loses essential nuance; collapses contradiction into one claim.
→ "Reading the novel as simply 'anti-war' is reductive."

nuanced — Captures subtle distinction and complexity; holds contradiction.
→ "Her nuanced reading attends to both critique and respect."

Trick: reductive = the reading shrinks; nuanced = the reading expands. They are direct opposites — the most important evaluative pair you own.

✏️ Practice Exercises

Exercise 1: Choose the Right Word from Each Cluster

For each sentence, pick the correct word from the paired cluster. The context dictates which nuance fits.

  1. The novelist doesn't build a single cloth from many tales; rather, she runs the symbol of the river ________ (weave / thread) through every chapter.
  2. The composer's score ________ (infuse / suffuse) the film with melancholy — here the mood is the active spreader, not the director.
  3. Colour and sound don't merely appear in the scene; they ________ (permeate / saturate) it to the point of overload.
  4. The director ________ (calibrate / modulate) the score between tension and calm, shifting register scene by scene.
  5. He ________ (construct / arrange) the entire sonnet around a single volta, building its architecture from scratch.
  6. Years of revision ________ (hone / sharpen) her imagery, whereas the final stanza ________ (hone / sharpen) the poem's emotional cut in an instant.
  7. The columnist's ________ (polemical / tendentious) piece wears its anger openly; contrast it with the op-ed whose ________ (polemical / tendentious) framing hides its bias behind fake neutrality.
  8. The comedy is ________ (satirical / subversive) in method — it mocks — but its deeper ________ (satirical / subversive) aim is to erode the reader's trust in authority.
  9. Calling the novel "just a love story" is ________ (reductive / nuanced); a truly ________ (reductive / nuanced) reading would hold its contradictions.

Exercise 2: The Full-Arsenal Sentence

Write one analytical sentence for each prompt, deliberately combining words from different Week 1 groups (analysis + craft + evaluation). This is the synthesis IB rewards.

  1. Combine foreground (A) + layer (C) + nuanced (E) — how does foregrounding a layered image yield a nuanced effect?
  2. Combine destabilise (B) + frame (C) + ambivalent (E) — how does a destabilising frame produce textual ambivalence?
  3. Combine elicit (B) + distil (D) + reductive (E) — why is distilling an emotion to elicit sympathy the opposite of a reductive reading?
  4. Combine subvert (A) + weave (C) + subversive (E) — link the verb and adjective forms in one claim.

🎯 What Week 1 Gave You

  1. A three-layer analytical language. Layer 1 — what texts do (analysis verbs, Days 1–2). Layer 2 — how texts are built (craft verbs, Days 3–4). Layer 3 — how we judge texts (evaluative adjectives, Day 5). Most students only have Layer 1. You now have the full stack.
  2. Precision over approximation. The 9 clusters exist because "they're basically the same" is where analysis collapses. Choosing weave over thread, or nuanced over reductive, is a Criterion A/B precision move that separates 5s from 7s.
  3. Your caution vs. aspiration vocabulary. reductive, tendentious, insidious let you name a text's (or your own draft's) failure precisely. nuanced, ambivalent, allegorical let you describe success. Deploy both in conclusions.
  4. Stance verbs with exact targets. polemical (open attack), satirical (mock with wit), subversive (undermine power), transgressive (cross boundaries), provocative (jolt). Match the word to the precise challenge the text mounts.
  5. Craft verbs that earn you "how" marks. Instead of "the author uses imagery," you can now say the author distils an emotion, calibrates a revelation, or inscribes a history. That specificity is what IOC and Paper 2 examiners reward.
  6. A consolidation habit. Today proved that reviewing 60 words in one sitting is doable and high-yield. Keep this rhythm: learn across the week, consolidate on Day 6, assess on Day 7. Memory is built in the retrieval, not the first read.
