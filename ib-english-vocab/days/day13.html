⚖️ The 15 Distinction Pairs (Your Week 2 Exam Armour)

These near-synonym pairs are where marks are won and lost. For each, know the test question.

stark / austere
bare & harsh vs. severe but dignified (chosen restraint)
desolate / bleak
emptied of people vs. drained of comfort/hope
oppressive / claustrophobic
crushing from outside vs. enclosing/suffocating space
saturnine / sanguine
gloomy/sardonic vs. optimistic/cheerful (temperament poles)
taciturn / gregarious
reserved/quiet vs. sociable/seek-company (social poles)
mercurial / capricious
mood swings vs. whimsical choices (feelings vs. decisions)
sombre / melancholic
dark/weighty atmosphere vs. inward pensive sadness
spectral / ethereal
ghostly & unsettling vs. delicate & enchanting
foreboding / haunting
anticipatory dread (forward) vs. lingering disturbance (back)
sparse / laconic
minimal text/style vs. terse speech (level: author vs. character)
sardonic / caustic
wry distanced mockery vs. corrosive attacking wit
formal / colloquial
elevated/standard vs. informal/spoken (register poles)
poignant / evocative
keenly moving (feel) vs. strongly suggestive (remember)
jarring / discordant
sudden jolt of contrast vs. sustained clashing
ephemeral / palpable
fleeting/transient vs. tangible/solidly present

Exercise 2: Pick the Precise Pair

For each, choose the better word and say why:

  1. A character who silently bears trauma → stoic or inscrutable?
  2. A text that calls memories to mind → evocative or poignant?
  3. An author who writes few words on the page → sparse or laconic?
  4. A ghastly, unsettling ghost → spectral or ethereal?
  5. A sudden cut from calm to violence → jarring or discordant?

Exercise 3: Build a Full Reading

Using at least one word from each of the five layers, write one analytical sentence on a text you know. Example skeleton:

"In [text], the [PLACE] setting frames a [PERSON] protagonist whose [MOOD] is rendered in a [VOICE] style whose [TEXTURE] detail makes the theme unavoidable."

🎯 Consolidation Tips

  1. Five layers, one scene. Place + person + mood + voice + texture is a complete descriptive reading. In your next commentary, try to deploy all five on a single passage — that's a 7-range response.
  2. The pairs are the prize. Examiners can't award top marks for "sad" or "weird." They can for "elegiac" vs. "wistful," or "sardonic" vs. "caustic." Drill the 15 pairs until the right word arrives without thinking.
  3. Know tone vs. register cold. It's the most commonly confused Week 2 distinction. Tone = attitude (sardonic, caustic, dispassionate, strident). Register = level (formal, colloquial, vernacular, sparse, laconic).
  4. Use verbs + adjectives together. "The passage suffuses the scene with a palpable dread" — analysis verb (Day 3) + texture adjective (Day 12) in one clause. Density = sophistication.
  5. Rest is part of the method. Spaced repetition needs the gap to work. After today's pass, sleep on it; Day 14's assessment activates the words one more time before Week 3.
