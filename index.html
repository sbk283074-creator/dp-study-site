Your subjects

The four content subjects are built to be sufficient on their own — read them in order and you have the syllabus. The two language subjects are built around the exam components (Paper 1, Paper 2, individual oral) and the specific works you are studying. The DP core section covers theory of knowledge and the extended essay — the two compulsory pieces that sit outside your six subjects but decide three of your diploma points.

Which syllabus am I on?

You sit exams in May 2028. That puts five of your six subjects — and theory of knowledge, and CAS — on the guides currently in force, and two things on newer ones: Computer Science (first assessment 2027) and the extended essay (first assessment 2027). This matters, because the books and the internet are full of material for the old versions.

Standing caveat

Weightings, paper lengths and internal-assessment details on this site reflect the published guides above. Schools occasionally run a subject on a slightly different plan, and the IB revises guides — if your teacher says something different from this site, your teacher wins. Everything else here (the derivations, the worked examples, the method) is version-proof.

How to use this site

1. Read for understanding, not coverage

Open a topic page and work top to bottom. Every key point goes: intuition → formal statement → derivation → how to use it → worked examples → traps → connections. Do not skip the derivations; they are what makes the formula stick and what lets you rebuild it in the exam if you forget it.

2. Use the closed-book test

Every page has a practice set. Cover the screen and answer with nothing but a pen. If you cannot do it, you have not learned it yet — re-read that one section and retry in 48 hours. Re-reading feels productive and is not; retrieval is what produces marks.

3. Tick pages off

"Mark this page complete" at the bottom of each page is saved in your browser, and the dashboard tracks it. Use it honestly: tick only when you can do the practice questions unaided.

4. Search across subjects

Press ⌘K (or /) to search every page. Useful for the connections you will not expect: the same integration technique appears in calculus, in physics fields, and in continuous probability distributions.

5. Print what you need

Every page has a print button and a clean print stylesheet — derivations and answers are forced open so nothing is hidden on paper. Build a paper folder for the last month.

6. Do the languages differently

English and Chinese are not knowledge subjects for you — they are skill subjects. Work through the method pages once, then spend the time writing: commentaries, essay plans, and timed individual orals. Rehearsal, not reading, is what raises those grades.

The connections worth knowing early
  • Maths ↔ Physics: calculus is the language of kinematics and fields; vectors carry both forces and 3-D geometry; statistics underpins every uncertainty calculation in the physics IA.
  • Maths ↔ CS: algorithmic complexity is analysis of functions; recursion and induction are the same idea in two languages; machine learning is statistics with a computer attached.
  • Physics ↔ CS: the data-based Paper 1 questions are built from the same graph-and-uncertainty skills as the physics investigation.
  • English ↔ Chinese: the two language courses share the same assessment architecture (Paper 1 analysis, Paper 2 comparison, individual oral) — the analytical toolkit transfers directly, and the works set an unusually rich pairing of East Asian and Western traditions.
  • TOK ↔ every subject: the knowledge questions you use in the essay are the same questions hiding inside your subjects — what counts as evidence in history and in the natural sciences, why a mathematical proof is a different kind of certainty from a physics measurement.
  • EE ↔ your HL subjects: the strongest extended essays borrow a method from an HL subject you already know well. A physics-flavoured EE lets you reuse the uncertainty analysis from your IA; a CS EE lets you reuse the case-study method.
  • Business ↔ Maths: half of Unit 3 and Unit 5 is arithmetic you already have — ratios, break-even, payback, descriptive statistics. The marks are in the interpretation, not the calculation, which is the opposite of what you are used to.
  • Business ↔ English & Chinese: Paper 1 and Paper 2 Section B are marked with markbands on a balanced two-sided argument — the same discipline as a Paper 2 comparative essay, in a subject where you also have to quote the numbers.
  • Business ↔ CAS: the ethics and global-significance learning outcomes in CAS are stakeholder analysis and STEEPLE wearing a different hat. Keep the projects separate, though — nothing submitted for a course can count as CAS.
