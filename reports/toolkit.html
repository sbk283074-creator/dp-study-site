Checklists & pre-submission

A report is finished when it passes its own tests, not when you run out of page. This page collects the self-checks that turn a draft into a defensible report: a per-section test for each part of IMRaD, a pre-write plan you fill in before you touch the apparatus, and a final pre-submission pass. Use it as a reference every time you write up science.

1. Per-section self-check

For each section, ask the test question. If the answer is no, that section is not done.

SectionThe test it must pass
IntroductionDoes it name the research question and the theory that predicts the answer? Could a reader who skipped the manual still know what you studied and why?
Methods reproducibility testCould a competent student, with your school's equipment, repeat this from your words alone — apparatus, ranges, intervals, repeats, controls, safety?
Results data-integrity testIs every table/figure referred to in the text? Is there zero interpretation here (no "this proves")? Are raw and processed data separated?
AnalysisIs the working shown for at least one representative case? Is the final value quoted as result ± uncertainty with consistent figures?
Discussion "so what" testDoes it state the finding, compare it to theory with numbers, name specific limitations and their effects, and propose tied improvements?
Conclusion direct-answer testDoes it answer the research question set in the Introduction — not summarise the report? Does it state the degree of certainty?
Math report reflection testIs there a real reflection section naming assumptions, consequences and a concrete alternative — not "I learned a lot"?

2. Pre-write plan (fill this in first)

Before you collect data or open a document, answer these. They become your Introduction and Methods almost verbatim.

Expand the planning template
Research question
"How does [independent variable X] affect [dependent variable Y] in [specific system]?" — both measurable.
Background theory
The equation/principle that predicts the relationship. Write it. This is your Discussion yardstick.
Variables
Independent = what you change. Dependent = what you measure. Controlled = what you hold, and how.
Method sketch
Apparatus, range of X, interval between points, number of repeats. Enough that the reproducibility test (§1) will pass.
Expected relationship
What theory says Y does as X changes. So the Discussion can later say "agreed / did not agree, because…".
Uncertainty plan
What uncertainties enter, and how you'll quote the final result. (The maths: Physics IA page.)

3. The pre-submission pass

Run this on the finished draft. Each item is a known mark-loser if missed.

✓
Units everywhere. Quantity + unit on every axis and in every table header. Space between number and unit ("5 m s⁻¹").
✓
Significant figures consistent. Uncertainty to 1 sig fig; value matched to it; same decimal places down a column.
✓
Every figure/table cited. No orphan visual; each has a caption and a sentence pointing to the key finding.
✓
Evidence and judgement apart. No interpretation in Results; no new data in Discussion.
✓
Hedged to the evidence. "suggests / consistent with", not "proves". Confident only where the data supports it.
✓
No banned vague words. Search for very / big / small / good / bad / believe / think / got and replace with measurements.
✓
Tense right. Past for what you did/found; present for facts and your interpretations.
✓
References traceable. Any theory, published value or method you borrowed is cited and findable.

The final editing trick

Read your draft aloud, section by section, and stop at every sentence you can't defend. "We did the experiment" fails immediately. "The gradient 2.01 ± 0.04 agrees with theory within uncertainty" survives. The aloud test catches vagueness that silent reading skips — it is the cheapest quality control you have.
