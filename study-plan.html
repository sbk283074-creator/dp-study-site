The two-year plan

You have roughly 20 months from the start of DP1 to the first exam. This page is the schedule and the method. The subject pages are the content; this is how you convert content into grades.

Class of 2028 Updated for the 2027 CS guide Read this first
The one idea on this page

Grades at IB come from retrieval under exam conditions, not from coverage. Reading a page and understanding it produces almost no exam marks by itself. What produces marks is: learning a method, attempting a question without notes, getting it wrong, and fixing the specific error. Everything below is organised to maximise the number of times you do that loop before May 2028.

Why the IAs come first

Your four HL internal assessments are worth 20 % each (plus 30 % → 20 % for CS — check your guide), and they are the only components you can fully control. They are also the single biggest cause of final-year panic, because they all land at once. Start the physics investigation and the CS solution in DP1, even informally. A finished IA in November 2027 removes a whole subject's worth of stress from your final six months.

A weekly rhythm

Your subjects are not equal. Three HLs with heavy content, plus two languages that need rehearsal rather than reading. A working allocation for a normal week of DP1:

SubjectHours / weekWhat those hours are for
Maths AA HL5 3 h new content with derivations, 2 h questions. Never less than 4 — AA HL punishes gaps because every topic builds on the last.
Physics HL4 2 h content, 1 h problem sets, 1 h practical/uncertainty skills (these are examined directly in Paper 1B).
Computer Science HL4 2 h theory, 2 h actually writing code. CS is learned at the keyboard — reading about recursion teaches you nothing.
English A SL2 1 h reading/annotating the set works, 1 h writing (a paragraph, a plan, a timed commentary). Output, not input.
Chinese A SL1.5 Reading the set works + one writing task a fortnight. Your strongest subject — protect the mark, don't neglect it.
Core (TOK / EE / CAS)2 The EE overlaps well with physics or CS; do it early.
How to run a study session that actually works
  1. Two minutes of recall first. Before opening the page, write down everything you remember from last session. This is the single highest-value two minutes in studying.
  2. Learn the method from the page. Read the derivation, not just the result. If you cannot follow a line, that is the thing to fix — not the question at the end.
  3. Do two worked examples with the solution visible, then immediately do two more with it hidden.
  4. Attempt the practice set closed-book. Mark it honestly.
  5. Log every error in one line in an error log (see the toolkit page). Wrong answer, the reason, the fix.
  6. Schedule the retry. Anything you got wrong comes back in 48 hours, then a week, then a month.

Revision technique, briefly

Spaced repetition

Review new material after 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 3 weeks. The forgetting curve is steep and predictable; four short reviews beat one long re-read.

Interleaving

Mix topics inside a session — do five calculus questions and five statistics questions, not ten of one. It feels harder and produces far better exam transfer, because exams mix topics and never tell you which technique to use.

Past papers are the syllabus

Past papers are not a test of learning, they are the best learning material there is. From January 2028, most of your time should be past papers, marked against the markscheme.

Markschemes before answers

After attempting a question, read the markscheme even for the ones you got right. You are learning the examiner's expectations — which steps carry marks, and which comments are ignored.

Don't re-read; retrieve

Every hour spent re-reading a page you already understand is close to wasted. Close the page and write what you remember instead.

Sleep and spacing beat cramming

An all-nighter before a mocks paper buys you a few marks and costs you the next three days of learning. Protect sleep in the final month especially.

The final eight weeks

8 weeks out
All content finished. Every page on this site ticked. Build a one-page "formula and method" sheet per subject — writing it is the revision.
6 weeks
Timed Paper 1 and Paper 2 for maths and physics. Mark strictly. Start the error-log review: every entry gets one more attempt.
4 weeks
Second full paper per subject. Focus exclusively on question types you got wrong the first time — not on whole topics.
2 weeks
Mixed, interleaved question sets only. Rehearse the language orals: two timed full run-throughs each, recorded and listened back to.
Final week
No new material. Light review of your sheets, one short mixed set per day, early nights. Walk into the exam rested rather than crammed.
The trap most students fall into

Doing past papers without marking them properly. If you glance at the markscheme and say "oh yes, I knew that", you have learned nothing and you have also convinced yourself you are better than you are. Mark it line by line, in a different colour, and write the reason for every lost mark. The students who improve fastest are the ones who are brutally honest with themselves in private.
